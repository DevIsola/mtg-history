## I. The Brothers and the Ancient Stones

Long before the great Phyrexian invasions, before the
Weatherlight and the Mending, the continent of Terisiare
on Dominaria became the setting for a conflict whose
consequences would last for millennia.

Its central figures were <CharacterLink id="urza">Urza</CharacterLink>
and <CharacterLink id="mishra">Mishra</CharacterLink>: two brothers
united by their fascination with ancient artifacts,
but increasingly divided by their ambitions.

Their early education took place under

<CharacterLink id="tocasia">Tocasia</CharacterLink>, an archaeologist whose
investigations of the Thran opened an extraordinary chapter in their lives.

Tocasia's dig sites offered the brothers a place
to study machines and powerstones from a civilization
that had disappeared millennia before their birth.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[3]}>

For Urza and Mishra, the ruins were more than
reminders of a distant past. They contained
technologies that promised to transform the world
around them.

Tocasia's guidance helped develop their talents
as artificers, but even their shared discoveries
could not prevent their ambitions from pulling
them apart.

</HistoricalMoment>

Their investigations eventually brought them to the
Caves of Koilos, where they discovered an ancient
powerstone of extraordinary significance.

The stone became divided into two artifacts:
the Mightstone, held by Urza, and the Weakstone,
held by Mishra.

Each brother possessed a fragment of immense power.
Neither was satisfied with possessing only one.

## II. The Death of Tocasia

The rivalry between the brothers intensified after
their discovery.

Their competing desires for the powerstones culminated
in a violent confrontation at Tocasia's camp.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[0]}>

Tocasia attempted to intervene in the brothers'
struggle, but the power unleashed during their
confrontation killed her.

Her death ended the brothers' shared life under
their mentor's guidance.

Rather than reconcile through their grief, Urza
and Mishra went their separate ways.

The tragedy that divided their family would
eventually grow into a conflict between nations.

</HistoricalMoment>

<HistoricalEventReference id="tocasias-death" />

## III. The Rise of Two Powers

After the death of Tocasia, the brothers followed
different paths.

Urza traveled to Yotia, where his expertise in
artifice brought him into the royal court. His
relationship with Kayla bin-Kroog eventually tied
him to the kingdom's ruling family.

Mishra's journey took him among the Fallaji, whose
world was shaped by the desert regions of Terisiare.

His knowledge of ancient artifacts and his growing
ability to command mechanical forces contributed
to his rise among them.

In time, Mishra established a position of considerable
military and political power. His influence gave him
resources with which to challenge Urza on a scale
neither brother could have imagined at Tocasia's camp.

The war would no longer be fought simply over
the possession of two stones. The brothers
had become leaders of competing powers.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[1]}>

Urza's mastery of machines and artifacts became
central to his growing political and military
influence.

His inventions would eventually shape the
armies of Yotia and the course of the war.

Yet his accomplishments were inseparable from
his determination to overcome his brother.

</HistoricalMoment>

The brothers now possessed not only the stones
they coveted, but also the resources and influence
needed to turn their private rivalry into war.

## IV. The War That Consumed Terisiare

The conflict expanded across Terisiare.

Armies marched beneath the banners of competing
powers, and machines of increasing size and
destructive capability transformed warfare.

The struggle drew in many other figures, including
the artificers Tawnos and Ashnod.

As the conflict continued, the ancient threat
of Phyrexia began to influence the war.

<CharacterLink id="gix">Gix</CharacterLink>, a praetor serving
<CharacterLink id="yawgmoth">Yawgmoth</CharacterLink>, sought to advance
Phyrexian interests on Dominaria.

The conflict between Urza and Mishra offered
an opportunity to extend that influence.
The brothers were already consumed by their
competition, and the destruction they caused
created opportunities for forces with
far older ambitions.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[4]}>

Gix represented the continuing reach of the
Phyrexian empire founded after the fall of the Thran.

Mishra's growing association with that power
changed the nature of his struggle against Urza.
The war was becoming entangled with a threat
that neither brother had created.

</HistoricalMoment>

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[2]}>

Mishra's rise among the Fallaji was followed
by a growing dependence on forces associated
with Phyrexia.

The ancient threat would become increasingly
entangled with his military ambitions.

His personal rivalry with Urza had become part
of a much larger and more dangerous struggle.

</HistoricalMoment>

The war devastated communities and landscapes,
while generations lived under the consequences
of decisions made by the two brothers.

## V. The Last Battle on Argoth

The final confrontation took place on Argoth,
an island whose forests and natural life became
caught in the destruction of the war.

By then, Mishra had been transformed through
Phyrexian corruption.

Urza confronted his brother amid the devastation,
bringing their rivalry toward its catastrophic end.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[5]}>

The Sylex was not simply another weapon in
the brothers' increasingly destructive arsenal.

Its activation unleashed a magical catastrophe
that devastated Argoth and transformed the
course of Dominaria's history.

In the aftermath, Urza emerged as a planeswalker.
The conflict had ended, but the consequences
of the blast would continue for generations.

</HistoricalMoment>

The blast devastated Argoth and ended the
Brothers' War.

Urza survived and became a planeswalker, but the
destruction also carried consequences far beyond
the battlefield.

<HistoricalEventReference id="sylex-blast" />
## VI. The Legacy of the War

The Sylex Blast changed Dominaria.

The devastation contributed to the climatic
catastrophe that eventually brought about the
Ice Age, reshaping the world's history.

The conflict also established the significance of
Urza's long struggle against Phyrexia, a struggle
that would influence events throughout later
chapters of Magic's story.

The Brothers' War was not simply the end of a
family rivalry. It was the beginning of consequences
that would echo across the Multiverse.

## VII. How Magic Told This Story

The Brothers' War is particularly interesting
because its fictional chronology and publication
history are separated by decades.

_Antiquities_ (1994) introduced the conflict
through cards representing artifacts, machines,
locations, and other remnants of the ancient war.

The novel _The Brothers' War_ by Jeff Grubb,
published in 1998, developed the historical
narrative in much greater detail.

In 2022, Wizards of the Coast returned to the
conflict through the expansion _The Brothers' War_,
offering new depictions of the central characters,
events, and machinery.

These releases provide different perspectives on
the same historical period, making the conflict
an ideal example of how Magic tells its history
across multiple generations of cards.
