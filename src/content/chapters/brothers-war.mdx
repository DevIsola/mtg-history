## I. The Brothers and the Ancient Stones

Long before the great Phyrexian invasions, before the
Weatherlight and the Mending, the continent of Terisiare
on Dominaria became the setting for a conflict whose
consequences would last for millennia.

Its central figures were <CharacterLink id="urza">Urza</CharacterLink>
and <CharacterLink id="mishra">Mishra</CharacterLink>: two brothers
united by their fascination with ancient artifacts,
but increasingly divided by their ambitions.

Their early education took place under

<CharacterLink id="tocasia">Tocasia</CharacterLink>, an archaeologist whose
investigations of the Thran opened an extraordinary chapter in their lives.

Tocasia's dig sites offered the brothers a place
to study machines and powerstones from a civilization
that had disappeared millennia before their birth.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[3]}>

For Urza and Mishra, the ruins were more than
reminders of a distant past. They contained
technologies that promised to transform the world
around them.

Tocasia's guidance helped develop their talents
as artificers, but even their shared discoveries
could not prevent their ambitions from pulling
them apart.

</HistoricalMoment>

Their investigations eventually brought them to the
Caves of Koilos, where they discovered an ancient
powerstone of extraordinary significance.

The stone became divided into two artifacts:
the Mightstone, held by Urza, and the Weakstone,
held by Mishra.

### The stones were older than their owners

The brothers had not invented the stones that made their rivalry so dangerous.
Their origins lay in the final days of the <CharacterLink id="glacian">Thran engineer Glacian</CharacterLink>,
whose broken powerstone survived the sealing of Phyrexia by
<CharacterLink id="rebbec">Rebbec</CharacterLink>. The Caves of Koilos placed an artifact of
that distant disaster in the hands of two young archaeologists who had not
lived through it and could not recognize every danger it contained.

Urza's Mightstone and Mishra's Weakstone became more than tools: each fragment
was a source of power, an object of study, and evidence that the other brother
possessed something the first lacked. Their names describe how the fragments
came to be understood, not two separate inventions of the boys' own making.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[9]}>

The 2022 card *The Mightstone and Weakstone* shows the ancient powerstone
legacy resurfacing during the Brothers' War. Its modern card mechanics should
not be confused with a literal scene from the historical narrative.

</HistoricalMoment>

The distinction matters because the catastrophe did not begin with an evil
artifact suddenly appearing. It began when the promise of rediscovered Thran
technology met pride, grief, and the refusal to surrender an advantage.

<HistoricalEventReference id="brothers-koilos-stones" />
Each brother possessed a fragment of immense power.
Neither was satisfied with possessing only one.

## II. The Death of Tocasia

The rivalry between the brothers intensified after
their discovery.

Their competing desires for the powerstones culminated
in a violent confrontation at Tocasia's camp.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[0]}>

Tocasia attempted to intervene in the brothers'
struggle, but the power unleashed during their
confrontation killed her.

Her death ended the brothers' shared life under
their mentor's guidance.

Rather than reconcile through their grief, Urza
and Mishra went their separate ways.

The tragedy that divided their family would
eventually grow into a conflict between nations.

</HistoricalMoment>

<HistoricalEventReference id="tocasias-death" />

### A death that became a permanent division

<CharacterLink id="tocasia">Tocasia</CharacterLink> was the person who had
made cooperation possible. Her camp had given the brothers an intellectual
home, disciplined study, and a purpose larger than their personal rivalry.
When she died trying to intervene, there was no surviving mentor with the
same authority over both students.

Their separate departures also broke the partnership that might have tested
each brother's discoveries against the other's. Once Urza and Mishra began
to command other people, their disagreement could be fought through soldiers,
industrial resources, and entire populations rather than through argument.
Neither man would be the sole author of the coming tragedy; both retained
the ability to choose how far to escalate it.
## III. The Rise of Two Powers

After the death of Tocasia, the brothers followed
different paths.

Urza traveled to Yotia, where his expertise in
artifice brought him into the royal court.

There, his relationship with

<CharacterLink id="kayla">Kayla bin-Kroog</CharacterLink>, a member of the
kingdom's ruling family, became an important part of his political rise.

Their marriage connected Urza to Yotian power.
For Kayla, however, that connection eventually
carried an enormous cost: a family conflict
that would threaten her kingdom and the people
she was expected to protect.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[6]}>

Kayla's place in the story reveals a different
perspective on the Brothers' War.

She experienced the conflict not primarily as
a competition between artificers, but as a crisis
that threatened her home, family, and people.

Her later account of the war would help preserve
the history of those who survived it.

</HistoricalMoment>

Mishra's journey took him among the Fallaji, whose
world was shaped by the desert regions of Terisiare.

His knowledge of ancient artifacts and his growing
ability to command mechanical forces contributed
to his rise among them.

In time, Mishra established a position of considerable
military and political power. His influence gave him
resources with which to challenge Urza on a scale
neither brother could have imagined at Tocasia's camp.

The war would no longer be fought simply over
the possession of two stones. The brothers
had become leaders of competing powers.

### From the Fallaji desert to a war machine

The Fallaji were not simply followers waiting for Mishra to lead them.
They were a desert people with their own political life and competing
interests. Mishra entered their world as an outsider whose knowledge of
ancient machines could become a source of influence. His rise depended on
what others believed those machines could win for them.

That ascent altered the meaning of the brothers' private rivalry. Mishra
could now use the armies and resources of a political coalition; Urza's
inventions had become valuable to Yotia for similarly strategic reasons.
The brothers were embedded in institutions that could reward escalation
long after reconciliation had become difficult.

<HistoricalEventReference id="brothers-fallaji-ascent" />
<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[1]}>

Urza's mastery of machines and artifacts became
central to his growing political and military
influence.

His inventions would eventually shape the
armies of Yotia and the course of the war.

Yet his accomplishments were inseparable from
his determination to overcome his brother.

</HistoricalMoment>

Urza's expanding efforts also depended on other
artificers, including

<CharacterLink id="tawnos">Tawnos</CharacterLink>, a talented apprentice whose
knowledge of mechanical invention made him a valuable collaborator.

Tawnos's interests extended beyond the manufacture
of weapons. His work reflected the possibility that
artifice could serve curiosity and everyday life,
even as the war increasingly demanded machines
designed for destruction.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[7]}>

Tawnos's inventions reveal the creative potential
of artifice beyond the battlefield.

His later experiences would force him to confront
the difficulty of separating peaceful technologies
from their possible military uses.

The very machines intended to support a recovering
society could become instruments of another war.

</HistoricalMoment>

### Artifice in service of a kingdom

Urza's place at the Yotian court connected research to state power.
Through his marriage to <CharacterLink id="kayla">Kayla bin-Kroog</CharacterLink>,
he became part of a ruling family whose security and subjects had needs
that could not be reduced to the artificer's rivalry with Mishra.
Yet the expanding conflict made it ever harder to distinguish the
protection of Yotia from the pursuit of Urza's own objectives.

<CharacterLink id="tawnos">Tawnos</CharacterLink>, meanwhile, represented a
less destructive possibility for engineering. Automatons could perform work,
not only kill. That possibility did not make his position innocent: technology
built for construction and logistics could be redirected to war, and an
apprentice could find his work used in ways that troubled him.

By the time great armies assembled, the brothers were no longer the only
people shaping the conflict. Kings, commanders, inventors, laborers, and
families had all been drawn into the system that sustained it.
The brothers now possessed not only the stones
they coveted, but also the resources and influence
needed to turn their private rivalry into war.

## IV. The War That Consumed Terisiare

The conflict expanded across Terisiare.

Armies marched beneath the banners of competing
powers, and machines of increasing size and
destructive capability transformed warfare.

The destruction extended far beyond the armies
that fought in the brothers' names.

One of its most consequential episodes was the
fall of Kroog, the Yotian capital where Urza
had established his position of influence.

The attack devastated a major center of
civilian life. For

<CharacterLink id="kayla">Kayla</CharacterLink>, the loss of Kroog marked a
personal and political catastrophe.

The war had already divided the brothers.
Now it was destroying the homes of people
who had never chosen their rivalry.

<HistoricalEventReference id="fall-of-kroog" />

### The destruction of Kroog was not an abstraction

Kroog was a living capital, not merely a marker on a war map. Its people
were drawn into consequences they could neither predict nor control.
The city was devastated in retaliation following a failed peace meeting,
a sequence remembered in the flavor and illustration of *The Fall of Kroog*.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[10]}>

In this 2022 story-spotlight printing, the ruined city stands for what a
political and military rivalry cost ordinary Yotians. The image was published
millennia after the event it depicts in the fiction.

</HistoricalMoment>

For Kayla, the loss was personal: her father, home, and the political world
in which she had grown up were gone. The historical importance of the event
cannot be measured solely by which brother gained a tactical advantage.
The fate of survivors would remain a major part of the story after both
commanders' campaigns ended.
The struggle drew in inventors and researchers
whose talents were increasingly directed toward
military production.

While Tawnos worked within Urza's sphere,

<CharacterLink id="ashnod">Ashnod</CharacterLink>
became associated with the forces of
<CharacterLink id="mishra">Mishra</CharacterLink>.

Ashnod was an exceptionally inventive artificer,
but her work often involved disturbing experiments
with living bodies, powerstones, and machinery.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[8]}>

Ashnod's experiments reveal the darker possibilities
of mechanical innovation during the war.

Where some artificers attempted to imitate living
creatures through machines, Ashnod explored the
transformation of flesh itself.

Her inventions contributed to a conflict in which
the boundary between living beings and manufactured
weapons became increasingly uncertain.

</HistoricalMoment>

Yet Ashnod was not simply an extension of Mishra's
will. Her independent decisions and personal
connections would eventually complicate her
position within his war effort.

As the conflict continued, the ancient threat
of Phyrexia began to influence the war.

<CharacterLink id="gix">Gix</CharacterLink>, a praetor serving
<CharacterLink id="yawgmoth">Yawgmoth</CharacterLink>, sought to advance
Phyrexian interests on Dominaria.

The conflict between Urza and Mishra offered
an opportunity to extend that influence.
The brothers were already consumed by their
competition, and the destruction they caused
created opportunities for forces with
far older ambitions.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[4]}>

Gix represented the continuing reach of the

Mishra's growing association with that power
changed the nature of his struggle against Urza.
The war was becoming entangled with a threat
that neither brother had created.

</HistoricalMoment>

### The Third Path and the limits of allegiance

Not every scholar or mage accepted the demand to serve one brother.
The Third Path, associated with Terisia City and figures such as Hurkyl,
preserved a tradition of magical study outside the brothers' industrial
and military contest. Its existence demonstrates that their model of
increasingly destructive artifice was never the only kind of knowledge
available on Terisiare.

As the conflict grew, such alternatives came under pressure. The war
absorbed resources, threatened communities that were not its principal
combatants, and made the refusal to choose sides difficult to sustain.
Even when the major armies left a region, the social consequences of
mobilization and destruction remained.

The Third Path's learning also outlived the war. In later stories, Kayla
encounters writings associated with Hurkyl while trying to understand magic
in a devastated world. That survival gives the story an intellectual
counterpoint: knowledge could still be passed on for restoration rather
than domination.
The war devastated communities and landscapes,
while generations lived under the consequences
of decisions made by the two brothers.

Yet the histories of Kayla, Ashnod, and Tawnos
show that this conflict was never only about
Urza and Mishra.

<HistoricalEventReference id="brothers-war" />

### The failure of an easy ending

By the final campaign, the struggle had become intertwined with
Phyrexia's older ambitions. <CharacterLink id="gix">Gix</CharacterLink>
exploited the war for <CharacterLink id="yawgmoth">Yawgmoth</CharacterLink>,
and the corruption of <CharacterLink id="mishra">Mishra</CharacterLink> made the
last confrontation more than a contest between two rival artificers.

Yet describing Phyrexia as the hidden threat should not erase the brothers'
own choices. Urza and Mishra had expanded the war, supplied its machines,
and drawn other societies into it before the final revelation of Mishra's
transformation. Even without Phyrexian intervention, their struggle had
already inflicted immense harm.

Argoth therefore became the convergence of two catastrophes: a long
human war driven by ambition and the encroachment of an artificial empire
whose history reached back to the Thran.
## V. The Last Battle on Argoth

The final confrontation took place on Argoth,
an island whose forests and natural life became
caught in the destruction of the war.

By then, Mishra had been transformed through
Phyrexian corruption.

Urza confronted his brother amid the devastation,
bringing their rivalry toward its catastrophic end.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[5]}>

The Sylex was not simply another weapon in
the brothers' increasingly destructive arsenal.

Its activation unleashed a magical catastrophe
that devastated Argoth and transformed the
course of Dominaria's history.

In the aftermath, Urza emerged as a planeswalker.
The conflict had ended, but the consequences
of the blast would continue for generations.

</HistoricalMoment>

The blast devastated Argoth and ended the
Brothers' War.

Urza survived and became a planeswalker, but the
destruction also carried consequences far beyond
the battlefield.

### Argoth and the price paid by the land

Argoth's forests and inhabitants were not parties to the brothers'
quarrel, but the island became its final battlefield. The campaign and
Sylex's detonation devastated an environment that could not simply be
rebuilt like a damaged machine.

The 2022 story revisits the last battle through Teferi's temporal
observation. Its depiction makes the cost difficult to ignore: the
moment of Urza's apparent victory is also the moment a vast part of
the world is destroyed. The Sylex's effects helped set in motion the
climatic disasters and temporal consequences explored in later chapters.

## VI. The Legacy of the War

The Sylex Blast changed Dominaria.

For those who survived, the end of the fighting
did not bring an immediate return to peace
or prosperity.

<CharacterLink id="kayla">Kayla bin-Kroog</CharacterLink>
continued confronting the responsibilities of leadership as communities
struggled to recover.

<CharacterLink id="tawnos">Tawnos</CharacterLink>
also faced the consequences of the inventions that had helped sustain the
conflict. In the years that followed, the relationship between technology,
survival, and violence remained an urgent question for the devastated world.

The devastation contributed to the climatic
catastrophe that eventually brought about the
Ice Age, reshaping the world's history.

The conflict also established the significance of
Urza's long struggle against Phyrexia, a struggle
that would influence events throughout later
chapters of Magic's story.

The Brothers' War was not simply the end of a
family rivalry. It was the beginning of consequences
that would echo across the Multiverse.

### Kayla's account and the work of surviving

The war did not end for its survivors when Urza disappeared into
planeswalking. <CharacterLink id="kayla">Kayla</CharacterLink> faced the task of
sheltering people, holding communities together, and living with the losses
that had accompanied her husband's decisions. The postwar account does not
present her as a passive witness: she makes judgments about what the war
cost and what kind of future the living should seek.

In the 2022 fiction, <CharacterLink id="tawnos">Tawnos</CharacterLink>
returns with plans to employ machines for practical reconstruction. Kayla
has reason to distrust even benevolent inventions after the devastation
those same disciplines enabled. Their encounter asks a question the
brothers never solved: can artifice be governed by obligations to people
rather than by the ambitions of its creators?

<HistoricalEventReference id="brothers-kaylas-reconstruction" />

The aftermath also belongs to the history of Dominaria's Ice Age. The
survivors were confronting a world whose climate, politics, and resources
had been transformed. This belongs to the next chapter's history, not to
a neat conclusion in which the war's end restored the old order.
## VII. How Magic Told This Story

The Brothers' War is particularly interesting
because its fictional chronology and publication
history are separated by decades.

_Antiquities_ (1994) introduced the conflict
through cards representing artifacts, machines,
locations, and other remnants of the ancient war.

The novel _The Brothers' War_ by Jeff Grubb,
published in 1998, developed the historical
narrative in much greater detail.

In 2022, Wizards of the Coast returned to the
conflict through the expansion _The Brothers' War_,
offering new depictions of the central characters,
events, and machinery.

### Story dates are not release dates

The article's approximate 20–64 AR timeframe describes the ancient
fictional conflict. It should not be mistaken for publication dates.
The original *Antiquities* cards appeared in 1994, Jeff Grubb's novel in
1998, and the retrospective *The Brothers' War* set and its associated
fiction in 2022. The latter story also uses later characters investigating
events of the past; its publication does not place those ancient events
in the present-day Multiverse.

Where sources differ in viewpoint or detail, Kayla's in-world chronicle
and retrospective retellings should be read as historical testimony and
narrative framing, not as interchangeable transcripts.
These releases provide different perspectives on
the same historical period, making the conflict
an ideal example of how Magic tells its history
across multiple generations of cards.
