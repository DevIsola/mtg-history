## I. The Price of an Elder Dragon's Ambition

The <CharacterLink id="nicol-bolas">Elder Dragon Nicol Bolas</CharacterLink> had known a Multiverse in which the most powerful planeswalkers could wield almost godlike influence. The Mending altered that balance. Planeswalkers retained the ability to cross the Blind Eternities, but lost the extraordinary, nearly limitless power associated with the old spark. Bolas regarded the change not as liberation for ordinary people, but as an insult to his own supremacy.

He did not intend merely to conquer one plane. His design was to reclaim vast power by using other planeswalkers as fuel. That required a method of summoning them, a means of preventing their escape, an army capable of taking their sparks, and a way to transport that army to the chosen battlefield. These requirements explain why apparently separate stories on Kaladesh, Amonkhet, Ixalan, and Ravnica formed a single, prolonged conspiracy.

The citizens of Ravnica would ultimately experience the resulting invasion as a catastrophe without precedent. For the planeswalkers, it was the convergence of warnings many had encountered but had not yet assembled into a complete picture.

## II. The Pieces Were Gathered on Other Worlds

Bolas's agents pursued the **Planar Bridge** on Kaladesh, a device associated with the artificer Rashmi and later seized by Tezzeret. Its ability to move nonliving matter between planes became a means of transporting a specially prepared undead force. The distinction is important: the bridge did not simply move a living conventional army across the Multiverse.

### The artificer who carried an invasion

<CharacterLink id="tezzeret">Tezzeret</CharacterLink> did more than steal an
invention for an employer. He became the bridge's living bearer and
transport operator. The lazotep coating of Amonkhet's dead made the
Eternals suitable for passage through a device that was otherwise
lethal to ordinary living travelers. The bridge thus linked two
seemingly separate projects: Kaladesh's engineering and the brutal
trials through which Bolas built his undead army.

_Tezzeret, Master of the Bridge_ portrays Bolas's artificer as an
important conspirator. It does not depict the original theft of the
device or the arrival of an individual Eternal on Ravnica.

</HistoricalMoment>

On **Amonkhet**, Bolas built a civilization around his supposed divinity, deceptive trials, and the promise of an exalted afterlife. The dead selected through its brutal system were embalmed in lazotep. When Bolas revealed the truth behind his teachings, his army of **Eternals** became an instrument not of Amonkhet's salvation but of conquest elsewhere. The God-Eternals would play a central role in the later harvesting of sparks.

On **Ixalan**, the **Immortal Sun** prevented planeswalkers from leaving while it remained active. Bolas sought precisely such a prison: a device he could place on Ravnica once his prey had assembled. The Sun's earlier history and imprisonment of individual planeswalkers are not equivalent to the later mass trap; Ravnica turned an already dangerous artifact into a strategic weapon.

Finally, Ravnica offered a particularly effective lure. It was a crossroads of guild politics, a place many planeswalkers had reason to visit, and the site of an Izzet beacon capable of calling them from other worlds. Bolas also exploited factional weakness, including collaborators within the city's own ruling institutions.

## III. Ravnica: A Hostage Before the Battle

Ravnica was not merely an empty arena selected for a duel. Its population and ten guilds were living political entities with rival priorities. The Guildpact's first collapse, earlier conflicts, and the emergence of Jace Beleren as a Living Guildpact had made the city's institutions both influential and unstable. Even when guilds recognized an external threat, convincing them to cooperate would require effort.

Bolas made use of that weakness. Dovin Baan's control of the Azorius Senate and other compromised leadership helped suppress opposition. Ral Zarek and allies worked to restore communication and make the guilds face a danger larger than their customary rivalries. Ravnica's defenders would have to protect residents while organizing people who did not know, or trust, the planeswalkers gathered among them.

### Dovin and the capture of civic authority

<CharacterLink id="dovin-baan">Dovin Baan</CharacterLink> turned the
Azorius Senate's legal machinery into an obstacle for the city's
defenders. His cooperation with Bolas made the Guildpact's institutions
part of the battlefield before the Eternals appeared in the streets.
For ordinary Ravnicans, this meant that even the institutions charged
with maintaining order could be directed against those sounding the
alarm. The defenders had to resist official obstruction as well as
an approaching interplanar threat.

The confrontation was also the culmination of a long personal struggle. Jace and the Gatewatch had already met Bolas's reach across several planes. Their victories had been incomplete, and his apparent defeats rarely meant that the schemes behind them had ended.

## IV. The Beacon, the Trap, and the Dreadhorde

When the beacon drew planeswalkers to Ravnica, the Immortal Sun left them unable to depart by planeswalking. The gathered visitors were no longer a coalition that could choose when and where to fight. They were targets placed in a sealed hunting ground.

### An invitation that became a trap

<CharacterLink id="ral-zarek">Ral Zarek</CharacterLink> was among the Izzet
mages whose engineering made the Interplanar Beacon possible. The
signal could reach travelers on distant worlds, but its purpose had
been turned toward Bolas's trap. The Immortal Sun supplied the second
half of that plan by closing off planeswalking as an escape route.
The sequence matters: summoning visitors and preventing their departure
were two different magical operations, neither sufficient by itself.

The Planar Bridge then brought Bolas's lazotep-clad Eternals from Amonkhet into the city. These soldiers were not ordinary zombies. Their training and preparation made them terrifying against individual defenders, and their role in the scheme was specifically to harvest planeswalker sparks. Ordinary Ravnicans were endangered both by the invasion itself and by the urban war that followed.

## V. Why the Elderspell Mattered

The **Elderspell** was the purpose for which the components had been assembled. Its intended result was to take the sparks of captive planeswalkers, killing them in the process, and channel that energy into Bolas. To the trapped visitors, the loss of an escape route was therefore only the beginning of the threat. Bolas planned to turn their defining gift into the instrument of his transformation.

The card _The Elderspell_ visualizes the scheme directly. In game mechanics it destroys planeswalkers and transfers power to another planeswalker; in the story the process relied on Bolas's Eternals, the captive sparks, and the dragon's own magic. The game card illustrates the design but is not a procedural account of every narrative event.

### The first stolen sparks

The scheme was not only a future threat. Its victims included Domri
Rade, whose spark was harvested after Bolas's forces reached Ravnica.
The death of a young planeswalker shows why the Immortal Sun and the
Eternals had been assembled: to turn unwilling visitors into power
for another being. The spell's apparent grandeur depended upon a
sequence of individual killings.

## VI. Liliana's Bargain and the Cost of Defiance

<CharacterLink id="liliana-vess">Liliana Vess</CharacterLink> did not command the Dreadhorde because she shared every aspect of Bolas's vision. Her connection to the dragon had become a coercive bargain. The Chain Veil, her pursuit of freedom from old demonic contracts, and her dealings with Bolas had led her into a new form of servitude. By the time Ravnica was invaded, she possessed immense power but little freedom to refuse her master's orders.

As the army's general, Liliana could direct the Eternals. She also understood that turning those soldiers against Bolas would be an act of rebellion with an immediate cost: the magic of their bargain threatened to kill her. This made her decision both a moral break and a dangerous gamble, not a simple last-minute change of allegiance with no consequences.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[3]} significance={frontmatter.cards[3].significance}>

_Liliana, Dreadhorde General_ represents the contradiction at the center of the invasion: its terrifying commander is simultaneously an unwilling instrument of Bolas and a person capable of choosing to defy him.

</HistoricalMoment>

## VII. A City Tries to Become an Alliance

Bolas's invasion forced old adversaries to recognize shared interests. Planeswalkers who had never served alongside the Gatewatch met Ravnican guild members whose homes were being destroyed. Jace, Ral, Kaya, Vraska, Gideon, and others helped organize resistance, even while mistrust and conflicting loyalties impeded cooperation.

Dovin Baan's grip on the Azorius and divisions among the guilds were obstacles as real as the armies in the streets. Some defenders were preoccupied with evacuations, others with cutting off the Planar Bridge or stopping the beacon, still others with ending the Immortal Sun's imprisonment. No single intervention could accomplish all of these aims.

### Disabling an interplanar war machine

The defenders could not defeat Bolas solely by fighting Eternals.
The beacon had to stop summoning planeswalkers, the Immortal Sun had
to cease trapping them, and the Planar Bridge had to stop sending
reinforcements. Ral and other defenders worked alongside people from
rival guilds to bring down parts of this apparatus. The shutdowns
made escape and organized resistance possible, but did not undo the
sparks already stolen or the deaths already suffered.

<HistoricalEventReference id="spark-invasion-devices-disabled" />

The Firemind Niv-Mizzet represented a possible answer to the city's factional weakness. His death and subsequent return through a complex plan that required guild cooperation led to his emergence as the **Living Guildpact**. This political and magical transformation mattered independently of the fighting: it created a center of authority intended to connect the guilds rather than elevate one of them at the others' expense.

_Niv-Mizzet Reborn_ represents a Ravnican political turning point in the middle of the Multiverse-wide battle. The Firemind's rebirth was an answer to the divided city's vulnerability, although his return did not end the fight by itself.

</HistoricalMoment>

## VIII. Gideon's Choice

When Liliana turned the Eternals against her master, Bolas's binding magic punished her defiance. <CharacterLink id="gideon-jura">Gideon Jura</CharacterLink>, who had repeatedly placed himself in danger for the Gatewatch and its allies, intervened. His protective magic allowed him to bear the fatal consequence intended for Liliana. He died so she could survive the choice she had made.

Gideon's death is not an interchangeable casualty in a general account of the war. It transformed Liliana's survival into an obligation that her earlier bargains could not have predicted. The arc's culmination belonged as much to the difficult choices of individuals as to the clash of armies and ancient magic.

_Gideon's Sacrifice_ is an unusually direct correspondence between a card and a specific canonical moment. The illustration depicts the intervention that cost Gideon his life and spared Liliana from Bolas's punishment.

</HistoricalMoment>

## IX. The Dragon's Defeat and the Prison Beyond

By the battle's closing stages the alliance had disrupted the beacon, the Immortal Sun, and the Planar Bridge. The Eternals were still deadly, however, and the Elderspell had already taken lives and sparks. Bolas's plan did not fail simply because his opponents reached the citadel; he remained terrifyingly powerful even as its supporting machinery was dismantled.

Liliana ordered Eternals against Bolas himself. The God-Eternal Bontu took the sparks he had amassed, but could not contain their power and was destroyed. The stolen energy dispersed. Bolas was defeated and deprived of the power he had sought, while Ugin played a decisive role in ensuring that the dragon did not simply return to threaten the Multiverse.

Bolas's fate is often condensed to the assertion that he died on Ravnica. That is misleading. He was stripped of his spark and confined in the Meditation Realm, with Ugin serving as his jailer. The end of this war was a victory over his plan and his freedom, not proof that the dragon's existence had ended.

## X. After the War

The victory left Ravnica scarred. Its guilds had cooperated, but not without casualties, compromises, or unresolved tensions. The deaths of Gideon Jura, Domri Rade, Dack Fayden, and many less celebrated defenders remind us that the conflict did not become costless because Bolas failed. Liliana survived with a complicated legacy; the surviving planeswalkers faced the work of rebuilding relationships as well as damaged places.

### Justice after the ceasefire

The restored guild leadership also confronted the question of who had
helped Bolas. Niv-Mizzet and guild representatives identified Tezzeret,
Dovin, and Liliana as people whose actions demanded accountability,
although Liliana's late defiance made her role morally complicated.
Ravnica's leaders sought planeswalkers to pursue them beyond the city:
Ral was assigned Tezzeret, Vraska was tasked with Dovin, and Kaya was
asked to find Liliana. Victory therefore opened a new chapter of
political and personal consequences instead of ending every dispute.

<HistoricalEventReference id="spark-postwar-accountability" />

The war also revealed something structural about the post-Mending Multiverse. Planeswalkers could assemble and act across worlds, but their sparks did not make them immune to manipulation or death. Ravnicans without sparks were not merely a backdrop. Their institutions, courage, and willingness to resist made the city's survival possible.

The story moves next toward the rise of New Phyrexia and another conflict that would cross planar boundaries. That later invasion was not part of Bolas's campaign: it emerged from different histories, even though its consequences would again be felt far beyond one world.

## XI. Reading the Record

This chapter follows the convergence and major outcome of the Bolas arc rather than treating the 2019 card set as a complete transcript of the narrative. The set's art and story-spotlight cards visualize selected moments. The _War of the Spark: Ravnica_ novel, published web fiction, the preceding block stories, and the card gallery provide overlapping but not identical views of the conflict.

The distinction between **events in the Multiverse** and **when those events were published** remains important. The Planar Bridge's creation, the theft and use of the Immortal Sun, and Bolas's preparations on Amonkhet predated the war's 2019 publication. The short battle on Ravnica was the culmination of those histories, not the origin of every device and allegiance that shaped it.
