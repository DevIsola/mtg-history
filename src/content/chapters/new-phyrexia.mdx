## I. An Old Enemy in a New Body

The first Phyrexian invasion of Dominaria ended with Yawgmoth's destruction and the collapse of an empire that had seemed capable of devouring entire worlds. That was not the end of *Phyrexia*. Its power had never depended only on one ruler: it also endured through artifacts, ideology, servants, and the glistening oil that could turn living bodies into instruments of a larger design. This is the bridge between the ancient history of <CharacterLink id="yawgmoth" /> and the catastrophe that would follow generations later.

The new invasion began with a world whose creator had hoped to escape the old conflict. <CharacterLink id="karn" /> fashioned the artificial plane Argentum, later called Mirrodin, as a place of metal landscapes and constructed life. His history with Phyrexia, including traces of its oil, would haunt that creation. Memnarch's rule transformed the plane long before the praetors openly contested it. The struggle across *Scars of Mirrodin*, *Mirrodin Besieged*, and *New Phyrexia* revealed that the oil's influence had become a civilization of its own: the Mirrans fought for their home as Phyrexia took it away.

The surviving resistance did not regard this change as a change of name. Their valleys, cities, and people were still Mirrodin; the machinery built over them was occupation. Among those who refused to yield was <CharacterLink id="koth" />, a geomancer from the plane who continued to organize resistance even as the New Phyrexian praetors divided power among themselves. That distinction—the invaders' assertion of perfection against the inhabitants' claim to their home—would define the entire war.

### The people Mirrodin could not afford to forget

Long before the Invasion Tree, the Mirran resistance had learned to survive in hidden enclaves. The Phyrexians' control of the surface did not erase Mirran identity. Refugees crossed dangerous territory, kept their own names and traditions, and defended one another against contamination. Their victories rarely looked like the conquest of a citadel: they consisted of rescues, supplies delivered, and people living another day.

<CharacterLink id="melira" /> was particularly important because her body resisted the corrupting effects of Phyrexia. That gift did not make her a planeswalker or grant her the ability to defeat a praetor alone. It gave other Mirrans a chance to fight rather than succumb, and it made her central to the later Seedcore expedition. <CharacterLink id="koth" /> continued organizing resistance with the knowledge that most of Dominaria and the wider Multiverse knew little about their struggle.

## II. The Machine Orthodoxy and Its Fractures

Five praetors represented different visions of Phyrexian supremacy. <CharacterLink id="elesh-norn" /> of the Machine Orthodoxy sought not merely military victory but universal submission to a single doctrine. <CharacterLink id="sheoldred" /> advanced through infiltration and political corruption. Jin-Gitaxias pursued experimentation and forced transformation; Vorinclex embodied predatory growth; Urabrask rejected Norn's demand for uniform obedience and could become a reluctant source of opposition to her rule.

Norn nevertheless gained the initiative. Her agents acquired the means to cross worlds before her armies could do so en masse. Tezzeret's Planar Bridge, stolen from an earlier conflict, enabled praetors to travel between planes. Vorinclex brought back an essence of Kaldheim's World Tree. Jin-Gitaxias's work on Kamigawa helped make compleation of planeswalkers possible without necessarily destroying their sparks. These were separate operations, not one sudden technological breakthrough: they were the infrastructure of an invasion assembled over time.

  Norn's title proclaimed maternal unity, but her regime required coercion. The ideology of a perfect whole also made her intolerant of other Phyrexian leaders' independence.
</HistoricalMoment>

### Five competing visions of compleation

Norn's regime was powerful, but the word *praetor* did not describe a single ideology. <CharacterLink id="jin-gitaxias" /> regarded incompleteness as an experimental problem. His Chrome Host collected information about the societies it intended to conquer; where that information was accurate, its invasion tactics could be devastatingly precise. <CharacterLink id="vorinclex" /> regarded predation, adaptation, and ruthless growth as the natural order. These philosophies could both serve Norn's plans without truly sharing her theological vision.

### An experiment that changed planeswalking

On Kamigawa, Jin-Gitaxias studied the relationship between spirits and matter. He refined a process that could compleat a planeswalker while preserving the spark necessary to travel between worlds. <CharacterLink id="tamiyo" /> became an early and terrible demonstration. Her knowledge of history and story, once freely gathered for the benefit of others, was redirected to serve Phyrexia.

</HistoricalMoment>

### A furnace that refused to submit

<CharacterLink id="urabrask" /> governed the furnace layer, but did not accept that every being had to be forced into Norn's singular vision. The furnaces sheltered Mirrans under a policy of toleration that astonished the other praetors. Later, Urabrask opposed Norn more openly, collaborating with other enemies of the Machine Orthodoxy even though his purposes were not identical to those of the resistance.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[7]} significance={frontmatter.cards[7].significance}>

This *Multiverse Legends* (2023) portrait of *Urabrask the Hidden* is retrospective. The character first appeared in 2011. His refuge for Mirrans and eventual rebellion explain why an apparently unified Phyrexia could turn on itself.

</HistoricalMoment>

The invasion's machinery was therefore also a story of internal conflict. Norn's treatment of Urabrask and Sheoldred showed that the promise of perfect unity rested on punishing disobedience, including disobedience by other Phyrexians.

### The praetors disagreed about what perfection meant

New Phyrexia presented itself as a force capable of turning difference into a single perfected order, but its most powerful leaders did not share a stable interpretation of that ideal. Elesh Norn's Machine Orthodoxy centered obedience and unity under her leadership. Other praetors pursued ambitions that overlapped with hers without becoming indistinguishable from it. Their disagreements affected how resources were used, how local populations were transformed, and how the invasion was prepared.

That fragmentation did not make Phyrexia harmless. Different programs of domination could reach into the same world, sometimes producing rival strategies that were nonetheless devastating to the people subject to them. To those targeted by compleation, the distinctions between praetors offered little comfort. Yet the disagreements are historically significant because they complicate the idea of a flawless, inevitable machine. New Phyrexia was powerful, adaptive, and deeply dangerous, but it was also ruled by individuals whose rivalries could damage their own cause.

Mirrodin's transformation into New Phyrexia was an earlier disaster with a population and history of its own. The Mirran resistance did not begin on the day the Invasion Tree reached other planes. It had already lived through dispossession, the loss of familiar places, and a struggle to remain itself under an occupying order that sought to make resistance physically and psychologically impossible.

## III. Dominaria: The War No One Recognized Soon Enough

The old battleground was the natural place to test this strategy. In *Dominaria United*, Sheoldred planted sleeper agents into institutions the defenders believed they understood. Citizens, soldiers, and friends could serve Phyrexia without visibly appearing to belong to it. The threat was not simply another army crossing a border: an opponent could already stand within the command structure of the people preparing to resist.

<CharacterLink id="jaya" />, <CharacterLink id="teferi" />, Karn, and their allies faced this crisis while searching for ways to stop Phyrexia before the next invasion began. The compleation of <CharacterLink id="ajani" /> proved particularly devastating. A familiar friend became an agent of the enemy, and the confrontation cost Jaya her life. For Chandra Nalaar, Jaya's student, it was both a military loss and a personal wound that would follow her to New Phyrexia.

The campaign also reached an old survivor of the Weatherlight.
<CharacterLink id="squee">Squee</CharacterLink>, who had been condemned
to repeated resurrection by Yawgmoth's spell, was captured and
compleated by Ertai. The 2022 story *Death and Salvation* reveals
that corruption interrupted the spell's hold on his soul. Squee
chose to return for one finite life rather than regain endless
immortality. His decision connects this second Phyrexian war to
an unfinished consequence of the first.

Time itself offered the defenders one desperate possibility. Teferi reached backward to study the Brothers' War and the ancient Sylex, looking for knowledge that could be used against New Phyrexia. The 2022 publication of *The Brothers' War* revisited an ancient in-universe event, but Teferi's journey to witness that history belongs to this later conflict. The difference matters: a modern character's research in the past does not move the Brothers' War itself into the post-Mending era.

## IV. The Seedcore Mission

A coalition of planeswalkers chose to attack New Phyrexia before Realmbreaker could link it to innumerable worlds. Their intended weapon was a new Sylex; their target was the Seedcore, the center of the tree's growth. But the journey fractured under anti-planeswalking defenses, separation, injury, and the ever-present danger of Phyrexian oil. Allies were lost or corrupted. Jace, Vraska, Nahiri, Lukka, and Nissa were among those whose fates exposed how dangerous the new techniques of compleation had become.

Nissa's transformation was especially cruel because her affinity for life and land had defined so much of her identity. The corrupted <CharacterLink id="nissa" /> did not cease to be powerful; that power was redirected against the living worlds she once sought to protect. <CharacterLink id="chandra" /> would later confront the unbearable problem of resisting her without surrendering hope that she could be saved.

When the surviving mission reached the Seedcore, it was already too late to assume the Sylex could be used without consequence. Realmbreaker had begun opening connections outward. Detonating the weapon threatened worlds already touched by the tree. Kaya hesitated; Jace moved to activate it. <CharacterLink id="elspeth" /> took the Sylex into the Blind Eternities instead, saving the connected worlds from that immediate catastrophe but leaving Norn's invasion to begin.

### A failed mission can change the shape of a later victory

The effort to strike at New Phyrexia's heart forced the opposing planeswalkers to operate inside territory whose institutions and ecology had been remade around the enemy. What they encountered was not simply a fortress with a central villain to remove. The Seedcore and the preparations for Realmbreaker reflected a system capable of extending invasion across worlds, while compleated former allies made every encounter morally and tactically difficult.

These decisions required judgments under uncertainty. A spell or weapon that seemed to stop an immediate threat could endanger someone who might yet be saved; delay might give the Phyrexians time to expand; retreat might preserve a defender at the expense of another region. The original mission did not end in the clean, decisive success its planners sought, and the consequences help explain why later victory required action on several fronts instead of a single heroic strike.

The distinguishing feature of Realmbreaker was its reach. It converted a campaign that might have remained centered on Mirrodin into multiple simultaneous invasions. The distance between planes had protected many societies from the immediate consequences of one another's wars. The Invasion Tree undermined that protection. Defenders who did not know the broader conflict were suddenly facing an enemy whose resources, tactics, and ideology came from a history outside their own.


### Melira and the resistance that endured

Melira's strange immunity did not insulate her from the grief of occupation. The Mirrans had lived long enough under Phyrexian domination to know that rescue by a handful of visiting planeswalkers could not be their only strategy. She joined the expedition toward the Seedcore because she understood the layers of the conquered plane and the people still hiding within it.

<HistoricalEventReference id="phyrexia-melira-resistance" />

The contrast with Urabrask was revealing: an enemy praetor could make room for Mirran survival, but that did not make Phyrexia a safe home. The resistance did not fight for a more benevolent master. It fought to preserve the possibility of choosing its own future.



## V. Reading the Rise of New Phyrexia

The Scars-era conquest belongs to publications from 2010–2011; the Dominarian infiltration and Sylex research to 2022; the Seedcore mission to 2023. Teferi revisiting the Brothers’ War does not move that ancient war into this later era.

The original portraits of Jin-Gitaxias, Vorinclex and Melira and the later printings of Urabrask and Tamiyo preserve distinct publication contexts. Their images do not independently establish every event described here.

The failed Seedcore strike leaves Realmbreaker ready to carry the conflict across planes. Continue with [March of the Machine](../march-of-the-machine/#chapter-reader) for the invasion and its aftermath.
