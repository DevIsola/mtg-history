## I. The Weight of History

The final defeat of Yawgmoth did not restore Dominaria to its ancient condition. Wars had toppled cities, powerful magic had scarred the land, and the disappearance of whole regions had left unanswered questions that could last for centuries. In the aftermath of the Mirari crisis, the damage became impossible to ignore.

By roughly 4500 AR, nearly three centuries after the Phyrexian invasion, Dominaria was dying. Its mana was draining away through wounds in the fabric of time itself. Ordinary people saw failing lands, unpredictable magic, and fragments of bygone ages appearing in places where they did not belong.

The danger was not merely local. If these fractures continued to spread, they threatened the integrity of Dominaria and, ultimately, the interconnected Multiverse. The efforts to close them would change what being a planeswalker meant.

## II. How the Rifts Were Made

No single villain had opened every rift. The tears were an accumulated inheritance of past events—exactly the events that shaped the preceding chapters of this archive.

The Sylex Blast at the end of the Brothers' War left one of Dominaria's deepest scars. Urza's temporal research and a catastrophic accident at Tolaria added another. The Rathi Overlay at the end of the Phyrexian invasion had transplanted places from Rath onto Dominaria, disturbing Urborg and carrying Skyshroud into Keld. Later, the extraordinary birth and destruction of Karona destabilized Otaria.

These magical disasters were different in cause, but the wounds they left began to reinforce one another. They drew power out of the world. Worse, the rifts distorted time and possibility, allowing things from other eras or alternate histories to surface in the present.

The Mending was therefore more than a rescue mission. It required those who had inherited the power of earlier generations to confront the consequences of that power.

### A map of inherited catastrophes

The rifts had different origins, so no single spell could heal them all. In Shiv and Zhalfir, Teferi had displaced entire lands to escape Phyrexia. At Tolaria, experiments with time had already turned an academy into a disaster site. The Rathi Overlay left scars in Urborg and around the displaced Skyshroud forest; Otaria bore the magical aftermath of Karona.

The most disturbing consequence was cumulative. Each breach weakened the same world, so solving one local problem without considering the others risked trading one catastrophe for another. A plan that restored Shiv might leave Zhalfir inaccessible; a spell that drew upon another person's connection to a rift might heal the landscape while destroying that person's future.

## III. Teferi's Old Decision

<CharacterLink id="teferi">Teferi</CharacterLink> had been a student at Urza's
Tolarian Academy, a prodigy fascinated by the manipulation of time. During the
Phyrexian invasion he faced a terrible choice: the forces of Yawgmoth threatened
both his homeland of Zhalfir and the Shivan people whose world included his
friend <CharacterLink id="jhoira">Jhoira</CharacterLink>.

He had used extraordinary temporal magic to phase Zhalfir and Shiv out of normal reality, sparing them the immediate destruction of invasion. Yet safety had become separation. These lands and their inhabitants remained suspended beyond ordinary reach.

When Teferi returned centuries later, he hoped to restore both places. Instead, he discovered that the means he had used to preserve them had contributed to the growing temporal crisis.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[0]}>

The original _Time Spiral_ printing of Teferi presents a powerful mage whose future no longer resembles his past. His story is not simply about commanding time, but about accepting responsibility for the damage his own decisions helped cause.

</HistoricalMoment>

His first problem was immediate: bringing Shiv back carelessly could aggravate the rifts enough to destroy the world he was trying to save.

### Zhalfir: protection without consent

Teferi's choice in the invasion was neither a simple victory nor a straightforward failure. He believed the Phyrexians would destroy Zhalfir, but he had not asked a nation to surrender its place in the world. The people he saved were also separated from their neighbors, their histories, and families stranded outside the phased-out land.

The distinction matters because the Shivan solution did not resolve the Zhalfirin one. Teferi could sacrifice his own spark to return Shiv, but he could not promise to restore both places. Later accounts explicitly preserve his regret, even after the threat of the rifts had passed. The Mending repaired reality without restoring everything that reality had lost.

<HistoricalEventReference id="teferis-phasing-decision" />

## IV. Jhoira: An Engineer in a Broken World

Jhoira had known Teferi and <CharacterLink id="karn">Karn</CharacterLink> since the age of the Tolarian Academy. Her longevity and knowledge of artifice made her an unusually important witness to Dominaria's transformation.

Where powerful planeswalkers could be drawn toward solutions based on force, Jhoira looked for mechanisms, patterns, and practical ways to keep companions alive. Her loyalty to Teferi did not prevent her from questioning his recklessness.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[3]}>

Jhoira's _Future Sight_ card was published near the end of the block, although her part in the narrative begins much earlier. Her appearance here illustrates a crucial theme: mortal expertise can matter alongside the most overwhelming magic.

</HistoricalMoment>

Together, Teferi and Jhoira traveled through a Dominaria that scarcely resembled the world they remembered. Other figures they encountered would provide insights the ancient planeswalker could not have discovered alone.

### An artificer's response to broken time

Jhoira's strength was not simply that she had lived long enough to
remember Urza's Academy. She could approach temporal phenomena as
problems of observation and construction. In a company that included
planeswalkers capable of moving mountains, disciplined engineering
made her a necessary counterweight to spectacular but irreversible
solutions.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[8]}>

_Jhoira's Timebug_, printed in _Time Spiral_, takes the form of a
small artifact creature associated with adjusting time counters. It
is evidence of the set's mechanical treatment of time and an
invention associated by name with Jhoira, not a claim that this
specific device physically repaired a rift in the novels.

</HistoricalMoment>

The old Academy's experiments had helped cause one of Dominaria's
wounds. Jhoira's contribution demonstrates that the answer to
reckless research was not the abandonment of knowledge, but greater
care with what that knowledge could do to other people.

<HistoricalEventReference id="mending-jhoira-timekeeping" />

## V. Radha and the Forest of Skyshroud

In Keld, the transplanted Skyshroud forest suffered beneath the pressure of its rift. The ancient planeswalker <CharacterLink id="freyalise">Freyalise</CharacterLink> struggled to protect its inhabitants, even as her own strength diminished.

Among the warriors connected to this landscape was <CharacterLink id="radha">Radha</CharacterLink>, whose elven ancestry and Keldon heritage placed her between competing identities. She cared deeply about proving herself as a Keldon, while magical forces tied her to Skyshroud's unusual condition.

Radha could draw upon mana associated with the rift. That ability made her an invaluable subject for Teferi's investigation, but she was no passive instrument. She challenged the way ancient beings expected others to obey them and pursued a destiny of her own.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[1]}>

_Radha, Heir to Keld_ captures the warrior at the crossroads of her ancestry and ambition. Her connection to the damaged land would later make her central to another planeswalker's dangerous plans.

</HistoricalMoment>

Radha's own development showed Teferi that a living being could change the nature of a magical bond. That realization gave him a possible way to confront the Shivan rift.

### Radha is not a reservoir of mana

Radha's link to Skyshroud made her invaluable, but it also exposed an old imbalance: planeswalkers accustomed to moving nations and reshaping landscapes could mistake an individual for a convenient part of a ritual. She resisted being reduced to an instrument of someone else's rescue.

Her story links the beginning and end of the crisis. Teferi studied the way she interacted with rift mana; Jeska later attempted to draw on that connection for her own repairs. Their intentions differed, but both had to confront the cost of imposing a supposedly necessary solution on a living person.

<HistoricalEventReference id="radha-and-the-skyshroud-rift" />

## VI. Venser and the Promise of a Different Future

In Urborg, the travelers encountered <CharacterLink id="venser">Venser</CharacterLink>, a gifted artificer whose experiments in teleportation challenged established assumptions about travel and magic. He had been raised in the shadow of another powerful guardian, <CharacterLink id="windgrace">Lord Windgrace</CharacterLink>, who distrusted the technologies associated with Urza and other artificers.

Venser's devices could move objects and people in unexpected ways. More important, his own latent potential suggested that the next generation of planeswalkers might not resemble the nearly godlike figures of the past.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[2]}>

The original _Future Sight_ card depicts Venser as a brilliant, quick-thinking mage of displacement and escape. His role in the Mending became a bridge between the old age of planeswalkers and the new one.

</HistoricalMoment>

Venser's future was not predetermined. He was a vulnerable inventor pulled into a crisis involving beings who had manipulated entire nations. That contrast would become one of the defining consequences of the Mending.

### Venser's first crossing of the Blind Eternities

His first true planeswalk emerged from the same reality crisis he was
trying to comprehend. During the effort to reach Urborg, Venser and
his companions encountered a version of Dominaria in which history
had taken another course. This was not a new country on a familiar
map: the difference lay in what had happened to the entire world.

With Teferi and Jhoira's help, Venser learned to rely on the latent
spark within him rather than only his constructed teleportation
apparatus. He crossed between the divergent realities and returned
to the world he knew. Unlike the great interventions of the older
planeswalkers, this feat belonged to someone still learning what
his abilities meant and how easily he could be hurt.

<HistoricalEventReference id="mending-venser-first-walk" />

## VII. The Shivan Rift and Teferi's Sacrifice

The opportunity to restore Shiv brought Teferi's long struggle with his own past to a turning point. After observing Radha's connection to the Keldon landscape, he attempted to establish a corresponding bond with the Shivan rift.

The ritual succeeded, but it demanded the power that had made him a planeswalker. Shiv returned, and the rift was closed. Teferi survived, yet he was no longer an ageless, world-crossing being of overwhelming power. He had given up his spark.

Jhoira was furious that her friend had risked his life. Teferi, however, had shown the others that a rift could be healed by paying a real and personal price.

His loss was not the end of Dominaria's danger. The remaining rifts were still feeding on the plane, and other ancient guardians were being asked to confront sacrifices of their own.

The returned land and the absent homeland form two sides of the same decision. Shiv survived, but Zhalfir remained beyond reach. Teferi's new mortality made his earlier act of godlike intervention impossible to repeat, even when he wanted to make amends.

<HistoricalEventReference id="shivan-rift" />
## VIII. Freyalise and the Fate of Skyshroud

Freyalise had been a dominant figure in Dominarian history long before the temporal crisis. She had helped end the Ice Age, protected the elves associated with Fyndhorn and Skyshroud, and acted according to a fierce sense of responsibility toward her followers.

The Skyshroud rift became a crisis she could not defeat through ordinary force. Her people needed protection, but the continued existence of the wound threatened every inhabitant of the plane.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[4]}>

This later _Commander 2014_ card celebrates Freyalise's long-standing association with elven communities. Her final act in the Mending era was a sacrifice rather than another victory won by force.

</HistoricalMoment>

Freyalise gave her life to close Skyshroud's temporal fracture. It was an ending that reflected both her power and the limits of the old planeswalker order. She could protect the world only by accepting that she would not remain in it.

### The cost of guarding a displaced forest

Skyshroud had not always belonged to Keld. Its relocation during the Rathi Overlay made the forest both a refuge and evidence of a catastrophe far older than the crisis Freyalise now faced. Her followers needed more than another battle won against an invader: the wound threatening their home had to cease to exist.

That was the paradox of Freyalise's final act. She was a planeswalker with immense power, yet keeping faith with the community she protected required surrendering it permanently. Her sacrifice closed a particular rift; it did not erase the damage elsewhere on Dominaria.

<HistoricalEventReference id="freyalise-seals-skyshroud" />

## IX. Lord Windgrace and Urborg

The Rathi Overlay had brought foreign geography and old Phyrexian machinery into Urborg. The resulting wound was guarded by Lord Windgrace, a planeswalker who regarded the region as his responsibility and distrusted strangers arriving with elaborate plans.

His suspicion of artifice complicated cooperation with Venser and Jhoira. Nevertheless, the threat became too severe for any one guardian to address alone.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[5]}>

_Lord Windgrace_ was printed long after _Time Spiral_, but it gives this earlier character a distinct visual presence in the card archive. He is remembered for choosing to protect Urborg at the cost of his own life.

</HistoricalMoment>

Windgrace ultimately sealed the rift by sacrificing himself, leaving his influence bound to the land he had defended. His ending echoed Freyalise's: the greatest legacy of an ancient planeswalker might be the world that survived them.

### Urborg after the Overlay

Urborg's ruin was partly a legacy of Phyrexia's method of invasion. The Overlay had made the geography of Rath and Dominaria collide, leaving people and places that could no longer be cleanly separated. Windgrace's fierce attachment to Urborg explains both his distrust of artificers and why he ultimately could not abandon the rift to others.

His death was therefore not merely a second example of planeswalker sacrifice. It was a choice about stewardship: he saved a particular homeland, despite knowing that neither he nor its scars would simply vanish from history.

<HistoricalEventReference id="windgrace-seals-urborg" />

## X. Old History, Alternate Possibilities

As the crisis deepened, time itself ceased to behave consistently. Creatures, memories, and elements of distant eras emerged where they did not belong. In some places the problem extended beyond the past: alternate possibilities crowded into the present.

This gives the _Time Spiral_ block its distinctive structure. _Time Spiral_ looked backward, with mechanics and cards recalling Magic's earliest years. _Planar Chaos_ imagined changed circumstances and alternative versions of familiar ideas. _Future Sight_ turned toward possible futures and experiments that had not yet found their place in the game.

Not every future-themed card should be read as a guaranteed canonical event. The block's presentation deliberately mixed historical memory, alternate possibilities, and speculation, while the central narrative remained focused on restoring Dominaria. The archmage <CharacterLink id="jodah">Jodah</CharacterLink>, already a witness to the Ice Age, also appears among the old and new allies trying to understand the crisis.

For the Chronicles, that distinction matters. The story's internal chronology and the actual release order illuminate each other, but they are not interchangeable.

### Three ways of representing a broken timeline

The block's presentation is deliberately layered. _Time Spiral_ recalls established history, _Planar Chaos_ experiments with an alternate present, and _Future Sight_ shows possibilities rather than a single fixed destiny. A shifted-color card or a futuristic frame is evidence of the set's creative premise, not proof that its depicted scenario happened in the principal continuity.

The chapter therefore treats narrative events from the novels and later official histories differently from speculative futures shown through card design. This prevents an evocative image from being mistaken for a confirmed historical incident.

### When another Dominaria intruded

The danger behind _Planar Chaos_ was more immediate than a series of
counterfactual images on cards. Reality's boundaries were failing:
travelers could encounter a Dominaria shaped by an alternate Ice Age
and a renewed Phyrexian threat. These encounters mattered because
they threatened to turn temporary overlap into permanent damage.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[9]}>

_Reality Acid_ appeared in _Planar Chaos_ and communicates the idea
of a world coming apart. Its artwork is used as an illustrative
metaphor for planar instability, not as a confirmed portrait of
Venser's alternate-history encounter.

</HistoricalMoment>

The distinction between a real intrusion caused by the rifts and a
possible future explored through set design is essential. Neither
should be silently folded into the main timeline as if every
alternate version of Dominaria had become its lasting history.

<HistoricalEventReference id="mending-alternate-reality-incursion" />

## XI. Karn and the Wound at Tolaria

Karn's history began as Urza's silver golem, and his later transformation into a planeswalker had made him one of the surviving connections to the Weatherlight era. Tolaria's rift, meanwhile, grew from the temporal experiments and disasters of Urza's academy.

Because the wound was bound to events of the past, closing it required a dangerous journey through time. Karn traveled back to confront the origin of the fracture and succeeded in healing it, but disappeared in the process.

His absence became especially painful to his former companion <CharacterLink id="jeska">Jeska</CharacterLink>, who had known him as a mentor after her own ascension to planeswalkerhood.

The attempt to rescue Dominaria was increasingly a history of people disappearing, relinquishing power, or being lost to those who loved them.

### The danger of repairing yesterday

Tolaria's wound was not only a portal to danger in the present. It was entangled with the Academy's past, and Karn's intervention took him toward the point where Urza's dangerous temporal experiments had gone wrong. The achievement removed another threat, but it deprived the surviving travelers of the mentor who might have steadied their decisions.

That absence helps explain Jeska's vulnerability. Her grief and urgency were not new flaws suddenly created by the Mending; they echoed the way overwhelming power had already distorted lives during the Mirari crisis.

<HistoricalEventReference id="karn-heals-tolaria" />

## XII. Jeska Returns to a World of Rifts

Jeska's earlier lives had connected her to Kamahl, Phage, Akroma, Karona, and the Mirari's legacy. The disasters of Otaria had left consequences that survived for nearly two centuries. Returning as a planeswalker, she was determined to repair the damage and to understand Karn's fate.

Yet determination did not guarantee judgment. The old planeswalker Leshrac manipulated her grief and encouraged her to treat Radha's unusual affinity for rift mana as a source of power she could use without spending her own spark.

Jeska closed the Zhalfirin rift, but doing so eliminated Teferi's remaining hope of restoring the phased-out homeland through that opening. She later sealed another wound above Yavimaya, inflicting further harm in the process.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[6]}>

_Jeska, Thrice Reborn_ is a retrospective printing from _Commander Legends_, not a card released during the original _Time Spiral_ block. It illustrates the long arc from the Mirari story to Jeska's final decision during the Mending.

</HistoricalMoment>

Jeska was trying to help, but her willingness to override the warnings and autonomy of others showed how easily the methods of the old planeswalkers could reproduce the very catastrophes they hoped to solve.

### The cost paid by a homeland

Closing the Zhalfirin rift removed a dangerous opening in reality, but it also severed the remaining route by which Teferi hoped to recover his people. That makes the event one of the saga's sharpest moral conflicts. A repair can be necessary and still be an injury; the same act can look like rescue from one shore and abandonment from another.

Unlike Teferi's decision at Shiv, Jeska initially sought ways to use someone else's unique connection to the fractures rather than lose her own spark. The attempt placed Radha's autonomy at risk and moved Jeska toward the reckoning she would finally accept in Otaria.

<HistoricalEventReference id="jeska-seals-zhalfir-rift" />

### Yavimaya and the injury to an older guardian

The danger was not confined to Zhalfir. Jeska also turned to the
fracture above Yavimaya, a forest whose ancient maro,
<CharacterLink id="multani">Multani</CharacterLink>, had helped raise
Gerrard, Mirri, and Rofellos long before the Weatherlight's final
mission. The effort to close this rift placed the forest and its
protector at risk, and the repair was bound to a devastating loss.

Connecting these chapters changes the weight of the event. The loss
at Yavimaya was not merely another unnamed cost of magical repair:
it struck a guardian who had shaped the Weatherlight generation,
linking the Mending back to the earlier war against Yawgmoth.

## XIII. Leshrac, Nicol Bolas, and Madara

Leshrac was not helping Jeska out of compassion. He sought new strength and attempted to exploit her connection to Phage. His schemes intersected with those of the ancient dragon planeswalker Nicol Bolas, whose own involvement in the temporal crisis was driven by self-interest.

### The hidden price of Leshrac's counsel

<CharacterLink id="leshrac">Leshrac</CharacterLink> belonged to an
older era of destructive planeswalkers. His name was known on
Dominaria long before the temporal rifts, including through the
magic associated with the necromancer Lim-Dûl. During the Mending,
he recognized in Jeska's grief a means of advancing his own plans.

By presenting the use of Radha's connection to rift mana as a way
to save Jeska's spark, he encouraged her to spend someone else's
agency rather than confront the cost herself. He was also interested
in the lethal Phage aspect of her history. Jeska's wish to repair
what she had once damaged gave him an opening he did not deserve.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[11]}>

_Leshrac's Sigil_ first appeared in _Ice Age_ (1995). It is a
named remnant of the planeswalker's older influence, **not** a
portrait of him or an illustration of the duel with Nicol Bolas.

</HistoricalMoment>

<HistoricalEventReference id="mending-leshrac-deceives-jeska" />

Their confrontation became one of the most dangerous struggles of the era. Bolas ultimately defeated Leshrac and used the defeated planeswalker's power to seal the Madaran rift.

The episode was a sharp contrast with the sacrifices of Freyalise and Windgrace. Not every wound was closed by an act of freely chosen heroism. Even so, another threat to Dominaria had been removed.

The final and most difficult wound remained in Otaria.

### Bolas turns a rival into a weapon

The struggle between Leshrac and Bolas should not be flattened into a final heroic alliance. Leshrac sought an advantage through Jeska and the rifts; Bolas pursued his own survival and power. When Bolas used Leshrac's defeated power to address the Madaran fracture, a dangerous wound was closed through conflict between two beings whose motives were far from selfless.

The contrast with Skyshroud and Urborg is central to the Mending's history. There was no single moral pattern by which every rift was repaired: some closed through voluntary sacrifice, others through coercion and deadly rivalry.

## XIV. Otaria and the Last Rift

The devastation associated with Karona had left a fracture unlike the others. It was diffuse and deeply connected to Jeska's own history. Now that many of the other rifts had closed, there was one last opportunity to heal Dominaria before its collapse became irreversible.

Jeska worked with Venser and Radha, whose unusual ties to space, mana, and planeswalking provided the means to attempt the final repair. Their collaboration brought the three figures together despite earlier disagreements and injuries.

At the crucial moment Jeska chose not to make her companions pay the ultimate price. She sent them to safety and poured herself into closing the Otarian rift.

Her sacrifice ended the immediate temporal catastrophe and completed a change that spread far beyond a single plane. The woman who had survived the Mirari, become Phage, and been part of Karona gave her life to preserve a future for others.

### Jeska's final choice

The meeting of Jeska, Venser, and Radha brought together three lives reshaped by very different kinds of magic: the Mirari's legacy, experimental teleportation, and the strange energy of the fractures. The final ritual threatened to make Radha and Venser pay for a decision that was not theirs.

Jeska's answer marked the decisive contrast with her earlier attempts to repair the world. Rather than compel others to supply the final sacrifice, she removed them from danger and spent her own existence. It was not a reversal of all her previous damage. It was a deliberate refusal to repeat it.

## XV. The Mending of the Multiverse

The closing of the final rift brought about the Mending: a fundamental alteration in the nature of planeswalker sparks and the conditions of travel between worlds.

Before the Mending, planeswalkers commonly possessed immense, almost godlike abilities, including forms of immortality and extraordinary powers over reality. Afterward, possessing a spark no longer guaranteed that scale of power or freedom from aging and injury. Planeswalkers could still move between planes, but they were far more vulnerable and limited.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[7]}>

The _Dominaria_ Saga card _The Mending of Dominaria_ looks back on the restoration of the world. Its publication in 2018 reflects the continuing importance of the 2006–2007 storyline.

</HistoricalMoment>

The Mending also closed ordinary methods of interplanar travel that had existed in earlier periods, fundamentally altering how the Multiverse was connected. That transformation would shape the stories of the planeswalkers who appeared in later sets.

Restoration came at a cost. Teferi had lost his spark; Freyalise, Windgrace, and Jeska were dead; Karn's fate remained uncertain; and Zhalfir had not returned. Repair was not the same as undoing history.

### What changed for planeswalkers

The Mending altered the underlying conditions of the Multiverse, not just the abilities of the individuals involved in sealing rifts. A planeswalker spark still permitted travel between planes, but it was no longer the source of the vast, broadly reality-shaping power associated with most pre-Mending walkers. New walkers could be injured, age, and die; their capabilities varied with their own magic and knowledge.

That distinction also matters for publication history. The first planeswalker cards arrived in _Lorwyn_ later in 2007, following a story designed partly to make such characters workable within the game. Older planeswalkers who survived the transition did not all lose their sparks; they lived under the changed rules of a repaired Multiverse.

## XVI. The Story Beyond the Cards

The four illustrations added to this edition intentionally play
different historical roles. _Jhoira's Timebug_ names an invention
associated with an active participant; _Reality Acid_ and _Rift
Elemental_ evoke unstable time without claiming to depict particular
scenes; _Leshrac's Sigil_ was published before this story and
establishes an older textual association. Neither an artifact's
rules text nor an alternate-reality card makes every imagined scene
canonical. The novels and official retrospective accounts remain the
basis for the sequence of events.

The _Time Spiral_ block appeared in three expansions: _Time Spiral_ (2006), _Planar Chaos_ (2007), and _Future Sight_ (2007). Together they presented Dominaria as a plane haunted by the legacy of its oldest stories, while the card designs experimented with the past, alternative presents, and possible futures.

The accompanying novels offered a more detailed account: _Time Spiral_ by Scott McGough, _Planar Chaos_ by Scott McGough and Timothy Sanders, and _Future Sight_ by Scott McGough and John Delaney.

A later publishing milestone helps explain the Mending's importance outside the fiction. Planeswalker cards debuted in _Lorwyn_ in 2007, after the changes to planeswalker power had been established in the storyline. Wizards of the Coast has described the Mending as an intentional bridge between a mythology of near-omnipotent figures and a game in which planeswalkers could be characters and cards.

The effects of this transition reached far beyond Dominaria. The old world had been saved, but the Multiverse now belonged to a different kind of traveler—one capable of extraordinary acts, yet no longer beyond ordinary loss.
