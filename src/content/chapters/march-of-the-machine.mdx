## I. An Old Enemy in a New Body

The first Phyrexian invasion of Dominaria ended with Yawgmoth's destruction and the collapse of an empire that had seemed capable of devouring entire worlds. That was not the end of *Phyrexia*. Its power had never depended only on one ruler: it also endured through artifacts, ideology, servants, and the glistening oil that could turn living bodies into instruments of a larger design. This is the bridge between the ancient history of <CharacterLink id="yawgmoth" /> and the catastrophe that would follow generations later.

The new invasion began with a world whose creator had hoped to escape the old conflict. <CharacterLink id="karn" /> fashioned the artificial plane Argentum, later called Mirrodin, as a place of metal landscapes and constructed life. His history with Phyrexia, including traces of its oil, would haunt that creation. Memnarch's rule transformed the plane long before the praetors openly contested it. The struggle across *Scars of Mirrodin*, *Mirrodin Besieged*, and *New Phyrexia* revealed that the oil's influence had become a civilization of its own: the Mirrans fought for their home as Phyrexia took it away.

The surviving resistance did not regard this change as a change of name. Their valleys, cities, and people were still Mirrodin; the machinery built over them was occupation. Among those who refused to yield was <CharacterLink id="koth" />, a geomancer from the plane who continued to organize resistance even as the New Phyrexian praetors divided power among themselves. That distinction—the invaders' assertion of perfection against the inhabitants' claim to their home—would define the entire war.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[4]} significance={frontmatter.cards[4].significance}>
  Koth's story connects the lost world of Mirrodin to the invasion's final battle. His resistance predates Realmbreaker and outlasts the collapse of Elesh Norn's regime.
</HistoricalMoment>

### The people Mirrodin could not afford to forget

Long before the Invasion Tree, the Mirran resistance had learned to survive in hidden enclaves. The Phyrexians' control of the surface did not erase Mirran identity. Refugees crossed dangerous territory, kept their own names and traditions, and defended one another against contamination. Their victories rarely looked like the conquest of a citadel: they consisted of rescues, supplies delivered, and people living another day.

<CharacterLink id="melira" /> was particularly important because her body resisted the corrupting effects of Phyrexia. That gift did not make her a planeswalker or grant her the ability to defeat a praetor alone. It gave other Mirrans a chance to fight rather than succumb, and it made her central to the later Seedcore expedition. <CharacterLink id="koth" /> continued organizing resistance with the knowledge that most of Dominaria and the wider Multiverse knew little about their struggle.

## II. The Machine Orthodoxy and Its Fractures

Five praetors represented different visions of Phyrexian supremacy. <CharacterLink id="elesh-norn" /> of the Machine Orthodoxy sought not merely military victory but universal submission to a single doctrine. <CharacterLink id="sheoldred" /> advanced through infiltration and political corruption. Jin-Gitaxias pursued experimentation and forced transformation; Vorinclex embodied predatory growth; Urabrask rejected Norn's demand for uniform obedience and could become a reluctant source of opposition to her rule.

Norn nevertheless gained the initiative. Her agents acquired the means to cross worlds before her armies could do so en masse. Tezzeret's Planar Bridge, stolen from an earlier conflict, enabled praetors to travel between planes. Vorinclex brought back an essence of Kaldheim's World Tree. Jin-Gitaxias's work on Kamigawa helped make compleation of planeswalkers possible without necessarily destroying their sparks. These were separate operations, not one sudden technological breakthrough: they were the infrastructure of an invasion assembled over time.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[2]} significance={frontmatter.cards[2].significance}>
  Norn's title proclaimed maternal unity, but her regime required coercion. The ideology of a perfect whole also made her intolerant of other Phyrexian leaders' independence.
</HistoricalMoment>

### Five competing visions of compleation

Norn's regime was powerful, but the word *praetor* did not describe a single ideology. <CharacterLink id="jin-gitaxias" /> regarded incompleteness as an experimental problem. His Chrome Host collected information about the societies it intended to conquer; where that information was accurate, its invasion tactics could be devastatingly precise. <CharacterLink id="vorinclex" /> regarded predation, adaptation, and ruthless growth as the natural order. These philosophies could both serve Norn's plans without truly sharing her theological vision.

*Jin-Gitaxias, Core Augur* dates to the original 2011 *New Phyrexia* set. His subsequent research on Kamigawa should be understood as an extension of this ideology, not as an incident depicted on the older card.

</HistoricalMoment>

</HistoricalMoment>

### An experiment that changed planeswalking

On Kamigawa, Jin-Gitaxias studied the relationship between spirits and matter. He refined a process that could compleat a planeswalker while preserving the spark necessary to travel between worlds. <CharacterLink id="tamiyo" /> became an early and terrible demonstration. Her knowledge of history and story, once freely gathered for the benefit of others, was redirected to serve Phyrexia.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[16]} significance={frontmatter.cards[16].significance}>

*Tamiyo, Compleated Sage* was published in *Kamigawa: Neon Dynasty* (2022). It documents the transformation that made the subsequent invasion more frightening: planeswalkers were not immune to becoming instruments of an interplanar empire.

</HistoricalMoment>

<HistoricalEventReference id="phyrexia-jin-kamigawa" />

### A furnace that refused to submit

<CharacterLink id="urabrask" /> governed the furnace layer, but did not accept that every being had to be forced into Norn's singular vision. The furnaces sheltered Mirrans under a policy of toleration that astonished the other praetors. Later, Urabrask opposed Norn more openly, collaborating with other enemies of the Machine Orthodoxy even though his purposes were not identical to those of the resistance.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[14]} significance={frontmatter.cards[14].significance}>

This *Multiverse Legends* (2023) portrait of *Urabrask the Hidden* is retrospective. The character first appeared in 2011. His refuge for Mirrans and eventual rebellion explain why an apparently unified Phyrexia could turn on itself.

</HistoricalMoment>

The invasion's machinery was therefore also a story of internal conflict. Norn's treatment of Urabrask and Sheoldred showed that the promise of perfect unity rested on punishing disobedience, including disobedience by other Phyrexians.

## III. Dominaria: The War No One Recognized Soon Enough

The old battleground was the natural place to test this strategy. In *Dominaria United*, Sheoldred planted sleeper agents into institutions the defenders believed they understood. Citizens, soldiers, and friends could serve Phyrexia without visibly appearing to belong to it. The threat was not simply another army crossing a border: an opponent could already stand within the command structure of the people preparing to resist.

<CharacterLink id="jaya" />, <CharacterLink id="teferi" />, Karn, and their allies faced this crisis while searching for ways to stop Phyrexia before the next invasion began. The compleation of <CharacterLink id="ajani" /> proved particularly devastating. A familiar friend became an agent of the enemy, and the confrontation cost Jaya her life. For Chandra Nalaar, Jaya's student, it was both a military loss and a personal wound that would follow her to New Phyrexia.

The campaign also reached an old survivor of the Weatherlight.
<CharacterLink id="squee">Squee</CharacterLink>, who had been condemned
to repeated resurrection by Yawgmoth's spell, was captured and
compleated by Ertai. The 2022 story *Death and Salvation* reveals
that corruption interrupted the spell's hold on his soul. Squee
chose to return for one finite life rather than regain endless
immortality. His decision connects this second Phyrexian war to
an unfinished consequence of the first.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[0]} significance={frontmatter.cards[0].significance}>
  Sheoldred's Dominarian operation demonstrates how the invasion used deception and political access before the full armies of New Phyrexia were ready to move.
</HistoricalMoment>

<HistoricalEventReference id="phyrexia-dominaria-sleeper-war" />

Time itself offered the defenders one desperate possibility. Teferi reached backward to study the Brothers' War and the ancient Sylex, looking for knowledge that could be used against New Phyrexia. The 2022 publication of *The Brothers' War* revisited an ancient in-universe event, but Teferi's journey to witness that history belongs to this later conflict. The difference matters: a modern character's research in the past does not move the Brothers' War itself into the post-Mending era.

## IV. The Seedcore Mission

A coalition of planeswalkers chose to attack New Phyrexia before Realmbreaker could link it to innumerable worlds. Their intended weapon was a new Sylex; their target was the Seedcore, the center of the tree's growth. But the journey fractured under anti-planeswalking defenses, separation, injury, and the ever-present danger of Phyrexian oil. Allies were lost or corrupted. Jace, Vraska, Nahiri, Lukka, and Nissa were among those whose fates exposed how dangerous the new techniques of compleation had become.

Nissa's transformation was especially cruel because her affinity for life and land had defined so much of her identity. The corrupted <CharacterLink id="nissa" /> did not cease to be powerful; that power was redirected against the living worlds she once sought to protect. <CharacterLink id="chandra" /> would later confront the unbearable problem of resisting her without surrendering hope that she could be saved.

When the surviving mission reached the Seedcore, it was already too late to assume the Sylex could be used without consequence. Realmbreaker had begun opening connections outward. Detonating the weapon threatened worlds already touched by the tree. Kaya hesitated; Jace moved to activate it. <CharacterLink id="elspeth" /> took the Sylex into the Blind Eternities instead, saving the connected worlds from that immediate catastrophe but leaving Norn's invasion to begin.

</HistoricalMoment>

## V. Realmbreaker Opens the Multiverse

Where Bolas had confined planeswalkers to Ravnica in a single spectacular trap, Norn opened war on many planes at once. Realmbreaker grew from stolen World Tree essence and pierced the boundaries separating worlds. The interplanar bridge was no longer enough by itself: the Invasion Tree provided routes for armies, weapons, and compleated inhabitants to carry Phyrexia into places that could not otherwise be reached by ordinary soldiers.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[5]} significance={frontmatter.cards[5].significance}>
  Realmbreaker's image makes the strategic shift visible: Phyrexia had built a network of passages, allowing its war to be fought on a scale that no single defender could survey.
</HistoricalMoment>

On Kaldheim, the invaders' branches clashed with the World Tree and disturbed the realms in devastating ways. On Kaladesh, preparations enabled resistance around Ghirapur and the Aetherflux Reservoir. Kamigawa confronted the corruption of its people and technologies; Tamiyo's fate revealed that victory over a compleated planeswalker could still be a family tragedy. On Zendikar, <CharacterLink id="nahiri" />—herself compleated—turned intimate knowledge of the plane's geography against its defenders. Elsewhere, old rivalries gave way to improbable cooperation when the alternative was annihilation.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[6]} significance={frontmatter.cards[6].significance}>
  The Battle subtype introduced in *March of the Machine* turns the invasion's widespread sieges into a gameplay metaphor. Individual Battle cards represent fronts of one campaign, not a definitive minute-by-minute sequence.
</HistoricalMoment>

It would be misleading to describe this as one uniform front in which every world suffered in the same way. The invaders faced different cultures, magic, ecologies, and histories. On some planes they reached key sites quickly; on others they met prepared coalitions. The official side stories deliberately show different kinds of resistance, including choices made by people who could not planeswalk, wield a legendary weapon, or even know what the rest of the Multiverse looked like.

<HistoricalEventReference id="phyrexia-realmbreaker-invasion" />

### One invasion, different military doctrines

The Machine Legion did not simply send interchangeable soldiers to every world. Jin-Gitaxias's Chrome Host relied upon intelligence and careful adaptation to local defenses. The Furnace Host was far less centralized: its brutal assaults and experiments could have unpredictable results. Norn's porcelain armies emphasized submission and spectacle; the remaining black-aligned thanes competed for approval after Sheoldred's failure.

That difference mattered to the defenders. A tactic that succeeded against one host might fail against another, and local alliances were essential. The invasion's geographic reach did not grant Norn perfect knowledge. The official *Planeswalker's Guide* makes clear that imperfect intelligence could leave even Jin-Gitaxias's forces vulnerable to the cultures and powers they had misunderstood.

## VI. The Defenders Who Could Not Leave

For the Mirrans, this was not a war that began when foreign worlds noticed. They had been resisting for years while their own streets and landscapes were remade. Koth's refusal to abandon them and Melira's ability to resist Phyrexian corruption gave that resistance a direction distinct from the visiting planeswalkers' mission. Their fight was for the survival of a people, even if the planet beneath their feet could not be restored to what it had been.

Chandra returned to the conflict carrying grief for Jaya and fear for Nissa. Her strength could destroy enemies but could not by itself resolve the dilemma of a loved one forced to serve them. Wrenn, a dryad planeswalker who normally bonded with living trees, faced a different possibility: if a connection could be made with Realmbreaker, Phyrexia's own weapon might be turned against its maker. <CharacterLink id="wrenn" /> and her allies would have to reach the tree while the surviving Mirrans held the line.

The conflict also tested Norn's claim of unity. The praetors did not all obey her, and her brutal treatment of dissidents revealed how much of her imagined harmony rested on violence. Against that background, the defenders' cooperation mattered precisely because it did not erase their individual choices.

### Melira and the resistance that endured

Melira's strange immunity did not insulate her from the grief of occupation. The Mirrans had lived long enough under Phyrexian domination to know that rescue by a handful of visiting planeswalkers could not be their only strategy. She joined the expedition toward the Seedcore because she understood the layers of the conquered plane and the people still hiding within it.

<HistoricalEventReference id="phyrexia-melira-resistance" />

The contrast with Urabrask was revealing: an enemy praetor could make room for Mirran survival, but that did not make Phyrexia a safe home. The resistance did not fight for a more benevolent master. It fought to preserve the possibility of choosing its own future.

## VII. Elspeth Returns

Elspeth's decision in the Seedcore had not simply destroyed her. In the Blind Eternities, she confronted the history of her suffering: childhood terror under Phyrexia, the loss of homes and friends, and the fear that opposing Norn would require becoming as merciless as the enemy. Her transformation into an angel was both a supernatural event and the conclusion of an extended personal arc.

As Norn's forces pressed against the remaining resistance, Elspeth returned with radiant wings. She protected Karn and the others, defied the Mother of Machines, and created the space Wrenn needed to reach the tree. Crucially, Elspeth's appearance did not automatically end the war. Defeating a commander and disconnecting an interplanar army were different problems.

</HistoricalMoment>

## VIII. Wrenn, Eight, and the Lost Land

In another age, Teferi had phased the nation of Zhalfir out of Dominaria to protect it from Phyrexia. The Mending made its return seemingly impossible. Its people had not been living out the same centuries experienced by Dominaria, and their separation remained one of the unresolved wounds of <CharacterLink id="teferi" />'s past. During the new invasion, that old decision became central to the future.

Wrenn reached Realmbreaker and found a living remnant within the monstrous tree. She bonded with it as her eighth tree, giving it strength and direction. The instrument designed to invade planes instead opened a route to Zhalfir. Its soldiers crossed into New Phyrexia in the decisive counterattack, reversing Norn's expectation that connected worlds could only become victims.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[8]} significance={frontmatter.cards[8].significance}>
  The name *Eight* marks Wrenn's personal bond with the tree. This was an act of agency and sacrifice, not the invaders gaining a new ally.
</HistoricalMoment>

Wrenn's intervention went further than opening a gate. Zhalfir and New Phyrexia exchanged positions in the Multiverse. Zhalfir returned as a plane that could be reached; New Phyrexia was cast beyond ordinary contact. The collapse of the invasion's commanding connection left its remaining forces across worlds inert or without effective direction. Wrenn paid for this with her life, though Teferi later planted an acorn that preserved the possibility of a future for her.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[10]} significance={frontmatter.cards[10].significance}>
  The Battle card *Invasion of New Phyrexia* illustrates the extraordinary reversal: a once-isolated nation and its allies bring the fight to the invaders' own world.
</HistoricalMoment>

## IX. Karn and the End of Norn

Norn did not die merely because her plan had unraveled. After Elspeth confronted her and Zhalfir's forces broke the imperial advance, Karn faced the choice of what to do with the ruler whose ambition could reignite the war. The silver golem had long dreaded becoming a destroyer. As creator of the plane and an unwilling link in the transmission of its corruption, however, he now took responsibility for ending the threat. Karn dismantled Elesh Norn himself.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[11]} significance={frontmatter.cards[11].significance}>
  *Karn, Legacy Reforged* is an Aftermath-era portrait. It represents a survivor changed by his choices, not a claim that the plane he created was simply restored.
</HistoricalMoment>

The result was victory, but not a reset of history. New Phyrexia remained unreachable; the original Mirrodin was not returned to its survivors. Koth and other Mirrans now had to imagine a life on Zhalfir. Teferi could reunite with people he had feared lost, but even that reunion was not the same as erasing the centuries he had endured without them.

## X. Survival, Healing, and Irreversible Loss

The official ending stresses that defeating Phyrexia and repairing the damage were not interchangeable acts. Compleated soldiers across different planes became inert when the connection to New Phyrexia was severed, but that did not undo their injuries. Some would never return. Tamiyo's family on Kamigawa faced a reunion unlike the one they had hoped for. On Zendikar, corrupted structures had to be removed piece by piece. Other worlds mourned defenders and leaders whose deaths no new technology could reverse.

Nissa and Ajani survived in a state that the healers could not easily reverse. Melira offered a desperate cure, combining her own resistance to corruption with the power Karn still carried from Venser's spark. The attempt saved the two former compleated planeswalkers but cost Melira her life and Karn his remaining spark. Their recovery did not establish a universal method for restoring every victim, and it did not undo what they had done while controlled.

### Tamiyo's final story was not a cure

On Kamigawa, the compleated <CharacterLink id="tamiyo" /> turned her collection of stories into weapons. Nashi, the child who loved her as a mother, tried to reach the person he remembered. The Wanderer ultimately killed Tamiyo to stop the devastation, but her story did not end as a simple return to life or as total oblivion.

Long before her compleation, Tamiyo had prepared a magical record of her personality in anticipation of death. After her body was destroyed, this surviving memory appeared to her loved ones. The apparition was neither an uncompleated body nor proof that every Phyrexian victim could be restored. It was an expression of the storytelling magic that had always defined her.

The subsequent *March of the Machine: The Aftermath* stories and cards also revealed that numerous planeswalkers had lost their sparks. New **Omenpaths** began opening passages between worlds for travelers without the traditional planeswalking gift. These changes were neither part of Norn's intended political order nor a painless consequence of victory. They instead marked the beginning of another era—one in which ordinary movement, power, and cultural contact across the Multiverse would be transformed.

## XI. How to Read the Record

This chapter is a chronological synthesis of separate story releases, not an assertion that all its events occurred within a single card set. The Scars-era conquest of Mirrodin belongs to 2010–2011 publications; the Dominarian infiltration and Sylex research to 2022; the Seedcore disaster and multiversal invasion to 2023. A modern story involving Teferi revisiting the Brothers' War does not change the ancient position of that war in Dominaria's own chronology.

The **cards are historical illustrations**. Their artwork and mechanics select and dramatize particular moments, but not every card identifies an exact sequence of events. The official main story establishes the crisis and its conclusion; the companion Planeswalker's Guide and side stories reveal the local stakes on individual worlds. *The Aftermath* records what remained unresolved after the decisive battle.

The last century-spanning question is not simply whether Phyrexia was defeated, but what its defeat made possible and impossible. The people of Zhalfir returned; the Mirrans lost their plane yet gained a refuge; old powers diminished; new connections appeared. The next chapter begins with those Omenpaths, not with another invasion.

The card references also preserve two different moments in Phyrexia's history. The first appearances of Jin-Gitaxias, Vorinclex, and Melira belong to *New Phyrexia* in 2011. Tamiyo's compleation belongs to *Kamigawa: Neon Dynasty* in 2022, while Urabrask's illustrated *Multiverse Legends* printing comes from 2023. These cards illuminate connected events across multiple releases without collapsing the twelve-year publication history into one campaign.
