## V. Realmbreaker Opens the Multiverse

Where Bolas had confined planeswalkers to Ravnica in a single spectacular trap, Norn opened war on many planes at once. Realmbreaker grew from stolen World Tree essence and pierced the boundaries separating worlds. The interplanar bridge was no longer enough by itself: the Invasion Tree provided routes for armies, weapons, and compleated inhabitants to carry Phyrexia into places that could not otherwise be reached by ordinary soldiers.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[0]} significance={frontmatter.cards[0].significance}>
  Realmbreaker's image makes the strategic shift visible: Phyrexia had built a network of passages, allowing its war to be fought on a scale that no single defender could survey.
</HistoricalMoment>

On Kaldheim, the invaders' branches clashed with the World Tree and disturbed the realms in devastating ways. On Kaladesh, preparations enabled resistance around Ghirapur and the Aetherflux Reservoir. Kamigawa confronted the corruption of its people and technologies; Tamiyo's fate revealed that victory over a compleated planeswalker could still be a family tragedy. On Zendikar, <CharacterLink id="nahiri" />—herself compleated—turned intimate knowledge of the plane's geography against its defenders. Elsewhere, old rivalries gave way to improbable cooperation when the alternative was annihilation.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[1]} significance={frontmatter.cards[1].significance}>
  The Battle subtype introduced in *March of the Machine* turns the invasion's widespread sieges into a gameplay metaphor. Individual Battle cards represent fronts of one campaign, not a definitive minute-by-minute sequence.
</HistoricalMoment>

It would be misleading to describe this as one uniform front in which every world suffered in the same way. The invaders faced different cultures, magic, ecologies, and histories. On some planes they reached key sites quickly; on others they met prepared coalitions. The official side stories deliberately show different kinds of resistance, including choices made by people who could not planeswalk, wield a legendary weapon, or even know what the rest of the Multiverse looked like.

<HistoricalEventReference id="phyrexia-realmbreaker-invasion" />

### One invasion, different military doctrines

The Machine Legion did not simply send interchangeable soldiers to every world. Jin-Gitaxias's Chrome Host relied upon intelligence and careful adaptation to local defenses. The Furnace Host was far less centralized: its brutal assaults and experiments could have unpredictable results. Norn's porcelain armies emphasized submission and spectacle; the remaining black-aligned thanes competed for approval after Sheoldred's failure.

That difference mattered to the defenders. A tactic that succeeded against one host might fail against another, and local alliances were essential. The invasion's geographic reach did not grant Norn perfect knowledge. The official *Planeswalker's Guide* makes clear that imperfect intelligence could leave even Jin-Gitaxias's forces vulnerable to the cultures and powers they had misunderstood.

## VI. The Defenders Who Could Not Leave

For the Mirrans, this was not a war that began when foreign worlds noticed. They had been resisting for years while their own streets and landscapes were remade. Koth's refusal to abandon them and Melira's ability to resist Phyrexian corruption gave that resistance a direction distinct from the visiting planeswalkers' mission. Their fight was for the survival of a people, even if the planet beneath their feet could not be restored to what it had been.

Chandra returned to the conflict carrying grief for Jaya and fear for Nissa. Her strength could destroy enemies but could not by itself resolve the dilemma of a loved one forced to serve them. Wrenn, a dryad planeswalker who normally bonded with living trees, faced a different possibility: if a connection could be made with Realmbreaker, Phyrexia's own weapon might be turned against its maker. <CharacterLink id="wrenn" /> and her allies would have to reach the tree while the surviving Mirrans held the line.

The conflict also tested Norn's claim of unity. The praetors did not all obey her, and her brutal treatment of dissidents revealed how much of her imagined harmony rested on violence. Against that background, the defenders' cooperation mattered precisely because it did not erase their individual choices.

### Local resistance could not wait for a central command

Once Phyrexian forces arrived across the Multiverse, many communities fought with only partial knowledge of the enemy. Some possessed planeswalkers or experienced soldiers who recognized compleation; others understood that their neighbors were being attacked but had no reliable explanation for what the attackers wanted. Those asymmetries matter. A successful defense might depend on familiar terrain, an old alliance, a local leader's credibility, or the decision to protect civilians rather than pursue a distant strategic objective.

The conflict on New Capenna, for example, involved the city's existing power brokers, infrastructure, and the unusual importance of Halo against Phyrexian forces. On Ravnica, guilds confronted both invasion and the danger that familiar institutions could be compromised from within. Neither plane's defense should be reduced to a cameo by its famous characters. Each reacted through structures built by previous generations of local history.

This broad scale also limits what an encyclopedia can claim from a single illustration. An invasion card can establish that a conflict touched a plane; it cannot by itself reconstruct every battle, casualty, or political consequence there. The narrative must distinguish a documented local event from a reasonable but unverified extrapolation about what happened elsewhere.

## VII. Elspeth Returns

Elspeth's decision in the Seedcore had not simply destroyed her. In the Blind Eternities, she confronted the history of her suffering: childhood terror under Phyrexia, the loss of homes and friends, and the fear that opposing Norn would require becoming as merciless as the enemy. Her transformation into an angel was both a supernatural event and the conclusion of an extended personal arc.

As Norn's forces pressed against the remaining resistance, Elspeth returned with radiant wings. She protected Karn and the others, defied the Mother of Machines, and created the space Wrenn needed to reach the tree. Crucially, Elspeth's appearance did not automatically end the war. Defeating a commander and disconnecting an interplanar army were different problems.

## VIII. Wrenn, Eight, and the Lost Land

In another age, Teferi had phased the nation of Zhalfir out of Dominaria to protect it from Phyrexia. The Mending made its return seemingly impossible. Its people had not been living out the same centuries experienced by Dominaria, and their separation remained one of the unresolved wounds of <CharacterLink id="teferi" />'s past. During the new invasion, that old decision became central to the future.

Wrenn reached Realmbreaker and found a living remnant within the monstrous tree. She bonded with it as her eighth tree, giving it strength and direction. The instrument designed to invade planes instead opened a route to Zhalfir. Its soldiers crossed into New Phyrexia in the decisive counterattack, reversing Norn's expectation that connected worlds could only become victims.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[3]} significance={frontmatter.cards[3].significance}>
  The name *Eight* marks Wrenn's personal bond with the tree. This was an act of agency and sacrifice, not the invaders gaining a new ally.
</HistoricalMoment>

Wrenn's intervention went further than opening a gate. Zhalfir and New Phyrexia exchanged positions in the Multiverse. Zhalfir returned as a plane that could be reached; New Phyrexia was cast beyond ordinary contact. The collapse of the invasion's commanding connection left its remaining forces across worlds inert or without effective direction. Wrenn paid for this with her life, though Teferi later planted an acorn that preserved the possibility of a future for her.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[5]} significance={frontmatter.cards[5].significance}>
  The Battle card *Invasion of New Phyrexia* illustrates the extraordinary reversal: a once-isolated nation and its allies bring the fight to the invaders' own world.
</HistoricalMoment>

## IX. Karn and the End of Norn

Norn did not die merely because her plan had unraveled. After Elspeth confronted her and Zhalfir's forces broke the imperial advance, Karn faced the choice of what to do with the ruler whose ambition could reignite the war. The silver golem had long dreaded becoming a destroyer. As creator of the plane and an unwilling link in the transmission of its corruption, however, he now took responsibility for ending the threat. Karn dismantled Elesh Norn himself.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[6]} significance={frontmatter.cards[6].significance}>
  *Karn, Legacy Reforged* is an Aftermath-era portrait. It represents a survivor changed by his choices, not a claim that the plane he created was simply restored.
</HistoricalMoment>

The result was victory, but not a reset of history. New Phyrexia remained unreachable; the original Mirrodin was not returned to its survivors. Koth and other Mirrans now had to imagine a life on Zhalfir. Teferi could reunite with people he had feared lost, but even that reunion was not the same as erasing the centuries he had endured without them.

<HistoricalEventReference id="phyrexia-norns-fall" />


### The ending of Norn's rule left a different Multiverse

The defeat of Elesh Norn and the displacement of New Phyrexia ended an immediate project of conquest, but they did not restore the people and places touched by it to an untouched past. Compleation, death, separation, and the experience of occupation remained part of many survivors' histories. The Mirrans' survival and Zhalfir's return carried particular significance because each community had endured a different kind of displacement. Their futures could not simply be merged into a narrative of everyone going home.

The consequences for travel between planes were equally profound. Omenpaths changed which people might cross worlds, and the loss of many planeswalkers' sparks changed who could control that movement. These developments belong to the aftermath rather than to the old invasion plan's original purpose. The distinction helps explain why the Omenpath era should be a separate chapter: the same war created conditions that different peoples would experience as opportunity, danger, or grief.

The final question is not whether the Multiverse has become safe. It is whether worlds newly exposed to one another can build forms of knowledge and cooperation strong enough to respond to future crises without repeating the Phyrexians' logic of forced unity.

## X. Survival, Healing, and Irreversible Loss

The official ending stresses that defeating Phyrexia and repairing the damage were not interchangeable acts. Compleated soldiers across different planes became inert when the connection to New Phyrexia was severed, but that did not undo their injuries. Some would never return. Tamiyo's family on Kamigawa faced a reunion unlike the one they had hoped for. On Zendikar, corrupted structures had to be removed piece by piece. Other worlds mourned defenders and leaders whose deaths no new technology could reverse.

Nissa and Ajani survived in a state that the healers could not easily reverse. Melira offered a desperate cure, combining her own resistance to corruption with the power Karn still carried from Venser's spark. The attempt saved the two former compleated planeswalkers but cost Melira her life and Karn his remaining spark. Their recovery did not establish a universal method for restoring every victim, and it did not undo what they had done while controlled.

### Tamiyo's final story was not a cure

On Kamigawa, the compleated <CharacterLink id="tamiyo" /> turned her collection of stories into weapons. Nashi, the child who loved her as a mother, tried to reach the person he remembered. The Wanderer ultimately killed Tamiyo to stop the devastation, but her story did not end as a simple return to life or as total oblivion.

Long before her compleation, Tamiyo had prepared a magical record of her personality in anticipation of death. After her body was destroyed, this surviving memory appeared to her loved ones. The apparition was neither an uncompleated body nor proof that every Phyrexian victim could be restored. It was an expression of the storytelling magic that had always defined her.

<HistoricalEventReference id="phyrexia-tamiyos-last-story" />

<HistoricalEventReference id="phyrexia-meliras-last-gift" />

The subsequent *March of the Machine: The Aftermath* stories and cards also revealed that numerous planeswalkers had lost their sparks. New **Omenpaths** began opening passages between worlds for travelers without the traditional planeswalking gift. These changes were neither part of Norn's intended political order nor a painless consequence of victory. They instead marked the beginning of another era—one in which ordinary movement, power, and cultural contact across the Multiverse would be transformed.


## XI. How to Read the Record

This chapter follows the interplanar invasion and its aftermath in the 2023 March of the Machine stories. Mirrodin’s earlier fall, the praetors’ preparations and the Seedcore strike are covered in [Rise of New Phyrexia](../new-phyrexia/#chapter-reader). The original main story, side stories and Planeswalker’s Guide have different scopes; individual card images cannot substitute for those accounts.

Zhalfir returned, the Mirrans gained a refuge, and the defeated invasion left losses that could not simply be reversed. Continue with [the Era of Omenpaths](../omenpaths/#chapter-reader) for the new roads and diminished sparks.
