## I. The War That Never Ended

The Brothers' War ended with a catastrophe. The Ice Age
eventually passed. Kingdoms rose, fell, and gave way to
new civilizations.

But one conflict remained unfinished.

Urza had survived the destruction of Argoth and become
a planeswalker. Over the centuries that followed, he
dedicated himself to confronting Phyrexia.

The artificial plane ruled by Yawgmoth had already
influenced his brother Mishra and helped turn a
family rivalry into a world-changing war.

Urza understood that Phyrexia remained a danger to
Dominaria, even when its armies could not be seen.

Yet fighting Phyrexia would require more than powerful
spells or a conventional army.

Yawgmoth's forces could travel between planes,
corrupt living beings, and develop machines
designed for conquest.

Urza's answer would take centuries to prepare.

### Bloodlines, Time Experiments, and the Price of Preparation

Urza did not merely search for an army. He sought to prepare a future
champion: a person capable of assembling and activating the Legacy
when the invasion finally arrived. Over generations, his bloodlines
project shaped the families and circumstances from which
<CharacterLink id="gerrard">Gerrard</CharacterLink> would emerge.
The idea that a child could be born into someone else's elaborate
strategy is central to the saga's question of free will.

The Tolarian Academy supplied another part of the design. There,
Urza and <CharacterLink id="barrin">Barrin</CharacterLink> trained
artificers and mages while dangerous investigations into time and
power exposed students and colleagues to extraordinary risks.
<CharacterLink id="karn">Karn</CharacterLink> was created as a silver
golem able to endure and participate in temporal experiments.
His later compassion stands in stark contrast to the way Urza
sometimes evaluated lives as expendable components of a plan.

<HistoricalEventReference id="weatherlight-bloodlines-project" />

The Legacy was thus a technical undertaking and an ethical problem.
Urza intended to save Dominaria, but the people expected to achieve
that goal did not necessarily consent to the roles assigned to them.

## II. The Legacy and the Skyship

At the center of Urza's plans was the Legacy:
a collection of artifacts and living participants
intended to become a weapon against Phyrexia.

The Legacy was not a single object that could
simply be discovered and activated.

Its components were scattered, and their ultimate
purpose would only become clear when they were
brought together.

Among its most important elements was <CharacterLink id="karn">Karn</CharacterLink>,
a silver golem created by Urza.

Unlike many of the destructive machines built
throughout Dominaria's history, Karn developed
a gentle nature and a reluctance to harm others.

The Weatherlight was another essential part
of the plan.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[0]} significance={frontmatter.cards[0].significance}>

The Weatherlight was a remarkable flying ship
capable of traveling between planes.

Its mission would bring its crew into contact
with people, artifacts, and dangers across
the Multiverse.

The ship was both a means of exploration and
a vessel through which pieces of the Legacy
could eventually be reunited.

</HistoricalMoment>

What appeared to be a series of adventures
was connected to a much older design.

The fate of the ship and the destiny of its
crew would become inseparable.

### Serra's Realm: The Ship's Costly Source of Power

The Weatherlight's capacity to cross planes came at a cost seldom
apparent to those who first encountered the vessel. When Urza escaped
a confrontation with Phyrexian forces, he had found refuge in the
artificial plane created by Serra. Phyrexian infiltration subsequently
corrupted this sanctuary and contributed to Serra's departure.

The angel <CharacterLink id="radiant">Radiant</CharacterLink>
assumed guardianship of the realm. Her efforts to root out corruption
became increasingly severe, and distrust took hold among those she
believed she was protecting. With the plane already unstable, the
Weatherlight's intervention helped evacuate inhabitants willing to
leave. The plane's remaining energies, including Radiant's light,
were gathered into the great powerstone used to drive the skyship.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[18]} significance={frontmatter.cards[18].significance}>

**Radiant, Archangel** is an original _Urza's Legacy_ illustration
of the guardian who inherited Serra's burden. Her story connects the
invasion of an apparently peaceful realm to the manufacture of the
Weatherlight's engine. This card is not a depiction of the final
invasion battle on Dominaria.

</HistoricalMoment>

<HistoricalEventReference id="weatherlight-serras-realm" />

The ship that carried hope across the Multiverse was consequently
powered in part by the destruction of another world. That painful
contradiction complicates the heroic image of Urza's grand design.


### The Legacy was a network of relationships, not merely equipment

Urza's Legacy project was constructed across an enormous span of time, but its surviving pieces had meanings of their own before they could function together. The Weatherlight was a vehicle and a community; Karn was a person who remembered and chose; the other artifacts could be scattered, stolen, or misunderstood by people whose ambitions had little to do with Urza's plan. Calling them components of a weapon describes what Urza intended, not everything they were.

Gerrard's reluctance follows from this difference. To Urza, a carefully arranged lineage and a set of artifacts were means of reaching a predetermined end. To Gerrard, the pressure to accept that plan had already cost friends and a measure of freedom. Sisay did not solve that conflict simply by identifying the correct object or telling him that prophecy demanded obedience. She helped assemble a crew whose loyalties could sustain difficult decisions, even when the purpose of the journey remained contested.

The crew's conflicting temperaments therefore mattered strategically. Tahngarth's directness, Hanna's engineering, Karn's restraint, and the judgments of other companions made the ship more than an extension of one commander's will. Personal disagreements could endanger a mission, but they could also keep its members from mistaking obedience for wisdom. The conflict between Urza's long design and the crew's evolving trust runs through the entire saga.

## III. Sisay and the Gathering of the Crew

<CharacterLink id="sisay">Captain Sisay</CharacterLink> commanded the
Weatherlight during its search for the scattered
pieces of the Legacy.

Her crew included individuals with very different
backgrounds and loyalties.

<CharacterLink id="hanna">Hanna</CharacterLink> served as the
ship's navigator and an expert in magical artifacts,
while Orim provided healing and medical knowledge.

Hanna had studied at Tolaria and was the daughter of
Barrin, Urza's trusted colleague. Her decision to pursue
artifice rather than follow her father's preferred path
reflected her independent judgment.

She also shared a complicated history with Gerrard:
they had been close before he abandoned the ship and
the destiny Sisay hoped he would embrace.

Tahngarth, the minotaur warrior, brought
formidable strength to the ship.

Squee, a goblin, became one of its most
unusual and enduring companions.

Karn represented both the crew's capacity
for compassion and the deeper mystery
of the Legacy.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[1]} significance={frontmatter.cards[1].significance}>

Sisay understood that the artifacts carried
and sought by the Weatherlight were important.

Her leadership held together a crew whose
personal ambitions did not always align
with its greater mission.

When Sisay was captured by agents of Volrath,
the search for the Legacy became an immediate
rescue operation.

</HistoricalMoment>

Sisay was more than a captain carrying orders. Her faith
in Gerrard's eventual return helped preserve the crew's
purpose while he resisted the Legacy's demands.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[5]} significance={frontmatter.cards[5].significance}>

Hanna's technical expertise kept the Weatherlight and
its irreplaceable artifacts functioning during an
extraordinary journey. Her knowledge of machinery and
artifice brought practical insight to decisions that
others approached as questions of duty or destiny.

</HistoricalMoment>

The crew sought <CharacterLink id="gerrard">Gerrard Capashen</CharacterLink>, a warrior
whose life was bound to the Legacy's purpose.

Gerrard had previously left the ship after
personal tragedy.

The danger to Sisay drew him back toward
a destiny he had tried to avoid.

### Gerrard, Mirri, Rofellos, and Their Teacher

Long before the mission to Rath, Gerrard had been educated by
<CharacterLink id="multani">Multani</CharacterLink>, the maro who
embodied the forests of Yavimaya. Two other pupils shared this
formative period: <CharacterLink id="mirri">Mirri</CharacterLink> and
the Llanowar elf <CharacterLink id="rofellos">Rofellos</CharacterLink>.
Their relationship was older and more personal than the eventual
mission assigned to Gerrard through the Legacy.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[19]} significance={frontmatter.cards[19].significance}>

**Multani, Maro-Sorcerer** represents an elemental teacher rather
than a conventional academy lecturer. His appearance in _Urza's
Legacy_ retrospectively illustrates the education of protagonists
already active in the _Weatherlight_ set.

</HistoricalMoment>

<HistoricalEventReference id="weatherlight-yavimaya-training" />

Rofellos subsequently served alongside Gerrard and Mirri aboard the
Weatherlight. His death preceded the opening Rath rescue narrative.
It helped shatter Gerrard's confidence in his assigned destiny and
contributed to his departure from the crew. The separation also
wounded his relationship with Hanna and Sisay, who remained committed
to the mission even when Gerrard turned away from it.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[20]} significance={frontmatter.cards[20].significance}>

**Rofellos, Llanowar Emissary** was printed during the flashback
_Urza's Destiny_ block. Its illustration makes a lost companion
visible even though the main voyage began after his death.

</HistoricalMoment>

<HistoricalEventReference id="weatherlight-rofellos-loss" />

This history makes the rescue of Sisay more meaningful: Gerrard
returned not because he had suddenly forgotten Rofellos, but
because a person he still cared for needed help. The conflict
between chosen loyalty and inherited duty continued to define him.
## IV. Rath and the Shadow of Volrath

The rescue mission led the Weatherlight
to Rath, an artificial plane deeply
connected to Phyrexia.

Rath was governed by <CharacterLink id="volrath">Volrath</CharacterLink>,
a shapeshifter whose service to Phyrexia made him
one of the Weatherlight crew's most formidable enemies.

Before becoming the evincar of Rath, Volrath was known
as Vuel. He had grown up alongside Gerrard under Sidar
Kondo's care, but jealousy and resentment turned their
bond into an enduring rivalry.

Sisay's kidnapping thus drew Gerrard into a struggle
with someone from his own past, not merely the ruler
of a distant enemy plane.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[2]} significance={frontmatter.cards[2].significance}>

As Rath's evincar, Volrath controlled
the Stronghold and commanded forces
that threatened the Weatherlight crew.

His ability to assume other identities
made deception one of his most dangerous
weapons.

Rath itself was part of a larger strategy:
the artificial plane was being prepared
to help Phyrexia invade Dominaria.

</HistoricalMoment>

The Weatherlight's arrival brought the
crew into conflict with Volrath's servants,
including the fearsome Greven il-Vec.

Separated and pursued, the companions
had to rely on allies within Rath.

Their rescue mission exposed them to a
world whose existence had been shaped
by Yawgmoth's ambitions.


### Rath's inhabitants were not props in the rescue mission

For the Weatherlight crew, Rath first appeared as hostile territory controlled by Volrath. For those who lived there, it was a political and physical environment shaped by domination, forced displacement, and the expectation that the Stronghold's rulers could decide the fate of entire peoples. The struggle against Volrath therefore involved more than rescuing a few captives. It brought the protagonists into a world whose victims and resistance had histories before the skyship arrived.

Volrath's ability to manipulate appearances sharpened an already difficult problem. The crew needed help from people whose motives it did not fully understand, while the enemy could exploit fear for loved ones or present a familiar face under false pretenses. Starke's bargains and his concern for his daughter illustrate why information could be both valuable and suspect. Someone cooperating with a tyrant might be trying to survive or save a captive; someone offering a persuasive account might be deliberately engineering a trap.

The escape from Rath also produced consequences that could not be contained within the episode. Ertai's fate and the crew's incomplete knowledge of whom they had brought away fed later developments. A successful departure from one dangerous place was not proof that all allies had been recovered, all enemies identified, or all debts paid. The saga's scope expands precisely because its victories leave unfinished business.

## V. The Stronghold and the Cost of Escape


The Weatherlight's effort to rescue Sisay took the crew
into the dangerous fortress of <CharacterLink id="volrath">Volrath</CharacterLink>.
But the Stronghold held more than military threats.
It also brought a supernatural curse to its terrible conclusion.

<CharacterLink id="crovax">Crovax</CharacterLink>, a nobleman who
had joined the voyage, was searching for the angel Selenia.
He had once freed her from a cursed artifact, only to lose her
when Volrath drew her into his service on Rath.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[10]} significance={frontmatter.cards[10].significance}>

Selenia was the angel at the center of Crovax's tragedy.
Within the Stronghold, she confronted the Weatherlight crew.
The encounter forced Crovax to choose between saving a
companion and sparing the angel he loved.

</HistoricalMoment>

When Selenia attacked, <CharacterLink id="mirri">Mirri</CharacterLink>
intervened and suffered severe injuries.
Crovax killed Selenia to save the wounded cat warrior.
His act fulfilled the curse and transformed him into a vampire.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[7]} significance={frontmatter.cards[7].significance}>

_Crovax the Cursed_ presents a former companion in the
form created by Selenia's death. His vampirism was not an
innate condition when he first sailed with the Weatherlight:
it was the consequence of a specific tragedy on Rath.

</HistoricalMoment>

<HistoricalEventReference id="selenia-curse-crovax" />

Mirri had fought beside Gerrard since their early years.
She had been wounded while protecting the crew, but her
loyalty and concern for Crovax did not disappear.

After Crovax departed from the Weatherlight, Mirri followed
him. The cursed former companion killed her, ending a
friendship that had once helped hold the ship's crew together.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[8]} significance={frontmatter.cards[8].significance}>

_Mirri, Cat Warrior_ preserves the original version of the
Weatherlight fighter in _Exodus_. Her death was part of the
established story, not the alternative fate later explored
in _Planar Chaos_.

</HistoricalMoment>

<HistoricalEventReference id="mirris-final-confrontation" />

The rescue mission also depended on
<CharacterLink id="ertai">Ertai</CharacterLink>, a gifted young
wizard trained in Tolaria. The Predator had damaged the
Weatherlight's planeshifting system, forcing the crew to
seek another route away from Rath.

Ertai worked to open the escape portal. When the crew was
delayed at the Stronghold, the ship had to use the Legacy's
Skyshaper to reach the portal before it closed.
The Weatherlight escaped, but Ertai was left behind.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[9]} significance={frontmatter.cards[9].significance}>

_Ertai, Wizard Adept_ depicts the capable but boastful mage
before his later transformation. His abandonment on Rath
was one of the voyage's most consequential losses: the
wizard who made the escape possible could not join it.

</HistoricalMoment>

<HistoricalEventReference id="ertai-stranded-on-rath" />

Sisay was rescued, but the crew did not leave intact.
Mirri was dead, Crovax had broken from his companions,
and Ertai remained stranded on an enemy plane.
The struggle against Phyrexia had become intensely personal.

## VI. Mercadia and the Hidden Fleet

The Weatherlight's escape from Rath
carried the surviving crew to Mercadia.

This unfamiliar plane presented challenges
very different from those of Volrath's
Stronghold.

Trade, political maneuvering, and conflicting
local interests complicated the crew's
efforts to recover and repair their ship.

Volrath's influence also followed them.

Disguised as Takara, Starke's daughter, Volrath
had accompanied the rescued crew off Rath. The
shapeshifter used the disguise to sow distrust,
concealing his true identity until events on
Mercadia exposed him.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[6]} significance={frontmatter.cards[6].significance}>

_Unmask_ depicts Volrath emerging from Takara's
appearance. The card captures one of the saga's
most consequential acts of deception: a Phyrexian
agent moving among the heroes he had tormented.

</HistoricalMoment>

Volrath murdered Starke while still concealed
among the companions, showing how little safety
the Weatherlight's escape from Rath had actually
provided.

<HistoricalEventReference id="volrath-unmasked" />

### Mercadia's Social Order and the Concealed Shipyards

The inverted mountain above Mercadia City housed more than an
astonishing settlement. The Kyren goblins occupied privileged
positions within a mercantile political system in which trade,
contracts, and hidden allegiances mattered as much as battlefield
strength. Other communities, including the Cho-Arrim rebels, had
reasons to distrust the authorities that controlled the city.

The crew's ship was seized, and repairing it required the companions
to negotiate through unfamiliar institutions rather than simply
overpower an enemy stronghold. Mercadia therefore broadened the
saga beyond the personal rivalry with Volrath: corruption could be
embedded in commerce and government as well as in military command.

The colossal Phyrexian fleet concealed beneath the mountain
confirmed the larger pattern. Mercadia's rulers were aiding the
invasion of Dominaria. The Weatherlight's departure carried a
warning that the threat came from coordinated preparations on
multiple planes, not one ruler's private schemes.

<HistoricalEventReference id="weatherlight-mercadian-fleet" />

Even the goblin cabin boy <CharacterLink id="squee">Squee</CharacterLink>
had become part of the ship's unusual social fabric. His apparent
lack of status did not diminish his loyalty or his place among
companions who depended on one another to survive.

More importantly, the crew discovered that
Mercadia had become another part of the
Phyrexian invasion preparations.

A hidden fleet of warships was being built
beneath the great inverted mountain
of Mercadia City.

The threat was no longer limited to Rath.

Phyrexia had established preparations
across multiple worlds.

The Weatherlight eventually escaped
Mercadia and returned toward Dominaria
with the knowledge that an invasion
was approaching.

Events on Rath continued while the Weatherlight pursued its
mission across other planes. Crovax's transformation had
separated him from his former companions, and his ambitions
turned toward the leadership of the Phyrexian-controlled world.

When Volrath returned from Mercadia, he and Crovax fought
for the office of evincar. With Ertai's assistance, Crovax
killed his former master and took his place as ruler of Rath.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[11]} significance={frontmatter.cards[11].significance}>

_Ascendant Evincar_ depicts Crovax after his victory over
Volrath. The card does not portray the original curse, but
a later phase of the same character's descent into Phyrexian
power.

</HistoricalMoment>

Ertai's story also continued in a darker direction. His
experiences on Rath exposed him to Phyrexian transformation,
and the young wizard who had saved the Weatherlight became an
agent of those he had once resisted.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[12]} significance={frontmatter.cards[12].significance}>

_Ertai, the Corrupted_ contrasts with _Ertai, Wizard Adept_:
two printings that capture the same person at very different
points in the Weatherlight Saga.

</HistoricalMoment>

## VII. Urza's Long Design Revealed

The story of the Weatherlight cannot be
understood without returning to Urza's
earlier preparations.

Across the centuries, Urza had pursued
knowledge, powerful artifacts, and
allies capable of resisting Phyrexia.

His work involved the Tolarian Academy,
the creation of Karn, and long-term
plans that would affect future generations.

These preparations were extensive,
but they were not without moral cost.

Urza's determination to defeat Yawgmoth
frequently led him to treat other lives
as parts of a larger design.

Gerrard's place in the Legacy was
therefore more than coincidence.

The Weatherlight crew carried the
consequences of decisions made long
before many of them were born.

Much of this earlier history would
be revealed to Magic readers through
stories published after the first
Weatherlight adventures.

The difference between the publication
order and the fictional chronology
is essential to understanding the saga.

### Barrin: A Scholar Within Urza's Machinery

As headmaster of the Tolarian Academy, Barrin trained generations of
mages while assisting Urza's resistance. His daughter
<CharacterLink id="hanna">Hanna</CharacterLink> took a different path:
she served aboard the Weatherlight as a navigator and engineer.
Their disagreements over her choices never made her life less
precious to him.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[17]} significance={frontmatter.cards[17].significance}>

**Barrin, Master Wizard** was first printed in _Urza's Saga_,
which revisited the centuries before the invasion. Barrin's
ultimate fate would unfold later, when the war finally reached
Tolaria and his family suffered irreparable losses.

</HistoricalMoment>

Barrin's position exposes a second personal cost of Urza's plan.
No amount of foreknowledge or technical mastery guaranteed that
those who spent their lives preparing could protect their own
families when Phyrexia arrived.


### The difference between a secret plan and a public war

Urza had spent generations preparing weapons, bloodlines, and exceptional allies against the possibility of another Phyrexian assault. Dominaria, however, was not a laboratory whose populations could be rearranged entirely around those preparations. The invasion tested the ability of a real world to respond: cities needed warning, armies needed cooperation, and local defenders needed reasons to trust commanders whose strategic goals were sometimes concealed.

This is one reason the Coalition matters. Different peoples had old grudges, unequal resources, and their own histories with Urza. The threat of Yawgmoth created a reason to act together without erasing those differences. Defending a home was not always the same motive as completing the Legacy. Some allies fought to keep families alive or prevent the loss of a particular place; others understood that the war might reach worlds beyond Dominaria if Phyrexia prevailed.

The consequences were unevenly distributed. A battle won at one site could leave another region devastated. The Rathi Overlay transformed the geography of the struggle rather than simply adding more enemy soldiers, and ordinary inhabitants faced losses that a map of troop movements cannot convey. The story's scale should therefore move between strategic decisions and lived consequences. Urza's preparation helps explain the available weapons, but it does not make the people who used them replaceable.

## VIII. The Invasion of Dominaria

Phyrexia's preparations finally became an open invasion.
Portals opened across Dominaria, sending armies into lands
whose peoples had spent generations pursuing their own
conflicts. Benalia was among the territories struck by
the invading forces.

The defenders could not survive as isolated armies. The
Coalition brought together peoples and commanders with
very different traditions: Gerrard and the Weatherlight,
Benalian soldiers, elves, and other allies who had been
rivals before Phyrexia threatened their world.

The assault on Koilos, the ancient gateway between
Dominaria and Phyrexia, became one of the Coalition's
major victories. Yet that victory was not the end of
the invasion. Rath's purpose as a weapon had still
not been fully revealed.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[13]} significance={frontmatter.cards[13].significance}>

The original _Coalition Victory_ celebrates the
possibility of resistance through unity. The card
is a useful record of the Coalition's identity,
not a claim that the Battle of Koilos itself
permanently defeated Yawgmoth.

Its victory came at terrible cost. The defenders
could drive back armies without restoring the
people already lost to the war.

</HistoricalMoment>

Among those losses was <CharacterLink id="hanna">Hanna</CharacterLink>,
the Weatherlight's navigator, who died from a
Phyrexian plague. Her death also struck
<CharacterLink id="gerrard">Gerrard</CharacterLink> personally,
intensifying the grief that Yawgmoth would later exploit.

<HistoricalEventReference id="hanna-phyrexian-plague" />

### The Destruction of Tolaria

For Barrin, news of Hanna's death was devastating. His wife Rayne
had also died during the conflict, and returning to Tolaria brought
him face to face with a beloved place overtaken by Phyrexian forces.
The academy that had trained defenders of Dominaria could not be
recovered without another terrible price.

Barrin cast the forbidden spell known as **Obliterate**, annihilating
the area and himself. The card _Obliterate_ became one of the
recognizable memorials to the cost of the invasion. His act did not
replace the Coalition's wider resistance, but it left a scar on
the very institution that had enabled Urza's preparations.

## IX. The Rathi Overlay and the Nine Titans

The seeming respite after Koilos was brief. The artificial
plane of Rath had been prepared to merge with Dominaria,
transferring its strongholds, populations, and armies into
the heart of the defenders' world.

The Rathi Overlay transformed the battlefield. Territory
formerly separated by the Blind Eternities now existed
on Dominaria itself. The Stronghold became a focal point
of the continuing war, while survivors of Rath found
their futures bound to an unfamiliar world.

<HistoricalEventReference id="rathi-overlay" />

The invasion also had another front. <CharacterLink id="urza">Urza</CharacterLink>
led the Nine Titans, a group of nine planeswalkers who
attacked Phyrexia directly using enormous Titan Engines.
They included <CharacterLink id="freyalise">Freyalise</CharacterLink>
and <CharacterLink id="windgrace">Lord Windgrace</CharacterLink>,
whose own histories and loyalties differed markedly
from Urza's.

One of the Titans was <CharacterLink id="tevesh-szat">Tevesh Szat</CharacterLink>.
Long before the invasion, Szat had spread manipulation
and destruction across Dominaria. Bringing him into the
mission illustrated Urza's willingness to recruit people
he considered dangerous if they served his ultimate goal.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[14]} significance={frontmatter.cards[14].significance}>

The later _Commander Legends_ card depicts Tevesh Szat,
whose place among the Nine Titans became a critical
failure point in Urza's plan. This card is a character
portrait, not an illustration of the Titan Engines.

Szat betrayed the group and killed the planeswalkers
Daria and Kristina. Urza had anticipated this treachery;
he killed Szat and used his life essence to power the
soul bombs intended to devastate Phyrexia.

</HistoricalMoment>

Urza's strategy damaged the enemy plane, but the mission
also exposed how readily he could sacrifice allies as
pieces of a design. The remaining Titans faced losses,
while Urza's fascination with Phyrexia's artifice
threatened his own resolve.

## X. Yawgmoth's Arena

The final confrontation was as much a test of desire
as a contest of magical power. Yawgmoth exploited the
grief of Gerrard and the obsession of Urza, offering
each what he most wanted: Hanna's return for Gerrard,
and access to Phyrexia's deepest secrets for Urza.

Their long struggle against the same enemy gave way
to a horrifying spectacle. Under Yawgmoth's influence,
the two fought in an arena controlled by the god they
had spent their lives trying to defeat.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[15]} significance={frontmatter.cards[15].significance}>

The original _Phyrexian Arena_ depicts the personal
corruption of the war's final stage. The champions
of Dominaria became adversaries while Yawgmoth
controlled the terms of their fight.

Gerrard ultimately beheaded Urza. The planeswalker
did not immediately cease to exist: his living head,
containing the Mightstone and Weakstone, would still
be essential to the Legacy's completion.

</HistoricalMoment>

<HistoricalEventReference id="yawgmoths-arena" />

Yawgmoth then manifested on Dominaria as an overwhelming,
deadly presence. His power threatened the armies that
had resisted the invasion and rendered conventional
victory nearly impossible. The defenders' remaining
hope depended on bringing the scattered Legacy together.

## XI. The Legacy Weapon

The Legacy was not merely a stockpile of powerful
artifacts. It was the outcome of Urza's centuries-long
design, carried forward by people whose choices he
could not entirely control: Gerrard, <CharacterLink id="karn">Karn</CharacterLink>,
the Weatherlight's surviving crew, and others who had
endured the war.

The Mightstone and Weakstone formed an ancient link
back to <CharacterLink id="glacian">Glacian</CharacterLink>
and the collapse of the Thran. Their history had passed
through the Brothers' War and the planeswalker Urza,
and finally converged with Karn and the Weatherlight.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[4]} significance={frontmatter.cards[4].significance}>

The _Legacy Weapon_ card represents the culmination
of those scattered components. The design spanned
thousands of years of Dominarian history, connecting
the Thran, the Brothers' War, and the Weatherlight's
journey into a single final act.

The weapon's activation destroyed Yawgmoth's
presence and ended the immediate Phyrexian invasion.

</HistoricalMoment>

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[16]} significance={frontmatter.cards[16].significance}>

_Vindicate_ offers a different view of the finale:
not the assembled artifact, but the human sacrifice
associated with the release of its power. Its
Gerrard quotation anchors the card in the story's
conclusion, rather than portraying an unrelated
victory over Phyrexian forces.

Urza and Gerrard did not survive the weapon's final
activation. Karn, however, emerged transformed as
a planeswalker, carrying the Legacy's consequences
into a new era of the Multiverse.

</HistoricalMoment>

<HistoricalEventReference id="legacy-weapon" />


### What the final victory could not restore

The Legacy Weapon's success gives the saga a decisive endpoint, but it is a mistake to treat the defeat of Yawgmoth as the repair of all the damage he caused. The invasion left deaths, altered landscapes, broken institutions, and relationships that had been sacrificed to plans larger than their participants. Even some of the beings who helped make victory possible did not survive in the form readers first encountered. The cost was not an incidental dark note added to an otherwise complete triumph; it shaped the world into which the next stories would begin.

Karn's place in the aftermath is particularly important because he embodied both Urza's designs and the capacity for a different kind of choice. The artificial creature once placed at the center of a grand program would have a history that continued beyond that program's originator. Gerrard's journey similarly mattered for more than his usefulness as a final component. He had repeatedly struggled with the meaning of duty when the person defining that duty did not bear its immediate human cost.

The Weatherlight story was published through multiple sets and novels, including episodes whose order is easier to confuse when encountered as individual cards. The printed artifacts help establish continuity, but the narrative depends on changes in trust, sacrifice, and allegiance. Those changes are the reason the final weapon means something more than an unusually powerful object.

## XII. Aftermath and a New Beginning

Dominaria survived, but its victory could not
restore the world to its prewar condition. The
Coalition's losses extended beyond armies and
cities to the people, knowledge, and relationships
that had sustained the Weatherlight's voyage.

The sacrifice of Gerrard and Urza closed the main
conflict with Yawgmoth. Survivors such as
<CharacterLink id="sisay">Sisay</CharacterLink> had to
build lives after the end of the mission that had
once defined them.

Karn's transformation also prevented the ending
from being final in every sense. He would create
another artificial world, and the Phyrexian taint
that survived would eventually endanger it. That
later history belongs to a different phase of
the Multiverse's story.

The conclusion of the original Weatherlight Saga
therefore accomplished two things at once: it
resolved a conflict reaching back to the Thran,
and left a wounded world—and a surviving
Legacy—to shape the histories that followed.

### Squee and the Unlikely Survivor

Not every member of the Weatherlight's story achieved renown through
command or magical scholarship. <CharacterLink id="squee">Squee</CharacterLink>
was a goblin whose loyalty survived ridicule, captivity, and the
cruelties of war. During the Phyrexian conflict he became subject to
Yawgmoth's magic, which rebuilt his body when he died. The eventual
consequences of that disturbing form of immortality would extend far
beyond the saga's conclusion.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[21]} significance={frontmatter.cards[21].significance}>

**Squee, Goblin Nabob**, originally printed in _Mercadian Masques_,
shows this companion during the voyage, not the subsequent centuries
of his immortal existence. The later official story *Death and
Salvation* clarifies the role of Yawgmoth's spell in his survival.

</HistoricalMoment>

The original Weatherlight-era curse should not be mistaken for Squee's
permanent fate. In the 2022 story *Death and Salvation*, the returned
Ertai used Phyrexian corruption against Squee; his soul was separated
from the cycle of bodily resurrection imposed by Yawgmoth. Salvation,
the being associated with his old talisman, then offered him a choice.
Rather than resume endless resurrection or remain dead, Squee asked
to return to life **one last time**. This later decision belongs to
the renewed Dominarian Phyrexian crisis, not the original invasion.

<HistoricalEventReference id="weatherlight-squee-immortality" />

The contrast is instructive. Some heroes died to stop Yawgmoth;
others lived on with consequences they had never requested.
Neither outcome was part of the uncomplicated triumph that Urza
had once imagined.

## XIII. How Magic Told This Story

The Weatherlight Saga
marked a major shift
in how Magic told
its fictional history.

Earlier expansions
often introduced
lore through individual
cards and short
pieces of flavor text.

The Weatherlight storyline
introduced an ongoing
cast whose adventures
could continue across
multiple sets.

_Weatherlight_ (1997)
helped launch this
continuous narrative.

_Tempest_, _Stronghold_,
and _Exodus_ developed
the crew's adventures
on Rath.

The _Urza's Saga_ block,
released from 1998
to 1999, looked backward
to explore preparations
that preceded the main
Weatherlight story.

_Mercadian Masques_
(1999) moved the crew
to another plane.

Finally, _Invasion_
(2000), _Planeshift_
(2001), and _Apocalypse_
(2001) portrayed the
Phyrexian invasion
and its conclusion.

Novels and other fiction
expanded these events
beyond what could be
shown on cards alone.

Later releases also
returned to the saga's
characters.

For example, _Modern
Horizons_ (2019)
portrayed Sisay as
a legendary creature.

The result is a history
told through different
media, across different
years, and sometimes
in an order very
different from the
fictional sequence.

Two selected cards help illustrate the personal
cost of the voyage: _Hanna, Ship's Navigator_,
first printed in _Invasion_ (2000), and _Unmask_,
printed in _Mercadian Masques_ (1999). The latter
actually depicts Volrath shedding his Takara disguise,
connecting an important plot revelation to a card's art.

A later expansion, _Planar Chaos_ (2007), portrayed a
counterfactual path in which Mirri, not Crovax, killed
Selenia and inherited the vampiric curse. These cards
illustrate an alternate history, not the primary
Weatherlight chronology described in this chapter.

The Weatherlight Saga
is therefore both
a defining period
in Dominaria's history
and an important
turning point in
Magic's history
as a storytelling game.
