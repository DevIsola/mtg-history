## I. Before the Brothers

Thousands of years before the rivalry between Urza and
Mishra, Dominaria was home to one of the most technologically
advanced civilizations in its history: the Thran Empire.

The Thran built extraordinary machines and structures,
powered by magical crystals known as powerstones.

At the heart of their civilization stood Halcyon, a city
whose technological achievements reflected the wealth and
influence of the empire.

But the same technology that supported Thran society
would eventually contribute to its destruction.

## II. The Civilization of Powerstones

Much of the Thran Empire's technological supremacy rested
on the achievements of Glacian, an exceptionally gifted
artificer.

His development of powerstones allowed the Thran to store
and harness enormous quantities of energy.

Powerstones became fundamental to the empire's machinery,
architecture, and daily life.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[0]}>

Glacian's inventions transformed the Thran world.

Yet his achievements contained an unexpected danger.
Exposure to the powerstones was associated with a
devastating illness known as phthisis.

The civilization had become dependent on a technology
that could also threaten the lives of its people.

</HistoricalMoment>

Glacian's wife, Rebbec, was among the most celebrated
architects of the empire.

Her works included extraordinary structures that
combined engineering and artifice, culminating in the
magnificent Thran Temple above Halcyon.

Together, Glacian and Rebbec represented the remarkable
accomplishments of the Thran civilization.

Their lives would also become inseparable from its fall.

## III. The Arrival of Yawgmoth

As sickness spread through Thran society, the empire
faced a crisis that its remarkable technology could
not easily resolve.

<CharacterLink id="rebbec">Rebbec</CharacterLink> sought treatment for her
ailing husband and sought treatment for her ailing husband and used her
influence to bring a physician named Yawgmoth to Halcyon.

Yawgmoth was brilliant and ambitious.

His medical knowledge made him valuable to a society
desperate for a cure. But his methods and personal
ambitions would prove increasingly dangerous.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[1]}>

Yawgmoth gained influence through his apparent ability
to confront the suffering that afflicted the Thran.

His pursuit of medical progress became entangled with
political power, coercion, and experimentation.

The physician who presented himself as a potential
savior would eventually become the greatest threat
to the civilization that welcomed him.

</HistoricalMoment>

As his authority increased, Yawgmoth began to see
living beings as subjects that could be transformed
according to his own vision of perfection.

## IV. The Discovery of Phyrexia

A planeswalker named Dyfed introduced Yawgmoth to
an artificial plane known as Phyrexia.

The plane had been created by an earlier, unnamed
planeswalker and was no longer under its creator's
control.

Phyrexia contained a strange environment in which
artifice and living systems were deeply intertwined.

To Yawgmoth, it offered extraordinary possibilities.

He believed its properties could be used to confront
the disease afflicting the Thran, but the plane also
offered a place to conduct experiments far beyond
the restraints of his former society.

A portal established a connection between Dominaria
and Phyrexia.

Over time, Yawgmoth became the artificial plane's
new master.

Under his direction, it developed into the center
of a doctrine that sought to remake living beings
through a union of flesh, machinery, and magic.

## V. The Fall of the Thran Empire

The Thran Empire was already weakened by disease,
political division, and competing visions of its
future.

Yawgmoth's growing power made those divisions
increasingly destructive.

His experiments and use of Phyrexian forces
turned a crisis of medicine and government into
a catastrophe for the entire civilization.

Resistance to Yawgmoth escalated into war.

The empire's technological achievements, once
symbols of progress, became instruments of ruin.

Halcyon and the society surrounding it were
devastated.

What had once appeared to be the height of
Dominarian civilization came to an end.

## VI. Rebbec's Final Decision

As the conflict reached its conclusion, Rebbec
came to understand the danger represented by
Yawgmoth and his activities on Phyrexia.

The gateway between Dominaria and the artificial
plane presented an existential threat to the
surviving world.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[2]}>

Rebbec sealed the gateway to Phyrexia, sacrificing
herself in the process.

Her decision prevented Yawgmoth and his forces
from immediately returning to Dominaria through
that passage.

The act came too late to save the Thran Empire,
but it kept the growing Phyrexian threat
separated from Dominaria for generations.

</HistoricalMoment>

The collapse of the Thran did not bring an end
to Yawgmoth's ambitions.

Instead, it marked the beginning of Phyrexia's
long history as a threat to Dominaria.

<HistoricalEventReference id="rebbec-seals-phyrexia" />
## VII. The Legacy of Glacian

The Thran civilization disappeared, but some of
its artifacts endured long after its cities
had fallen into ruin.

Among the most important surviving objects were
two halves of a powerstone associated with Glacian.

These stones had been used in sealing the gateway
to Phyrexia.

Millennia later, the young brothers Urza and Mishra
would discover them during their investigations
of the ancient Thran ruins.

They would name the two halves the Mightstone
and the Weakstone.

Their discovery would become one of the causes
of a devastating new conflict.

Thus, the history of the Thran connects directly
to the beginning of the Brothers' War.

## VIII. How Magic Told This Story

Although the Thran existed in Magic's distant
fictional past, their history was revealed to
readers gradually through later publications.

The novel _The Thran_, written by J. Robert King
and published in 1999, explored the empire's
decline and Yawgmoth's rise in detail.

Later card releases revisited major figures
from this period.

_Modern Horizons_ (2019) introduced
**Yawgmoth, Thran Physician**, depicting the
character before his later transformation.

_Commander Legends_ (2020) introduced cards
representing Glacian and Rebbec, providing
illustrations of two central figures from
this ancient civilization.

Together, these publications illustrate how
Magic's historical storytelling extends far
beyond the chronological order of its expansions.

The Thran may have vanished thousands of years
before the Brothers' War, but the consequences
of their civilization survived through its
technology, its artifacts, and the rise of Phyrexia.
