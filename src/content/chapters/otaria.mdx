## I. A World After the Invasion

Yawgmoth's defeat ended the great Phyrexian invasion, but it did not erase its consequences. Dominaria was a world of broken institutions, scarred landscapes, and communities trying to build new lives after generations of conflict.

Across the sea from the most familiar theaters of the Weatherlight Saga lay Otaria, a continent whose own politics and ambitions would now occupy the center of Magic's story.

The struggle to come was different from Urza's war. No single invading empire united its enemies. Instead, rival groups competed for a source of power that promised to satisfy whatever its possessor most desired.

That source was the Mirari.

## II. The Mirari: A Mirror of Desire

The Mirari was a strange metallic artifact whose effects extended beyond ordinary spellcasting. It amplified magic and gave physical expression to the dreams, impulses, and ambitions of those who approached it.

The danger was inseparable from its appeal. Those who imagined they could use the Mirari to gain control over their lives often discovered that it magnified the very desires capable of destroying them.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[0]}>

The Mirari's power made it a prize, but it offered no wisdom about how that power should be used. Its influence became a recurring pattern in Otaria's history: discovery, obsession, extraordinary magic, and ruin.

The artifact would eventually be revealed as connected to <CharacterLink id="karn">Karn</CharacterLink>, whose own life had been shaped by the Legacy and the defeat of Phyrexia.

</HistoricalMoment>

Before that connection became clear, the Mirari passed among people who understood little of its origin and less of its cost.

## III. The Rival Powers of Otaria

Otaria was not a single kingdom with a unified purpose. The Cabal operated a powerful network of gladiatorial games, commerce, and dark magic, drawing wealth and influence from its arenas.

The Cabal's patriarch, sometimes called the First, exercised authority through minions and political calculation. Among the Cabal's most feared practitioners were dementia summoners, mages capable of drawing nightmare creatures from their own minds into reality.

Elsewhere, the Order sought to impose its vision of stability and righteousness. Merfolk rulers and their agents also pursued ambitions of their own. The scheming ambassador Laquatus saw the Mirari as an opportunity to increase his influence.

Beyond these competing powers stood the Pardic Mountains and the living depths of the Krosan Forest.

The Mirari brought these worlds into collision.

## IV. Kamahl and the Arena

<CharacterLink id="kamahl">Kamahl</CharacterLink> came from the Pardic Mountains, where combat prowess and personal honor shaped his identity. As a powerful barbarian and pit fighter, he traveled to Cabal City in pursuit of challenge and recognition.

The arenas promised fame to those who survived them, while the Cabal benefited from the spectacle and the struggles of those who entered.

Kamahl's encounter with the Mirari changed the meaning of his journey. A warrior who had trusted his own strength now faced a power that could magnify that strength far beyond his judgment.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[1]}>

The first card depicting Kamahl presents a formidable red-aligned warrior, consumed by the immediacy of combat.

Later cards would portray him very differently. The contrast between those versions is part of the historical record: Magic used the same character to show how experience, remorse, and responsibility could transform a life.

</HistoricalMoment>

Kamahl was not the only figure drawn toward the artifact. His path would soon become entangled with a young Cabal mage.

## V. Chainer and the Price of Nightmares

<CharacterLink id="chainer">Chainer</CharacterLink> was a dementia summoner, trained to draw terrifying creatures out of the recesses of his mind. His connection to the Cabal gave him access to power, but also placed him within an institution built on manipulation.

Chainer and Kamahl developed an unlikely friendship. Their differences mattered less than the understanding they came to share as outsiders to the ambitions of the Cabal's leadership.

But the Mirari's influence reached Chainer as well. His capacity to summon nightmares grew toward a scale that he could no longer safely command.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[2]}>

Chainer's story demonstrates a particular danger of the Mirari: increasing a mage's power did not increase that mage's ability to control its consequences.

The nightmare forces he unleashed threatened the society he had once served, and his confrontation with Kamahl ended in tragedy.

</HistoricalMoment>

Chainer's downfall left Kamahl with an even more painful understanding of what the Mirari could do. Nevertheless, possession of the artifact did not free him from its temptation.

## VI. The Wound That Changed Jeska

Kamahl returned toward the Pardic Mountains with the Mirari incorporated into his sword. Instead of granting mastery over his violent impulses, the artifact intensified them.

His desire for leadership and triumph became increasingly difficult to distinguish from the destructive influence of the weapon.

During a confrontation among the Pardic people, Kamahl lost control and struck his sister, <CharacterLink id="jeska">Jeska</CharacterLink>, with the Mirari sword.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[3]}>

Jeska had been a capable warrior in her own right, not merely a participant in her brother's story.

The wound inflicted by Kamahl refused to heal normally. Its supernatural corruption would change her identity, and eventually the history of Otaria itself.

</HistoricalMoment>

For Kamahl, the injury became the decisive failure of his old life. The champion who had pursued power and glory now had to confront the harm he had caused to someone he loved.

## VII. The Krosan Forest and a Different Kind of Strength

Searching for another way to live, Kamahl entered the Krosan Forest. There he encountered traditions built around growth, patience, and the relationship between living beings and the land.

The druidic guidance associated with Thriss, the Nantuko leader, helped Kamahl turn away from the impulses that had defined his earlier life.

His transformation did not erase the past. Jeska remained injured, and the Mirari continued to threaten those who sought it.

Eventually, Kamahl drove the Mirari sword into the earth of Krosa. His attempt to leave its corrupting influence behind released tremendous magical energy into the forest.

The event associated with *Mirari's Wake* transformed the landscape and helped establish the conditions of the following conflict.

The former pit fighter had become a defender of the natural world, but the consequences of his actions were still spreading.

## VIII. The Return of Jeska as Phage

Jeska's suffering did not end when Kamahl abandoned the Mirari. The Cabal's intervention transformed her into Phage the Untouchable, a being whose contact could bring death.

This rebirth changed far more than her physical condition. Phage became bound to the Cabal's arena culture and to conflicts that reflected the cruelty of its rulers.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[5]}>

Phage's original *Legions* card presents a devastating power that comes with equally dangerous limitations.

As a historical reference, the card captures how far Jeska had been changed from the warrior introduced during the Odyssey story.

</HistoricalMoment>

Kamahl continued to seek a way to save his sister. Yet Phage's presence had already drawn the attention of another mage, one whose grief would create a new and terrible enemy.

## IX. Ixidor, Nivea, and the Creation of Akroma

Ixidor was a gifted illusionist whose life collapsed after his lover Nivea was killed during a deadly arena encounter with Phage.

Driven by grief and vengeance, he discovered the capacity to reshape reality through his imagination. His power went beyond ordinary illusion: dreams could take on lasting form.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[4]}>

Ixidor's card presents a mage whose relationship with reality has fundamentally changed.

Rather than accept Nivea's death, he fashioned a powerful angel, Akroma, and directed his creation against Phage.

</HistoricalMoment>

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[6]}>

Akroma was created as an instrument of vengeance, but her existence soon became the center of a conflict far larger than a private feud.

Her followers and Phage's supporters became opposing forces whose devotion intensified the devastation of Otaria.

</HistoricalMoment>

The battle placed Kamahl in an almost impossible position: one side threatened the land he sought to protect, while the other was the transformed sister he still hoped to save.

## X. The Birth of Karona

The confrontation between Akroma and Phage drew additional people into its final violence, including the woman Zagorka.

When Kamahl struck Akroma, Phage, and Zagorka with the Soul Reaper axe, the three were joined into a single new being: Karona, the False God.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[7]}>

Karona's power was linked to belief and devotion. Her existence encouraged followers to treat her as a divine answer to their competing desires.

But the apparent fulfillment of so many hopes brought neither peace nor stability. The continent's conflicts had produced a figure whose power threatened to overwhelm the world around her.

</HistoricalMoment>

Karona's emergence was the culmination of the Otarian pattern: the pursuit of salvation through overwhelming power produced an even greater danger.

## XI. The Third Life of Jeska

Karona did not endure indefinitely. Two of her acolytes, Sash and Waistcoat, betrayed her and used the Mirari sword against her.

The merged being separated. Akroma and Zagorka perished, but Jeska survived a final extraordinary transformation: her planeswalker spark ignited.

She was no longer simply the warrior Kamahl had wounded or the deadly Phage the Cabal had created.

Now a planeswalker, Jeska would have a future beyond Otaria and beyond Dominaria itself.

The cycle of injury, corruption, conflict, and rebirth that had defined her life reached a new turning point.

## XII. Karn and the Artifact's Legacy

The Mirari's importance did not end with Karona. Karn returned and took responsibility for the artifact, whose existence was connected to his own attempts to understand and observe the world.

He would ultimately transform the Mirari into the sentient being Memnarch, who became the guardian of Karn's artificial plane, Argentum.

That decision would have far-reaching consequences. Memnarch's later corruption and the transformation of Argentum into Mirrodin established a bridge from the Otarian story to another major crisis in the Multiverse.

Jeska, meanwhile, became associated with Karn as a planeswalker and would reappear in the history surrounding Dominaria's temporal rifts.

The Mirari Saga therefore ended in Otaria without closing the histories of its surviving participants.

## XIII. How Magic Published the Otarian Saga

The fictional narrative of Otaria is not the same thing as its publication history.

*Odyssey* (2001) introduced Kamahl, the Mirari, and the new regional focus on Dominaria after the Weatherlight Saga.

*Torment* (2002) expanded the Cabal's influence and Chainer's nightmare magic. *Judgment* (2002) followed with Kamahl's transformation and the consequences of the Mirari's corruption.

The story continued immediately in the next expansion block. *Onslaught* (2002) introduced Kamahl's changed identity and the conflict involving Phage and Ixidor.

*Legions* (2003) portrayed Phage and Akroma as opposing forces, while *Scourge* (2003) brought the story of Karona to its climax.

Six associated novels developed the events beyond the limitations of individual cards: *Odyssey*, *Chainer's Torment*, *Judgment*, *Onslaught*, *Legions*, and *Scourge*.

Much later, *Commander Legends* (2020) revisited Kamahl and Jeska in cards and character biographies that made their transformations easier to trace.

This is why the archive follows two timelines. The events belong to the history of Dominaria, but their meaning has continued to develop through later publications and interpretations.

A single artifact's story had become the history of a continent, a family, and several beings whose destinies would shape the Multiverse long afterward.
