## I. A Continent After the Invasion

The destruction of <CharacterLink id="yawgmoth">Yawgmoth</CharacterLink> ended the Phyrexian invasion, but Dominaria did not return to the world it had been. Power had shifted, survivors carried the cost of the war, and distant communities still had their own rivalries. Roughly a century after the Legacy's culmination, Magic's central narrative moved to Otaria, a continent with its own cities, forests, mountain tribes, and underwater empire.

This history did not begin with a common enemy threatening the whole plane. It began with a small metallic sphere that seemed to offer extraordinary power to any person who desired it strongly enough. The Mirari made Otaria's rival powers more dangerous to one another, and often to themselves.

The story crosses two linked expansion blocks. _Odyssey_, _Torment_, and _Judgment_ follow the discovery of the Mirari, its successive owners, and the tragedy of <CharacterLink id="kamahl">Kamahl</CharacterLink> and <CharacterLink id="chainer">Chainer</CharacterLink>. _Onslaught_, _Legions_, and _Scourge_ follow the consequences, above all the changing identity of <CharacterLink id="jeska">Jeska</CharacterLink> and the birth of Karona.

## II. The Mirari's Discovery and Hidden Origin

The <CharacterLink id="karn">Karn</CharacterLink> of the Weatherlight Saga had become a planeswalker after the Legacy was activated. Unknown to Otaria's inhabitants, he sent a magical probe to observe Dominaria. This object was the Mirari. That origin is revealed only later; the first people who encountered it did not know they were handling an instrument of exploration.

It was <CharacterLink id="chainer">Chainer</CharacterLink>, a young Cabal dementia summoner, who found the Mirari in an abandoned mansion. Rather than keep the sphere, he presented it to the Cabal's leader, <CharacterLink id="cabal-patriarch">the First</CharacterLink>. This decision reflects Chainer's early loyalty to an institution he believed could give his abilities meaning and direction.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[0]} significance={frontmatter.cards[0].significance}>

The original _Mirari_ from _Odyssey_ is the artifact around which the six-set saga turns. Its rules text lets a player copy spells, but its fictional influence is much wider: it magnifies desires and makes dangerous wishes appear attainable. The card is evidence for the object, not a diagram of its later creation as Memnarch.

</HistoricalMoment>

The First understood its value as leverage. He placed it among the Cabal's treasures and made it a prize in the coming games, ensuring that ambitious champions and powerful factions would converge on Cabal City. The pattern of the Mirari's history had begun: possession promised mastery, but owners repeatedly discovered that intensified desire was not the same as control.

<HistoricalEventReference id="mirari-discovery" />


### Why the Mirari could destabilize a whole continent

The Mirari was dangerous not because it offered every person the same temptation, but because it amplified desires that were already different. A ruler might see an instrument of command, a pit fighter a path to victory, a cabalist an advantage over rivals, and a desperate outsider a chance to reverse an old injury. Such wishes were never purely private. Once a rumor spread that the artifact could fulfill them, institutions had an incentive to find, contain, display, or trade the object. A contest over possession became a contest over the authority to decide whose desires mattered.

Otaria's political fragmentation helped that contest expand. The Order and the Cabal offered rival visions of discipline and power, while independent warriors and local communities could be drawn into their campaigns without endorsing either ideology. The Mirari did not invent these tensions. It intensified them by making extraordinary force seem available through a single acquisition. The possession of the object could substitute for the slower work of building allegiance and legitimate authority.

The Cabal's arenas gave this process a public stage. Spectators witnessed spectacle and understood victory as evidence of strength; those who profited from the spectacle helped decide which fighters were elevated and which risks were acceptable. The resulting system transformed violence into status, revenue, and political influence. Treating it as colorful entertainment alone misses the institutions that made brutality appear normal.

## III. The Cabal: Spectacle, Commerce, and Control

The Cabal was more than an association of black mages. Its arenas were institutions of entertainment and coercion, with combatants, gamblers, patrons, and officials all contributing to its influence. Cabal City could turn suffering into a public spectacle while presenting success in the pits as an opportunity for wealth and fame. A prized artifact was therefore as much a political instrument as a magical one.

<CharacterLink id="cabal-patriarch">The Cabal Patriarch</CharacterLink>, known
as the First, presided over this system. His apparent indulgence toward
ambitious subordinates concealed an ability to manipulate their goals.
<CharacterLink id="braids">Braids</CharacterLink> served as one of the Cabal's
most unpredictable operatives. Chainer's emerging talent was valuable, but his
superiors valued that talent within the Cabal's hierarchy rather than as an
independent source of authority.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[8]} significance={frontmatter.cards[8].significance}>

_Cabal Patriarch_ represents the ruler who made the Mirari a political prize. This printing preserves the early portrait by Mark Zug. The card's sacrifice effects are evocative of the Cabal's methods, but should not be confused with a literal account of any single arena contest.

</HistoricalMoment>

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[9]} significance={frontmatter.cards[9].significance}>

The original _Braids, Cabal Minion_ gives a face to the Cabal's willingness to treat other lives and resources as expendable. Braids also participated in the Mirari's return to Cabal possession after the artifact's journey beyond the city.

</HistoricalMoment>

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[10]} significance={frontmatter.cards[10].significance}>

The _Torment_ printing of _Cabal Coffers_ supplies particularly direct historical evidence. Its flavor text places the Mirari inside the Cabal's vault and depicts its power spreading across Otaria. Unlike a retrospective thematic card, the original printing situates the sphere inside this phase of the story.

</HistoricalMoment>

The Cabal's power was formidable, but it was neither uncontested nor absolute. The Order opposed it in the name of righteousness, the merfolk empire pursued its own interests, and opportunists such as <CharacterLink id="laquatus">Ambassador Laquatus</CharacterLink> exploited the friction between them.

## IV. A Prize That Moved Between Rival Powers

The Mirari did not remain safely in the Cabal's possession. After an attack on Cabal City, the artifact passed to Lieutenant Kirtar of the Order. Convinced that he could turn its power toward his cause, Kirtar became another example of a seemingly principled person overtaken by what the sphere offered. Its influence produced disaster for the Order rather than salvation.

### The Order's lieutenant and the limits of certainty

The Order was not simply a collection of soldiers opposed to the Cabal's
arenas. Its leaders claimed a moral mandate to contain what they saw as
corrupting magic. <CharacterLink id="lieutenant-kirtar">Lieutenant Kirtar</CharacterLink>
believed possession of the Mirari could advance that cause, even though
its passage out of Cabal hands had not made it less dangerous.

The distinction between motive and consequence is central to the Mirari's
history. Kirtar did not seek the same kind of glory as Kamahl or the power
of a throne like Aboshan. Yet the sphere made his confidence in his own
judgment no safer than their ambitions. The Order's claim to righteousness
did not protect its members from a catastrophe of their own making.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[18]} significance={frontmatter.cards[18].significance}>

_Lieutenant Kirtar_ is the contemporary _Odyssey_ portrait of a named
figure in the conflict. It identifies the Order's commander but does not
literally depict his use of the Mirari or the outcome of that attempt.

</HistoricalMoment>

<HistoricalEventReference id="otaria-kirtar-and-the-order" />

The crisis extended into the underwater empire. <CharacterLink id="aboshan">Aboshan</CharacterLink>, the cephalid emperor, came into possession of the Mirari. His dream of mastery over the sea became a catastrophe when he tried to realize it. The resulting inundation devastated parts of Otaria and the imperial capital. The disaster made clear that the object was not merely dangerous in the hands of an obvious tyrant: it could turn any grand ambition into devastation.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[11]} significance={frontmatter.cards[11].significance}>

_Aboshan, Cephalid Emperor_ portrays one of the Mirari's destructive possessors, whose royal status could not give him control over its power. The original _Odyssey_ card establishes his role in the underwater polity; the flood belongs to the larger narrative told in the novel, not necessarily to an action depicted in the illustration.

</HistoricalMoment>

<CharacterLink id="laquatus">Laquatus</CharacterLink>, a merfolk ambassador with
political ambitions of his own, maneuvered around the emperor, the Cabal, and
the other contenders. His skill was not direct military power but the ability to
make others act in ways that served him. After the disaster at sea, the Mirari
found its way back toward Cabal City, where its presence would provoke an even
more intimate tragedy.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[17]} significance={frontmatter.cards[17].significance}>

_Laquatus's Creativity_ is a named contemporary reference to the ambassador. It is useful as evidence of his place in the _Odyssey_ setting, not as a literal illustration of his manipulation of Aboshan.

</HistoricalMoment>

## V. Kamahl and Chainer: Friendship Across the Pits

<CharacterLink id="kamahl">Kamahl</CharacterLink> arrived from the Pardic
Mountains seeking great opponents and a reputation worthy of his martial skill.
He was a formidable fire-wielding barbarian, accustomed to valuing honor and
strength in open combat. Cabal City's pits offered both challenges and an
audience, but they also exposed him to a society where a fight's outcome could
be determined by its owners.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[1]} significance={frontmatter.cards[1].significance}>

The red-aligned _Kamahl, Pit Fighter_ captures his original public identity: direct, imposing, and drawn to battle. Set beside the later green Kamahl card, it also records a rare long-term character transformation that Magic depicted through contrasting colors and abilities.

</HistoricalMoment>

Chainer came from the opposite world: a Cabal mage trained to reach into his own mind's darkness and call nightmare creatures into reality. Under his mentor Skellum, he developed powers that combined mental discipline with terrible imagination. The Cabal encouraged such mastery because a dementia caster could produce combatants and weapons that appeared from nowhere.

### Skellum and the discipline of nightmare magic

Skellum's teaching is important because dementia summoning was not just
an uncontrolled outpouring of terror. It required practitioners to form,
recognize, and control imagined horrors. Chainer's training offered
technique and a measure of personal confidence, while the Cabal's
hierarchy judged the results by their usefulness in its pits.

That tension foreshadowed the Mirari disaster. The skills that made
Chainer exceptional encouraged him to believe that still greater
summons could be mastered. Under the Mirari's influence, however, the
boundary between the summoner's imagination and the world around him
became perilously unstable.

<HistoricalEventReference id="otaria-skellums-dementia-training" />

The two men found an unlikely common ground. Kamahl respected competence and courage; Chainer was drawn to a fighter whose identity was not entirely defined by Cabal patronage. They fought together in the pits and became friends despite fundamentally different loyalties. Their connection gave each man a glimpse of a life outside the assumptions of his upbringing.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[12]} significance={frontmatter.cards[12].significance}>

The original _Chainer's Edict_ does not depict the friends' final battle. It captures the brutal moral climate of the Cabal arenas: even an act described as mercy can be indistinguishable from an imposed death sentence.

</HistoricalMoment>

The friendship matters because Chainer's eventual fall was not a simple confrontation between a hero and a villain. Kamahl was forced to oppose someone he knew, admired, and had fought beside.

### Balthor: another model of martial leadership

Kamahl's world also included <CharacterLink id="balthor">Balthor</CharacterLink>,
a dwarven warrior who led barbarians and served as a mentor to him.
Where the Cabal turned fighters into entertainment, Balthor's authority
came from experience, solidarity, and instruction. Neither way of life
made battle harmless, but the contrast helps explain why Kamahl's later
choice to protect Krosa was a change of purpose rather than simply a
change of magical color.

Balthor's own story did not end with his life. The expansions deliberately
portrayed him twice: first as a living barbarian leader in _Torment_,
then as an undead figure in _Judgment_. That second portrayal records
a transformation, not a second character with a similar name.


### Chainer and Kamahl chose different forms of belonging

The friendship between Chainer and Kamahl complicates a simple opposition between virtuous outsider and corrupt cabalist. Chainer was formed by the Cabal's world, but he was also capable of genuine affection and loyalty. Kamahl prized strength and sought a place where he could test himself, yet his identity was not identical to the audience's appetite for violent spectacle. Their closeness made the eventual divergence more painful because it exposed the difference between caring for a person and accepting the institution that defines that person's opportunities.

Chainer's pursuit of power emerged within a culture that rewarded extraordinary manipulation of fear and nightmares. As his abilities expanded, the boundary between protecting friends and controlling the world around him became harder to maintain. The resulting catastrophe should not be interpreted as a punishment for one isolated mistake. It grew from repeated compromises in a setting where the price of advancement was measured in domination.

Kamahl's later transformation does not erase their friendship. It gives the story a way to ask whether a person can escape the values of a violent environment without denying that some relationships formed there were real. Chainer's fate and Kamahl's choices together make the Mirari cycle more than the travels of an unstable magical prize.

## VI. Chainer's Rise and the Nightmare Catastrophe

As the Mirari returned to the Cabal's orbit, Chainer's power and standing increased. His aspirations grew beyond becoming a skilled summoner. He came to imagine that he could impose order on a Cabal whose politics he had learned to distrust. The First sought to control that ambition, while Laquatus saw an opportunity to destabilize a rival power.

Chainer seized the Mirari and displaced the Patriarch from Cabal City. Yet the artifact did not make him a wiser ruler. With its power, he expanded the reach of dementia magic and drew horrific beings from the minds of other summoners. The distinction between creating a nightmare and containing it collapsed. Cabal City's own source of spectacular violence became a threat to everyone inside it.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[2]} significance={frontmatter.cards[2].significance}>

_Chainer, Dementia Master_ was introduced in _Torment_, whose unusually strong emphasis on black magic reinforces the story's shift toward the Cabal. Chainer's printed abilities evoke temporary control over creatures and the ultimate cost of that control; the narrative makes the human consequences explicit.

</HistoricalMoment>

Kamahl confronted him not merely to win the Mirari but to stop a friend from destroying himself and the city. Chainer could no longer reliably contain what he had summoned. His final attempt to gather the nightmares back into himself left him horribly transformed, and Kamahl killed him at his apparent request. The friendship ended in an act of tragic necessity, not a triumphant prizefight.

Chainer had first discovered the Mirari and delivered it to the Cabal. By the end, its power had undermined the institution he served, erased his independence, and forced his closest friend to end his suffering. Kamahl inherited the artifact and the danger that had destroyed Chainer.

<HistoricalEventReference id="chainer-downfall" />

### Balthor's death and return

Balthor's transformation offers a smaller, deeply personal parallel to
Chainer's catastrophe. Warfare and necromancy did not merely remove
fighters from the living world; the dead could be returned in altered
forms, carrying traces of their earlier identities into a new service.
The surviving story presents Balthor as a mentor and leader whose fate
illustrates the cost of the same violent world that shaped Kamahl.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[19]} significance={frontmatter.cards[19].significance}>

_Balthor the Defiled_ depicts the dead dwarf from _Judgment_. Read beside
_Balthor the Stout_ (_Torment_), it illustrates one character before and
after death. Wizards' later design retrospective confirms that these
two cards were conceived as a linked pair.

</HistoricalMoment>

## VII. The Mirari Sword and Jeska's Wound

Returning toward the Pardic Mountains, Kamahl incorporated the Mirari into his sword. The relic magnified the pride and aggression that had once brought him success as a pit fighter. Convinced of his right to lead, he tried to draw the Pardic tribes under his authority. His power increased even as his judgment narrowed.

His sister <CharacterLink id="jeska">Jeska</CharacterLink> was not a passive witness. She was an able warrior with her own will and her own sense of what was happening to Kamahl. When she confronted him, he struck her with the Mirari sword, nearly killing her. The wound was not an ordinary injury: its magical corruption endured, beginning the long process that would transform her into Phage.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[3]} significance={frontmatter.cards[3].significance}>

The _Judgment_ card _Jeska, Warrior Adept_ preserves her identity as a Pardic warrior before her later transformations. It is important to distinguish this Jeska from Phage and Karona: they are successive states of one life, not three unrelated characters.

</HistoricalMoment>

The injury shattered Kamahl's belief that strength could solve every problem. He left behind the status he had pursued and sought another way of understanding power. His remorse did not magically heal Jeska, however. The consequences of the strike would continue while he changed.

<HistoricalEventReference id="jeska-wounded" />


### Jeska's wound binds private tragedy to public history

When Jeska is injured and later transformed, the story's scale changes. The contest for the Mirari had seemed capable of turning every relationship into a struggle for possession, but its consequences now reshaped a particular family. Kamahl could not treat her condition as just another opponent to defeat. The sword associated with his own growing power also implicated him in an injury he could not solve through the methods that had previously made him successful.

Jeska's later identities must be followed carefully. Phage and Karona are not interchangeable names for the same unchanging political actor. They represent transformations produced by different events and forces, each with consequences for the people who encountered her. The danger of reducing the sequence to a list of spectacular forms is that Jeska's own history disappears behind the names assigned to her.

Krosa offered Kamahl another vocabulary for strength: restraint, belonging to a living environment, and the ability to allow another being a future independent of one's wishes. That change does not make earlier harms disappear, but it supplies a meaningful contrast to the Cabal's arenas and the Mirari's seductive promise that more power is always the answer.

## VIII. Krosa: A Different Kind of Power

Kamahl went into the Krosan Forest, a living region where the ambitions of cities and armies seemed less important than the relationships among its creatures, vegetation, and guardians. <CharacterLink id="seton">Seton</CharacterLink>, a centaur defender of Krosa, represented a way of life radically different from the Cabal's spectacles. Kamahl also learned from Thriss, the Nantuko elder whose teachings helped him redirect his strength toward living things.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[13]} significance={frontmatter.cards[13].significance}>

_Seton, Krosan Protector_ was printed in _Odyssey_, before Kamahl fully adopted the path of a druid. The card establishes Krosa's defenders in the early Otarian setting, rather than implying that Seton alone performed Kamahl's later training.

</HistoricalMoment>

The transformation was not a sudden reversal from violent man to perfect guardian. Kamahl had to practice restraint and accept that he could not undo Jeska's suffering through force. He began to understand that power could protect and nurture rather than compel obedience.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[15]} significance={frontmatter.cards[15].significance}>

The green _Kamahl, Fist of Krosa_ was originally introduced in _Onslaught_. The displayed _Archenemy_ reprint retains its original artwork, allowing the contrast with the earlier red _Kamahl, Pit Fighter_ to remain visible without confusing the printing's publication date.

</HistoricalMoment>

Even after Kamahl turned away from the Mirari, others still wanted it. In _Judgment_, he confronted Laquatus's designs on the sphere. Kamahl ultimately drove the Mirari sword into the soil of Krosa, releasing a tremendous infusion of energy into the forest. The event became known as _Mirari's Wake_: the land itself responded, and the growth it provoked shaped the struggles that followed.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[14]} significance={frontmatter.cards[14].significance}>

The original _Mirari's Wake_ carries flavor text describing the land drinking power from the Mirari. It is direct evidence of the artifact's continuing effect on Krosa, not proof that the forest could safely absorb all its power without consequences.

</HistoricalMoment>

## IX. The Cabal Remakes Jeska as Phage

While Kamahl pursued a new life, Jeska's unhealed wound left her vulnerable to others' plans. The Cabal took possession of her and subjected her to dark intervention that changed rather than restored her. The once-independent Pardic warrior emerged as Phage the Untouchable. Her touch was lethal, a horrifying inversion of the recovery her brother had sought.

The transformation became a new source of power for the Cabal. As Phage, Jeska was drawn into the pits and the political calculations of <CharacterLink id="cabal-patriarch">the Patriarch</CharacterLink>. The institution that had already exploited Kamahl's hunger for glory now turned his sister's condition into another instrument of spectacle and influence.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[5]} significance={frontmatter.cards[5].significance}>

_Phage the Untouchable_ is her original _Legions_ card. Its fearsome ability represents her deadly contact, while its casting restriction emphasizes that the power was inseparable from risk. It should not be mistaken for a portrait of Jeska before the Mirari wound.

</HistoricalMoment>

Kamahl had begun to change, but Phage's existence proved that personal redemption could not immediately reverse the consequences of violence. Another person had been remade without the freedom to return simply to who she once was.

<HistoricalEventReference id="jeska-becomes-phage" />

## X. Ixidor, Nivea, and a Vengeful Creation

<CharacterLink id="ixidor">Ixidor</CharacterLink> was an illusionist whose
talent and ambition brought him into the dangerous world of the Cabal's games.
His companion Nivea was killed in a pit encounter involving Phage. Grief drove
him toward magic that did more than deceive the senses: he learned to impose
imagined forms on reality itself.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[4]} significance={frontmatter.cards[4].significance}>

_Ixidor, Reality Sculptor_ provides a contemporary _Onslaught_ portrait of the dreamer who could make imagined beings real. His story begins in personal loss, but the form his vengeance took would embroil armies, institutions, and religious followers.

</HistoricalMoment>

From his imagination came <CharacterLink id="akroma">Akroma</CharacterLink>, a powerful angel fashioned in relation to Nivea's memory and driven against Phage. Akroma's certainty and seemingly overwhelming power made her an ideal focus for others who desired an answer to the Cabal's cruelty. Yet she was born from vengeance, not impartial justice.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[6]} significance={frontmatter.cards[6].significance}>

The original _Akroma, Angel of Wrath_ appears in _Legions_, opposite the set's _Phage the Untouchable_. The two cards preserve the protagonists of a conflict that seems morally simple from a distance but is rooted in loss, exploitation, and the inability to let go.

</HistoricalMoment>

Kamahl was now caught between a sister whose lethal power threatened the living and an angel whose campaign of retribution could devastate the land he meant to defend.

<HistoricalEventReference id="akroma-created" />

### Nivea's death and an angel made for vengeance

Nivea deserves to remain visible in the historical account even though
she does not have a famous legendary creature card of her own. Her death
was the personal loss that drove Ixidor to create Akroma. A portrait of
Akroma alone can make the resulting struggle look like a contest between
an angel and an assassin, hiding the human relationship that preceded it.

Ixidor gave his creation a purpose centered on the enemy he held
responsible for Nivea's death. Akroma was therefore not a detached
representative of justice: she was born from grief and a specific
demand for retribution. This origin complicates the devotion that later
gathered around her, and it makes Phage's role more tragic because Phage
was herself Jeska transformed by injuries she had not chosen.

<HistoricalEventReference id="otaria-nivea-and-akroma" />

## XI. Armies, Devotion, and the Threat to Otaria

What began as Ixidor's grief and Phage's servitude to the Cabal spread into a wider struggle. Both figures attracted followers who treated them as symbols of salvation or certainty. The Cabal could present Phage as its champion; Akroma's supporters believed in a terrible purifying answer to that power. Ideology, devotion, and military force made the feud much larger than either original grievance.

Neither faction offered a simple cure for the corruption set loose by the Mirari. The Cabal's domination, the old Order's certainties, the merfolk court's manipulation, and the new fervor around Akroma all had a common weakness: each placed impossible hopes in a single superior power.

The Krosan Forest could not remain insulated from the escalating conflict. Kamahl's new responsibility as a defender required him to confront both sides, even though one of the combatants remained his sister. His personal tragedy had become a crisis for a continent.

## XII. A Single Blow and the Birth of Karona

At the climax of the struggle, Akroma and Phage came together with Zagorka, an elderly woman whose fate became inseparable from theirs. Kamahl struck the three with his Soul Reaper axe. Instead of simply ending the confrontation, the blow fused them into one extraordinary being: <CharacterLink id="jeska">Jeska</CharacterLink>'s second rebirth, Karona, the False God.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[7]} significance={frontmatter.cards[7].significance}>

The original _Scourge_ card _Karona, False God_ depicts the merged being rather than a separate deity who existed before the confrontation. The title emphasizes the central paradox: tremendous supernatural power can command worship without making its possessor a wise or benevolent god.

</HistoricalMoment>

Karona appeared to embody the accumulated hopes and fears of the people around her. Devotion itself became part of the danger. Those who wanted a source of unquestionable authority found a figure powerful enough to claim it, while her instability threatened to magnify rather than resolve Otaria's divisions.

The Mirari had not directly made every participant or caused every subsequent decision. But its influence had begun the chain: Chainer's discovery, the struggle among owners, Kamahl's wound to Jeska, and the transformations that eventually collided in Karona.

<HistoricalEventReference id="birth-of-karona" />

### Why Zagorka matters to the false god

Zagorka's part in the merger can be easy to overlook because Akroma and
Phage carry the most recognizable cards and the most conspicuous powers.
Yet Karona came from three people, not a simple combination of the angel
and Jeska. Zagorka's presence also means the newly worshipped figure
contained a life without the grand ambitions of the rival champions.

The convergence was not a moral reconciliation. It produced a being
whose very existence encouraged others to surrender judgment to what
they called divine power. The final betrayal by Sash and Waistcoat would
again expose the fragility of leadership built on worship rather than
responsibility.

<HistoricalEventReference id="otaria-zagorkas-convergence" />

## XIII. The False God's Fall and Jeska's Third Rebirth

Karona did not remain beyond betrayal. Two of her acolytes, Sash and Waistcoat, turned on her and pierced her with the Mirari sword. The blow separated the composite being into the three women who had formed her. Akroma and Zagorka died; Jeska survived, and her planeswalker spark ignited.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[16]} significance={frontmatter.cards[16].significance}>

The much later _Commander Legends_ card _Jeska, Thrice Reborn_ is a retrospective record of the same woman's lives as Jeska, Phage, and Karona, culminating in planeswalkerhood. It should not be used as if its 2020 illustration were an original _Scourge_ depiction of the separation.

</HistoricalMoment>

Karn took the newly ascended Jeska under his guidance. She had been a warrior, a weapon made by others, a component of a being worshipped as a god, and finally an independent traveler between planes. Her later history belongs to the crisis of Dominaria's temporal rifts and to the Mending; that story continues in Chapter 06.

<HistoricalEventReference id="jeska-spark" />

## XIV. Karn Reclaims the Mirari

Karn eventually recovered the probe whose journey had helped devastate Otaria. The Mirari had never been designed for the uses to which its possessors had put it. Its strange amplification of desire had made ordinary ambitions catastrophic and linked many separate tragedies.

Karn took the object to his artificial plane, Argentum, and used it in creating the sentient guardian Memnarch. That transformation connected the end of the Otarian saga to the history of Mirrodin. What began as a tool of observation could become an agent with ambitions of its own; Karn's solution to one disaster helped prepare another.

<HistoricalEventReference id="mirari-aftermath" />


### Why Karona's birth was not a continent's solution

The end of the Otaria cycle illustrates how dangerous it is to equate a miraculous appearance with reconciliation. Different peoples projected their hopes and fears onto Karona. A being who seemed to promise a new order could become a focus for worship even when no shared political or moral agreement existed among those worshippers. The resulting devotion did not dissolve old rivalries; it gave them a common object around which to reorganize.

Zagorka's significance in the story is easy to overlook beside the powerful figures who dominate card art and military accounts. Her actions show that a historical turning point may depend on an ordinary person refusing the interpretations imposed by more influential institutions. The fall of a false god is therefore also a crisis of belief: people who have organized their lives around an apparent salvation must decide how to proceed when it disappears.

The Mirari's eventual removal closes the most immediate contest over the artifact, but not every consequence. Jeska's future connects Otaria to the Time Spiral story, where responsibility for past damage becomes inseparable from the attempt to preserve the present. Treat these arcs as successive changes in one person's life, not as independent legends that happen to reuse her names.

## XV. Sources, Publication, and Historical Cautions

The two added historical portraits also demonstrate a particular strength
of this archive. _Lieutenant Kirtar_ (_Odyssey_, 2001) records an Order
leader involved in the early Mirari crisis. _Balthor the Defiled_
(_Judgment_, 2002) records the later state of a warrior whose living
identity had appeared as _Balthor the Stout_ in _Torment_ (2002). Those
cards were released close together, but the relevant distinction is the
sequence of events they depict. Their abilities are not literal battle
reports, and an illustrated character does not make every scene involving
that character visible on the card itself.

The chronology of Otaria's fictional history should not be confused with the order of the cards' publication. The _Odyssey_ block (_Odyssey_, 2001; _Torment_ and _Judgment_, 2002) tells the story of the Mirari's owners, Chainer's fall, Jeska's wound, and Kamahl's shift toward druidic responsibility. The following _Onslaught_ block (_Onslaught_, 2002; _Legions_ and _Scourge_, 2003) follows Jeska as Phage, the rise of Akroma, and Karona's creation and destruction.

The corresponding novels—Vance Moore's _Odyssey_, Scott McGough's _Chainer's Torment_, Will McDermott's _Judgment_, and J. Robert King's _Onslaught_, _Legions_, and _Scourge_—provide scenes and motivations not fully recoverable from card illustrations alone. Later Wizards of the Coast articles summarize the major transformations and connect them to Karn and the Mending.

Several distinctions matter to a historical archive. Chainer discovered the Mirari but did not immediately claim it for himself. Kirtar and Aboshan were significant intervening possessors, not footnotes in a straight line from the Cabal to Kamahl. Kamahl's remorse preceded Jeska's transformation into Phage, and his druidic identity did not erase the injury. Finally, Karona was formed from Phage, Akroma, and Zagorka; her destruction ignited Jeska's spark rather than restoring all three women to ordinary life.

Exact in-world dates for later _Onslaught_ events are disputed across secondary chronologies. For that reason, the chapter keeps the broad sequence clear without giving every battle a falsely precise Argivian Reckoning date. The thread binding these events is not a single war or empire but the recurring danger of power that promises to satisfy desire while denying the limits of responsibility.
