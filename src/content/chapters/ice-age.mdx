## I. The Shadow of the Sylex

The Brothers' War ended with a catastrophe whose effects
extended far beyond the death of Mishra and the destruction
of Argoth.

When Urza activated the Golgothian Sylex, the explosion
unleashed an extraordinary concentration of magical energy.

The war was over, but Dominaria's recovery had barely begun.

On Terisiare, political structures that had depended upon
the great kingdoms of the war collapsed. Their armies,
industries, and networks of trade could not simply be rebuilt.

The continent entered a period of instability as survivors
attempted to make sense of the ruin.

Worse still, the blast had disrupted the world's climate.

The changes would unfold over generations, transforming
the aftermath of war into a much longer struggle for
survival.

## II. The Dark Age

In the centuries following the Sylex Blast, communities on
Terisiare increasingly feared the magic and artifice they
associated with the destruction of the Brothers' War.

Political and religious movements gained followers by
promising that rejecting the old world's dangerous powers
would protect the survivors from another catastrophe.

The Church of Tal was among the forces that condemned
magicians and the remnants of artifice. Its influence
combined religious devotion, hostility to the old machines,
and the power to organize armed followers.

The conflict was not merely theoretical. In 69 AR, crusaders
of Tal attacked Penregon, a city defended under the leadership
of <CharacterLink id="kayla">Kayla bin-Kroog</CharacterLink>.
The assault came from outside the walls and from sympathizers
within the city. The surviving machines created by
<CharacterLink id="tawnos">Tawnos</CharacterLink> became part
of the struggle over what the city should preserve or reject.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[4]}>

The 1994 card **Witch Hunter** offers an illustrated echo of
the suspicion and zealotry associated with the Dark Age.

It is a thematic artifact from the period, not a literal
illustration of the battle at Penregon. The siege itself
is described in the later official Brothers' War fiction.

</HistoricalMoment>

<HistoricalEventReference id="talite-siege-of-penregon" />

The hostility toward magic did not extinguish magical
knowledge. It changed who could study openly, which
institutions survived, and the dangers that scholars faced.

The Dark Age was therefore both a political crisis and a
struggle over the survival of knowledge. The choices of
survivors such as Kayla, and later the wizard Jodah, form
a connection between the ruins of the war and the centuries
to come.

## III. Jodah and the Preservation of Knowledge

One of the most important figures to emerge from this
dark period was <CharacterLink id="jodah">Jodah</CharacterLink>.

His early life unfolded in a world where the practice of
magic could invite danger, and where surviving institutions
struggled to maintain their knowledge.

Jodah became involved in the affairs of powerful wizards,
including the conflict surrounding the imprisoned mage Ith
and the ambitious Mairsil.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[0]}>

Jodah's story would eventually extend far beyond the
generation into which he was born.

His unusually long life allowed him to witness the
transformation of Terisiare from the Dark Age into
the centuries of ice.

He became a living connection between historical periods
that most people knew only through stories.

</HistoricalMoment>

The survival of magical knowledge would prove essential
when new threats arose in the frozen centuries ahead.

Jodah's story also reveals the importance of
preserving knowledge through periods of fear
and political instability.

The Dark Age was not simply a time when magical
power disappeared. It was a time when the
institutions capable of teaching and protecting
that knowledge became increasingly fragile.

Those who survived the persecutions and conflicts
could become the teachers, historians, and
protectors of generations yet to come.

## IV. The Decline of Sarpadia

The troubles of the postwar world did not unfold in the
same way on every continent of Dominaria. On distant
Sarpadia, several civilizations faced conflicts both
with neighboring powers and within their own societies.

The history preserved by _Fallen Empires_ describes five
major factions: Icatia's Order of Leitbur, the Vodalian
merfolk, the Order of the Ebon Hand, the dwarves, and the
elves. None faced only an external enemy. Each struggled
with an internal rival or upheaval.

The Icatians confronted the Farrelites; the Vodalians
faced encroaching homarids. Goblins and orcs threatened
the dwarves, while thallids challenged the elves.
The Order of the Ebon Hand faced revolt among the
thrulls it had bred and exploited.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[5]}>

**Order of the Ebon Hand** recalls one of the powers
that contested Sarpadia. The card's historical value
lies in its depiction of the faction and its religious
identity, not a single battle or monarch.

</HistoricalMoment>

<HistoricalEventReference id="sarpadian-factions-fracture" />

The thrull rebellion gives this wider history a particularly
striking dimension. Creatures created to serve the Ebon Hand
became a threat to the society that depended upon them.
What had seemed an instrument of control became an agent
of its undoing.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[6]}>

**Thrull Champion** provides a visual reminder that the
thrulls were more than anonymous servants in the story
of Sarpadia. Their growing strength helped change the
balance of power within the Ebon Hand's domain.

</HistoricalMoment>

<HistoricalEventReference id="thrull-uprising" />

Sarpadia should not be reduced to a single moment of
collapse, nor treated as a direct replay of the Brothers'
War. Its factions followed related but distinct paths,
with internal pressures as important as their conflicts
with one another. This is one reason the original
_Fallen Empires_ cards are unusually valuable as a
record of societies rather than individual heroes.

## V. The Frozen Kingdoms

As Dominaria's cooling intensified, glaciers spread across
Terisiare and changed the geography on which its kingdoms
depended. Harsh conditions made food, shelter, and defended
settlements matters of political survival.

Kjeldor developed a powerful military tradition, while
Balduvian communities retained their own identities and
political interests. Their long rivalry reflected different
ways of organizing power and life under the ice.

The elves of Fyndhorn and their allies also maintained
their traditions amid the changing environment. The peoples
of this age were not simply remnants of an earlier world:
they created institutions and alliances shaped by the
conditions in which they lived.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[7]}>

**Kjeldoran Outpost**, from _Alliances_ (1996), illustrates
the importance of fortified communities and organized
military defense. It is presented here as an artifact of
the setting, not as a depiction of the later World Spell.

</HistoricalMoment>

The need to survive did not make cooperation inevitable.
Competing rulers and communities continued to fight, even
when the consequences of division were severe. Only a
shared threat of extraordinary scale would bring some
rivals into a more consequential alliance.

That threat emerged in the armies of Lim-Dûl.

## VI. Lim-Dûl and the Armies of the Dead

Political conflict was not the only danger facing
the frozen kingdoms.

A necromancer named

<CharacterLink id="lim-dul">Lim-Dûl</CharacterLink>
gathered power by raising the dead and extending his influence through fear and
violence.

His command of undead forces added a new dimension
to the conflicts of Terisiare.

The frozen kingdoms faced an adversary who could
turn the casualties of warfare into instruments
of further conquest.

His ambitions threatened the living communities
of Terisiare.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[1]}>

Lim-Dûl's armies transformed the dead into instruments
of conquest.

For the kingdoms facing his forces, the struggle
was about more than political territory.

The necromancer threatened the survival of communities
already burdened by harsh winters, limited resources,
and generations of warfare.

</HistoricalMoment>

The danger eventually encouraged cooperation between
former enemies.

<CharacterLink id="jodah">Jodah</CharacterLink> and the task mage
<CharacterLink id="jaya">Jaya Ballard</CharacterLink>
became important figures in the struggle against Lim-Dûl.

Jaya was a talented pyromancer known for her
resourcefulness, independent temperament, and
formidable command of fire magic.

Their partnership brought together two very
different magical traditions.

Jodah possessed centuries of experience and
a deep understanding of arcane knowledge.
Jaya contributed practical skill, courage,
and a willingness to challenge established
authority.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[3]}>

Although Jaya was later depicted as a
planeswalker, her earlier role as a task
mage belongs to the history of the Ice Age.

Her friendship with Jodah became an enduring
connection between this period and later
generations of Dominaria's history.

</HistoricalMoment>

The struggle against the necromancer helped prepare
the way for a much greater change.

## VII. Freyalise and the World Spell

The planeswalker

<CharacterLink id="freyalise">Freyalise</CharacterLink>
sought to bring the long winter to an end.

Her answer was an act of magic on a scale far beyond
the ordinary spells of the age.

The World Spell was intended to reverse the effects
of the centuries-long catastrophe and restore
Dominaria's changing environment.

The attempt was also connected to the knowledge
and magical artifacts preserved by Jodah.

An enchanted mirror in his possession helped
Freyalise cast the World Spell.

The event demonstrates how the choices of
individuals separated by generations could
influence an entire world: knowledge preserved
during the Dark Age eventually contributed
to the end of the Ice Age.

<HistoricalMoment card={frontmatter.cards[2]}>

Freyalise cast the World Spell, ending the Ice Age
and beginning a period of thaw.

The spell also helped end the isolation associated
with the Shard of Twelve Worlds, a magical barrier
that had restricted travel between planes.

The world that emerged would be profoundly different
from the one that had entered the long winter.

</HistoricalMoment>

The return of warmer conditions did not erase the
damage of previous centuries.

Melting glaciers and rising waters brought new
challenges to the peoples of Dominaria.

Nevertheless, the reign of ice had ended.

## VIII. The Thaw and New Alliances

When Freyalise's World Spell ended the supernatural winter,
Terisiare did not simply return to the society that had
existed before the ice. The thaw altered terrain, travel,
and the resources on which communities had depended.

Kjeldor and Balduvia had already been forced to confront
the costs of their divisions during the struggle against
Lim-Dûl. The aftermath of the World Spell presented
further problems: how to govern, defend, and inhabit a
landscape that was changing again.

The expansion _Alliances_ explored tensions and shifting
loyalties in this new historical setting. Its title is a
reminder that political cooperation is not the same thing
as a permanent peace.

The legacy of the Brothers' War also endured. Ancient
artifacts and forgotten technologies continued to shape
those who rediscovered them; the Dark Age had shown that
the fear of such knowledge could be destructive in its
own right.

The thaw was therefore not a restoration of an untouched
past. It marked the beginning of another era of Dominarian
history, with new possibilities and unresolved conflicts.

## IX. How Magic Told This Story

The publication history of this period differs
significantly from its fictional chronology.

_The Dark_ (1994) explored the troubled centuries
following the Brothers' War.

_Fallen Empires_ (1994) portrayed Sarpadia through
five competing factions, their internal conflicts, and
the revolts that weakened them. Cards such as **Order of
the Ebon Hand** and **Thrull Champion** preserve distinct
perspectives on that struggle.

_Ice Age_ (1995) established the frozen setting
and its peoples. _Alliances_ (1996), which included
**Kjeldoran Outpost**, explored the tensions that
followed the thaw.

The later expansion _Coldsnap_ (2006) returned
to this historical setting.

The literary narrative was developed more fully
in Jeff Grubb's Ice Age Cycle:

_The Gathering Dark_, _The Eternal Ice_, and
_The Shattered Alliance_.

Much later, _Dominaria_ (2018) presented Jodah
as a legendary creature.

_Time Spiral_ (2006) introduced legendary
creature cards for both Lim-Dûl and Jaya Ballard,
representing these major Ice Age figures
years after the original expansion.

Jaya would later receive planeswalker cards
reflecting the subsequent history of her life.

_Dominaria United_ (2022) revisited
Freyalise's achievement through The World Spell.

Taken together, these releases demonstrate an
important principle of Magic's storytelling:

The cards that illustrate a historical event
are not necessarily published in the same
order as the events themselves.
