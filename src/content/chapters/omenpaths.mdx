## I. Victory Was Not a Restoration

The defeat of New Phyrexia ended the immediate invasion, but it did not return the Multiverse to the boundaries that existed before it. Realmbreaker had torn passages between worlds, carrying armies through a network fashioned for conquest. Once the war ended, movement between planes remained possible in a radically different form. **Omenpaths** became the defining phenomenon of a new era: openings through which people without planeswalker sparks could sometimes pass from one world to another.

This change cannot be reduced to a single triumph or a single law. Not every traveler can choose a destination; not every opening is permanent, predictable, or safe. The old privilege of interplanar travel had belonged principally to planeswalkers, plus rare engineered or exceptional means of passage. Now artisans, warriors, investigators, refugees, and criminals could enter stories once closed to them. That possibility was liberating, but it also meant that a threat born on one plane might arrive in another without any planeswalker arranging the journey.

At the same time, many formerly powerful planeswalkers had lost their sparks. Their losses were not identical: some retained formidable magic, while others were physically and emotionally diminished by what they had endured. The aftermath must therefore be told on two scales. One is cosmological—the question of where people can travel. The other is deeply personal—who they become when an ability that once defined their lives disappears.

### The difference between losing a spark and gaining a road

The transformation was asymmetric. A planeswalker losing a spark could no longer choose a destination by stepping into the Blind Eternities, but could still retain memories, skills, and magic accumulated across a lifetime. An ordinary person using an Omenpath gained a possible route to another plane without gaining a planeswalker's control over that route. The two changes happened alongside one another, but they were not interchangeable.

This distinction reshaped political responsibility. Worlds that once relied on a handful of planeswalkers for interplanar warning or aid had to prepare for arrivals they could neither predict nor reliably send home. Former planeswalkers, meanwhile, could no longer assume they could intervene elsewhere whenever a crisis began. The first stories after the invasion show these consequences in miniature rather than claiming a uniform system of permanent, safe portals.

## II. Zhalfir and the Burden of Survival

Chapter 09 ended with Zhalfir restored to ordinary reach and New Phyrexia removed from it. The survivors inherited that reversal, not an instant peace. For <CharacterLink id="nissa" />, recovery involved more than the disappearance of the Phyrexian threat. Her body had been changed; her relationship with the land and with the identity of a planeswalker was uncertain. In the official aftermath story *She Who Breaks the World*, she struggles with those limits on Zhalfir, while <CharacterLink id="chandra" /> remains a companion through whom her pain and hope can be understood.

Elsewhere, <CharacterLink id="nahiri" /> returned to an invaded Zendikar whose wounds could not be mended with a declaration of victory. The task of extracting corruption from the landscape called on the lithomancer's patience and power, even after the upheaval of her planeswalker status. The setting matters: the aftermath was not one common experience shared equally by every world. Eldraine mourned rulers; Zendikar and Zhalfir faced different kinds of rebuilding; some places had been damaged by invasion forces that arrived without an invitation and left permanent losses behind.

The term *desparking* describes a changed relationship to the planeswalker spark, not a universal inability to wield magic. Equally, an Omenpath is not a new spark bestowed on everyone. The distinction becomes clearest in the ordinary people who begin crossing the Multiverse and in the former planeswalkers who must learn to remain somewhere rather than leave at will.

## III. Eldraine Buries Its King and Queen

On Eldraine, the death of King Kenrith and Queen Linden struck at both family and state. Their children, the twins <CharacterLink id="will-kenrith" /> and <CharacterLink id="rowan-kenrith" />, survived into a country still suffering from the invasion. They, too, were among the planeswalkers who lost their sparks. The political question of who would govern could no longer be separated from the personal question of how to live after war.

</HistoricalMoment>

<HistoricalEventReference id="omenpaths-eldraine-mourning" />

Will tried to stabilize the realm through negotiation and repair, earning the image of a young king shaped by duty. Rowan understood the devastation through anger and a desire for power sufficient to prevent the next catastrophe. Their disagreement was not proof that one had ceased to care for Eldraine. It was a clash between forms of responsibility: the rebuilding of institutions versus the conviction that institutions had already failed.

The Wicked Slumber, a magical affliction spreading through Eldraine, made that dispute more urgent. As enchantment and old fae bargains moved back toward the center of the kingdom's history, the twins' paths diverged. One remained committed to an imperfect peace. The other moved toward dangerous sources of power and an increasingly isolated idea of rescue.

## IV. Kellan Steps Beyond the Fairy Tale

The other great Eldraine story began away from the succession dispute. <CharacterLink id="kellan" />, a young half-fae adventurer, was drawn into the crisis of the Wicked Slumber and came to understand that his own family history reached beyond his familiar home. His father was the elusive <CharacterLink id="oko" />, a planeswalker with a long record of manipulation and a talent for leaving unfinished consequences behind him.

Kellan did not acquire a spark to follow that trail. He encountered the new interplanar roads and became a traveler because the Multiverse itself had changed. This is the narrative significance of his quest: a person who would once have remained the protagonist of a local Eldraine tale could now recur across entirely different worlds, each with its own politics and history.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[2]} significance={frontmatter.cards[2].significance}>
  *Kellan, the Fae-Blooded* introduces the son before his search has taken him far beyond Eldraine. Later printings of Kellan in other sets turn that same character into a visual record of his changing experience.
</HistoricalMoment>

<HistoricalEventReference id="omenpaths-kellan-first-crossing" />

An Omenpath did not erase local geography. It joined it to another plane with its own dangers. A traveler emerging in Ixalan could not behave as though he had merely entered another Eldraine kingdom. This limitation is what made the new period historically interesting: connected worlds remained culturally, politically, and ecologically distinct.

## V. Ixalan's Ancient Depths

On Ixalan, the period after the invasion was marked by expeditions into the immense underworld beneath the surface. The history of the Oltec, Chimil, and long-hidden powers belonged to Ixalan long before Kellan arrived. He was a traveler entering an established conflict, not its creator. Huatli, Saheeli, and the newly sparked Quintorius pursued the discoveries and dangers at the center of *The Lost Caverns of Ixalan*.

The distinction between explorers mattered. Quintorius could planeswalk; Kellan could journey by Omenpath. Saheeli faced a changed relationship to her former planeswalker abilities, while Huatli's relationship to Ixalan's people and history remained central. Different ways of crossing worlds now coexisted. The result was not an identical society on every plane but new encounters between people whose stories previously had little reason to intersect.

In this chronology, Kellan's journey through Ixalan is chiefly evidence of how the post-invasion roads function in fiction: his search continued across planes without requiring a conventional planeswalker spark. The larger conflicts beneath Ixalan retain their own local historical weight and deserve a deeper treatment beyond the scope of this overview.

### Quintorius and the problem of interpreting a lost civilization

<CharacterLink id="quintorius-kand">Quintorius Kand</CharacterLink>, a loxodon archaeologist from Arcavios and newly awakened planeswalker, traveled to Ixalan to assist Saheeli and Huatli. His study of ancient records offered a second kind of cross-planar exchange: expertise carried between worlds, rather than an invading army or a fugitive crossing by accident. Saheeli, now without her spark, could send requests along recurring Omenpaths, while Quintorius could still travel as a planeswalker. Their collaboration made both forms of movement relevant to the same expedition.

At Orazca, a puzzle-door written in an unfamiliar history drew on Huatli's translation and Quintorius's archaeological methods. The route into Ixalan's depths exposed the expedition to evidence of the Oltec, the Inner Sun, and the Fomori. Those civilizations were not invented by newly arriving travelers; their histories existed long before anyone from Arcavios joined the investigation. The political tensions within the Sun Empire also continued independently of the mysteries beneath its cities.

## VI. Ravnica Investigates Its Own Wounds

The Omenpath journey next carried Kellan to Ravnica, a city already shaped by centuries of guild politics and more recent experiences of multiversal war. In *Murders at Karlov Manor*, the death and attempted killing of prominent citizens exposed tensions beneath the appearance of recovery. The case was investigated by Alquist Proft and the Agency of Magicological Investigations, with help from Kaya and Kellan.

Kellan's role again differed from that of a visitor granted authority by a spark. He was a young outsider learning an institution's methods and helping to uncover a conspiracy that Ravnica itself needed to confront. The investigation led through the guilds, their rivalries, and their unresolved injuries after the Phyrexian invasion. The eventual revelation of Oba's role within Trostani connected the murders to the Selesnya conclave's own trauma, rather than to a simple outside invader.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[5]} significance={frontmatter.cards[5].significance}>
  *Kellan, Inquisitive Prodigy* places Eldraine's young traveler inside a Ravnican detective story. The card is a publication-era marker of his changing role, not proof that Ravnica's crisis was caused by Omenpaths.
</HistoricalMoment>

<HistoricalEventReference id="omenpaths-ravnica-investigation" />

This is another important distinction between causation and chronology. The opening of planar roads explains Kellan's presence; it does not explain every murder, every grievance, or every guild's behavior. Earlier events within Ravnica remain essential to understanding the case.

### Evidence, manipulation, and the survivors' anger

<CharacterLink id="alquist-proft">Alquist Proft</CharacterLink> pursued the case as more than a search for someone convenient to blame. Early suspicion of the assassin Etrata did not resolve the problem of motive, testimony, or memories distorted by another person's magic. Proft's investigation, aided by Kellan, Kaya, and eventually Etrata herself, established that a living agent could be used as the instrument of another person's will. Ravnican law therefore had to distinguish the person who performed an act from the person who controlled it.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[12]} significance={frontmatter.cards[12].significance}>

The original *Alquist Proft, Master Sleuth* introduces the detective as a central actor in Ravnica's investigation. The card is a portrait of the investigator, not a literal illustration of Oba's confession.

</HistoricalMoment>

The deeper cause lay within <CharacterLink id="trostani">Trostani</CharacterLink>, the Selesnya Conclave's three-part dryad leadership. Oba had absorbed suffering during the Phyrexian invasion while protecting Cim and Ses from its memories. Her grief hardened into vengeance against people she deemed responsible for Ravnica's failure, including Teysa Karlov. The resulting murders exploited mind-controlling flowers distributed through the city. **Oba's choices must not be attributed equally to Cim and Ses**, who did not knowingly direct her killings.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[13]} significance={frontmatter.cards[13].significance}>

*Trostani, Three Whispers* portrays the composite Selesnya leadership. Its significance in the murder case lies in the disagreement concealed within one shared public identity, rather than in the three sisters acting as one willing conspirator.

</HistoricalMoment>

Proft's public confrontation exposed Oba, and the ensuing violence brought the investigation to a close without making the invasion's trauma disappear. The resolution showed how deeply Ravnica's apparently restored institutions still carried the costs of war. It also showed why evidence, culpability, and public accountability mattered as much as defeating another physical threat.

## VII. Thunder Junction: A Meeting Place for Outlaws

The frontier plane of Thunder Junction offered a different kind of Omenpath history. People from many backgrounds converged there, turning new routes into trade, settlement, opportunity, and crime. Unlike Eldraine's established monarchy or Ravnica's ten-guild system, Thunder Junction brought a frontier society and an ancient mystery into direct contact with visitors from across the Multiverse.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[7]} significance={frontmatter.cards[7].significance}>
  <CharacterLink id="oko" /> assembled a crew to break into a legendary vault. *Oko, the Ringleader* represents the convergence of interplanar criminal interests around the promise of treasure and power.
</HistoricalMoment>

The vault was not simply a storehouse of stolen coin. Its history and defenses belonged to far older structures, tied to the Fomori and their artifacts. Oko's group required unusual specialists, and its ambitions attracted rivals. Kellan eventually found the father he had followed across worlds—but meeting Oko did not resolve his questions about trust and belonging. Kellan had learned to make independent choices, while Oko treated deception as an ordinary tool.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[6]} significance={frontmatter.cards[6].significance}>
  *Kellan, the Kid* places the questing son at the point where his private search collides with the larger politics of a heist. Family becomes a test of judgment, not merely a destination.
</HistoricalMoment>

<HistoricalEventReference id="omenpaths-thunder-junction-vault" />

### An arrival route is not a claim to land

Thunder Junction's crossroads drew people from many planes, including survivors seeking a new start and opportunists who saw unfamiliar settlements as an opportunity. Its settlements, trade, and criminal alliances demonstrated that the new planar geography could produce a society whose inhabitants shared no single ancestral world. The resulting frontier was not empty merely because outsiders described it as newly discovered: the land also held evidence of the Fomori and their much older power.

Oko's crew made use of these networks, gathering specialists with motives that did not necessarily align. Kellan's search for his father put him in the same story as the vault heist without making the personal reunion the heist's purpose. That separation matters for chronology: Kellan crossed worlds in search of a relationship; others had already arrived to acquire wealth, escape old obligations, or seize the vault's secrets.

## VIII. Jace, Vraska, and the Cost of a Different Future

The story of Thunder Junction also revealed that the end of the Phyrexian war did not produce a tidy division between rescued heroes and defeated villains. <CharacterLink id="jace-beleren" /> and <CharacterLink id="vraska" /> survived with profound scars from compleation. Their reunion was bound to memories, care, and a desire to prevent future suffering—but those desires did not make every decision they took harmless.

Jace's reappearance demonstrated a crucial limit to generalized accounts of desparking: **not every planeswalker lost their spark**. He could still traverse the Blind Eternities. Vraska, meanwhile, participated in the events on Thunder Junction and worked toward ends that Oko's crew did not fully understand.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[8]} significance={frontmatter.cards[8].significance}>
  *Vraska, the Silencer* shows the former Phyrexian agent as a survivor acting within a complicated network of bargains. Her presence on Thunder Junction connects the new era to the unresolved personal damage of the invasion.
</HistoricalMoment>

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[9]} significance={frontmatter.cards[9].significance}>
  *Jace Reawakened* makes clear that the post-invasion world includes both desparked former planeswalkers and those who can still walk between planes. The card's date belongs to the Thunder Junction publication, not to the first emergence of Omenpaths.
</HistoricalMoment>

Their search led to <CharacterLink id="loot" />, the strange young creature discovered within the vault. He was the living prize around which larger ambitions could now form. The heist's result expanded the narrative from a local contest among outlaws toward questions about the shape and future of the entire Multiverse.

<HistoricalMoment cardId={frontmatter.cardIds[10]} significance={frontmatter.cards[10].significance}>
  *Loot, the Key to Everything* is the historical artifact at this chapter's turning point: the vault's hidden treasure was a living being, and his significance was not reducible to the price of the heist.
</HistoricalMoment>

<HistoricalEventReference id="omenpaths-loot-discovered" />

### Loot is not an ordinary piece of treasure

The vault's final revelation shifted the stakes from property to custody of a living being. Loot was neither a mere artifact nor a reward that could be assessed only by its value to the heist's participants. His existence suggested access to knowledge of the Multiverse on a scale that attracted Jace and Vraska. Their belief that they could protect a better future did not automatically confer the right to decide another being's future without regard for his own life.

This is the political edge of the early Omenpaths stories: travel has been democratized, but the power to control information, routes, and the lives of travelers remains unequally distributed. The opening of paths creates new possibilities for freedom while also creating new occasions for exploitation.

## IX. Roads, Borders, and the Problem of Trust

The Omenpaths did not abolish differences between planes. On Eldraine, legitimacy was debated through a monarchy wounded by war. On Ixalan, ancient civilizations and contested histories shaped exploration. On Ravnica, a murder investigation uncovered tensions within a densely governed city. On Thunder Junction, interplanar movement brought opportunists into a frontier already marked by power struggles. A shared means of travel did not produce a shared politics.

Nor was a road automatically a sanctuary. A passage could allow separated families to reunite, researchers to seek knowledge, or ordinary people to escape a crisis. It could also expose worlds to agents whose motives were hidden. The new geography expanded responsibility beyond those born with a spark, while exposing travelers to the consequences of entering unfamiliar histories.

Kellan's journey offers a useful reading thread because each setting tests him differently. Eldraine tests his relationship to family and duty. Ixalan challenges him to understand a world with histories deeper than his quest. Ravnica teaches inquiry and judgment. Thunder Junction asks whether finding a person means accepting that person's methods. Together the episodes form one character's route through several independently meaningful worlds.

### Three histories, not one universal itinerary

A comparison of the first Omenpath-era settings makes the difference explicit. On Ixalan, the urgent question concerned whose evidence and authority could explain a buried civilization. On Ravnica, it concerned how a community should assign guilt after trauma and magical coercion. On Thunder Junction, it concerned who could claim resources and exercise power in a society populated by arrivals from many planes. Kellan's route connected these stories for readers, but it did not make him their sole cause or central authority.

For this archive, a defensible cross-planar timeline therefore records encounters and publications without inventing a common clock. Kellan's visits offer a useful sequence of episodes. They do not prove that independent political conflicts elsewhere began because he arrived, nor that every Omenpath was open for the same length of time or to the same destination.

## X. Publication Sequence Is Not a Multiversal Calendar

The material in this chapter appeared from 2023 through April 2024. That is an editorial reading sequence, not an exact measure of travel times or a guarantee that all its stories happened in that order on a universal clock. The aftermath stories illuminate the immediate effects of the invasion; the later releases establish some of the ways those effects change life on different planes. Specific local dates and ambiguous intervals should remain attributed to their sources rather than assembled into a false master chronology.

There is also a limit to this chapter's scope. Later Magic stories continue to revisit Omenpaths, sparks, and interplanar societies. This article ends with the Thunder Junction revelations as an early-era turning point. Those later developments should be handled in separate expansions, with the same distinction between story chronology, publication history, and uncertain inference used throughout the archive.

The Multiverse survived the invasion, but it did not return to isolation. The next great historical questions were no longer simply who could planeswalk or which tyrant might break through a boundary. They concerned the people now walking through those boundaries—and what they might build, discover, steal, or destroy on the other side.
